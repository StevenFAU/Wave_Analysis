# Choi et al. (2020): Raw images → SWH (CNN classification, ConvLSTM regression)

**Citation:** Choi, H., Park, M., Son, G., Jeong, J., Park, J., Mo, K., & Kang, P.
(2020). Real-time significant wave height estimation from raw ocean images
based on 2D and 3D deep neural networks. *Ocean Engineering*, 201, 107129.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2020.107129 · BibTeX key `Choi2020`

**Verification:** V2 (abstract, metadata); details V3.

## Setup
Two tasks. (1) Single-image **classification** of SWH level on NDBC buoy-camera
images (20 buoys, 44,620 pairs; low < 1.52 m, high > 3.05 m, middle excluded).
Compares VGG, Inception v3, ResNet, and DenseNet with transfer learning and
global feature operators (GMP, GAP, a learned conv extractor). (2) Continuous
**regression** from 16-frame sequences captured every 1 s on a container ship
(97,458 images → 3,019 labelled clips), with labels from shipboard wave radar
about every 10 min.

## Method (regression)
Adjacent-frame differences → pretrained VGG → **bidirectional ConvLSTM**
(256 filters, 3×3) → GAP → FC → H_s.

## Results
84 % classification accuracy (NDBC); MSE 0.0177 (ship dataset). (V2, abstract)

## Limitations
~30-min offset between NDBC images and wave records. Dark and over-exposed
images removed by brightness thresholds. One ship. The radar reference is itself
a remote-sensing estimate. Bidirectional temporal modelling is not causal.

## Consequences for Wave_Analysis
- Store Δt for every pair (`processing.synchronize`).
- Annotate instead of brightness-filtering (`qc.imagery`).
- Reference implementation: `models/baselines/choi2020.py`.
- The thresholds equal 5 ft and 10 ft, probably a practical definition in
  imperial units.
- Current NDBC buoycam images are still stamped at minute 10 (V1, NDBC listing).
