# Implications of the five supplied wave-sensing papers

| Study | What the full text evaluates | Consequence for this project |
|---|---|---|
| Y. Liu, Xu et al. | Coastal camera representations trained against SWAN significant wave height. Evaluation includes separate seasonal time blocks as well as samples from the training period. The best reported spectral-image height RMSE is 0.27 m. | Reuse their spectral representation as an input comparison. Validate against instruments on held dates and test incremental skill over the wave model, since the published target itself is a model output. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2026.127543) |
| Ahammed and Dang | A polarimetric transformer receives raw polarization channels and predicts centimetre-scale significant wave height in a laboratory. Cross-validation holds out each of 12 wave trains in turn. | The raw polarization channels are a distinct sensor requirement. RGB buoy cameras support intensity-based comparisons only. Carry the paper's whole-wave-train split principle into field event or date splits. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2026.127753) |
| Li et al. | Public stereo sequences supply reconstructed 3-D labels. Spatial, temporal and frequency models target different quantities. Their instantaneous extreme is the average of the five largest reconstructed elevations in a frame. Non-overlapping sequence blocks are divided 70/15/15. | Treat extreme elevation, trough-to-crest individual height, and bulk significant height as separate targets. For stereo, retain an independent gauge and withhold whole deployments when testing transfer. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2026.126780) |
| G. Zhang et al. | Beaufort labels are ordered categories. Eight protocols cover in-domain performance, transfer between shipborne domains and external transfer to MU-SSiD. The authors report both exact accuracy and the severity of ordinal errors. | Once continuous-target baselines are fixed, an ordinal classifier can be compared on genuinely ordered labels with absolute class error and within-one-class accuracy. Beaufort accuracy cannot directly benchmark individual wave height. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2026.128212) |
| H. Liu, Qin and Ti | A monocular 3D-CNN uses simulated pretraining and field fine-tuning at an eastern Chinese bridge site. Synchronized wave-gauge targets are significant height and peak period. Simulation and field data each use a 3:1:1 division with non-overlapping video clips. | Test this transfer recipe after the field-only video reference is settled, with whole dates and sites held out. For individual crests we still need wave-resolved observations at the imaged point. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2026.124561) |
