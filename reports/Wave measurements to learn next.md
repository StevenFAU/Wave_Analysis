# Which wave measurements can these cameras learn?

Research review and development-set audit · November 18, 2026

**Train the next buoy model on wave slope. Train the next shore model on wave period.** The buoy study already has evidence that the images carry information about locally generated wind waves beyond the buoy's wind speed. A frequency-limited surface slope gives that model a new, independently defined numerical target. On shore, the camera records repeated crest passages for minutes, and the project has both those clips and buoy period labels. A model that counts and times visible crests offers a physically interpretable competitor to the video transformer. For both studies, the result worth publishing is the improvement over the strongest comparator on the same previously unseen locations or events.

## Where the research stands

I reviewed local main at `1dfcc5f29`, the three experimental worktrees, local packages and saved results. The repository collects camera imagery and physical reference records, preserves their provenance and quality flags, joins observations to camera times, packages examples with grouped splits, then measures camera performance alongside wind and wave-model baselines. Its scales range from individual breaking waves in lidar studies to offshore sea states averaged over roughly half an hour. These support different scientific claims. ### What an improvement over a published result would mean

The project's 99.1% accuracy on Choi's calm-versus-rough thresholds excludes the intermediate sea states. On Kim's finer five-class task the original image arm reached 69.4% using a conversion from significant to average height. Even a random split of this project's strips yielded 68.8%, while Kim reported 78.3% from a single camera's snapshots. The random-split check narrows the explanation but leaves differences in instruments, label definitions and sites unresolved. A direct advance would be to derive actual mean individual height from matched CDIP deployment displacement records, then compare a snapshot and the same clip's video against that measured target on held-out days. The historic CDIP bulk files lack displacement, so verify deployment coverage first. [Choi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2020.107129) · [Kim et al.](https://www.kriso.re.kr/sciwatch/handle/2021.sw.kriso/9712) · [Repo comparison protocols](../docs/literature/benchmark_targets.md) · [Time-domain estimators](../src/wave_analysis/physics/timeseries.py)

## Two label checks that change the interpretation

**Peak period.** NDBC's dominant period is the reciprocal of the frequency bin with maximum spectral density. The project's ADR 0010 selects the nearest record for peak parameters because the dominant system can switch between records. The S3 label builder puts `tp_s` in the columns averaged across the ±30-minute window. I compared every finite `tp_label_s` with every reciprocal frequency in the package's own 46-bin grid. **11,083 of 41,041 labels (27.0%) were more than 0.25 s from every grid period.** This is a lower bound on the discrepancy with any single record on that grid. Computing the actual nearest-record difference requires a timestamp join. More blended peaks can happen to fall on a valid bin. [NDBC wave calculations](https://www.ndbc.noaa.gov/faq/wavecalc.shtml) · [Project label policy](../docs/decisions/0010-label-rule-time-averaged-references.md) · [Audit calculation](../research_notes/wave_measurement_audit.py)

Keep the registered S3 package and its result identifiable. Before interpreting its peak-period comparison, build a separately versioned label table with nearest-record peak, peak of the window-averaged density, current mean of peaks, timestamp offsets, and peak-switch flag. Score the same saved predictions under each definition as a sensitivity check, then train a matched model for the intended target. Report the switched-system subset. NDBC partition peak periods and ERA5 partition mean periods also have different definitions. Compare like-defined moments for a measurement claim and label the cross-definition comparator explicitly. [NDBC definitions](https://www.ndbc.noaa.gov/faq/wavecalc.shtml) · [S3 label builder in the local worktree](../scripts/buoycam_spectral_labels.py)

**Energy and instrument bandwidth.** The spectral package has 41,041 energy-period labels, 38,695 wind-sea shapes and 40,038 mean-direction labels. Its frequency widths are inferred from adjacent rounded centers. For example, its 0.100-Hz bin gets 0.0085 Hz, while NDBC lists 0.010 Hz for that WPM bin. The branch reports a median spectral-height/reported-height ratio of 0.976–0.988 across stations. Reintegrate a sample with the payload's nominal centers and widths, audit the timing join, masked bins and low-frequency coverage, and report the change by station. The cause of the offset is open. This matters especially when squaring wavenumber to estimate slope. NDBC's operational sea/swell split can also use measured wind speed. A wind-independent fixed-frequency energy band is a useful companion endpoint when testing added value over wind. [NDBC band table](https://www.ndbc.noaa.gov/faq/wavespectra.shtml) · [NDBC partition algorithm](https://www.ndbc.noaa.gov/faq/algorithm.shtml)

## 1. Buoy strips → surface slope and visible whitecapping

**What to predict.** Start with the buoy-resolved RMS surface slope, dimensionless, defined as the square root of the sum of `k² × spectral density × bin width` over a stated band, where k is the wavenumber. The cached target uses deep-water dispersion and the measured spectrum. It describes the band resolved by the buoy. A second, explicitly annotated output can measure the fraction of visible water pixels covered by foam. Actual horizontal area coverage requires a metric projection. An image showing white water can contain residual foam, glare or spray. A separate active-breaking label needs temporal evidence or an expert annotation protocol. Shipboard measurements have linked equilibrium-range mean-square slope with whitecap coverage. Stringari and colleagues provide labeled active-breaking candidates and pretrained classifiers that can be tested for transfer to these cameras. [Schwendeman & Thomson, field measurements](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015JC011196) · [Stringari et al., method and data](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-83188-y) · [Released models and data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.4495599)

**What the local data already say.** I ran a CPU pilot on the original training partition. It included 26,328 complete strips from 53 buoys in 25 geographic groups. Each group was predicted by least squares fitted to the other folds. Two image features came from the strip itself across at least three accepted horizon fits. They measure RMS raw roll angle and the standard deviation of horizon row as a fraction of image height. This lets a new buoy contribute its first strip without estimating its mounting from later imagery. Each model saw the same records. [Pilot code](../research_notes/wave_measurement_audit.py) · [Target and horizon extraction](../scripts/buoycam_horizon.py)

| Predictor | Out-of-fold RMSE of RMS slope |
|---|---:|
| Training mean | 0.02133 |
| Two horizon features | 0.01733 |
| Buoy wind speed, linear | 0.01162 |
| Buoy wind speed, quadratic | 0.01167 |
| Quadratic wind plus both horizon features | **0.01131** |

The paired change from adding geometry to quadratic wind was −0.00035, about 3.0%, with a geographic-group bootstrap interval of [−0.00050, −0.00018] over 2,000 resamples. The linear wind fit also provides a strong comparator. These are exploratory development results conditional on strips with usable horizons and wind. They establish a modest increment to a simple wind model and leave the contribution from image texture unresolved. The older within-buoy correlation of about 0.56 answered a different question. [Pilot code](../research_notes/wave_measurement_audit.py) · [Previous within-buoy analysis](../docs/experiments/ndbc_buoycam_sea_state.md)

**Next fit.** Train a ridge head on frozen six-view features for slope, then gently tune the current ConvNeXt with a slope loss alongside wind-wave height. Compare with (a) a nonlinear wind-speed/gust model, (b) wind plus the two horizon features, (c) texture alone, and (d) all inputs. Fit wind and image baselines within identical folds. Test whether a manually validated water/foam mask improves the result above both horizons and raw pixels. Mask sky and hardware, and score glare, low sun and few usable views separately. Rebuild the reference with finite-depth dispersion where depth is known, and repeat with the highest bands removed to quantify sensitivity to spectral tails. [Existing dispersion solver](../src/wave_analysis/physics/dispersion.py)

**Decision.** Spend a full training run if the frozen probe or the texture-plus-wind model reduces slope RMSE over the wind/geometry comparator across held-out geographic groups. For a research claim, require a paired interval below zero and replication in later weeks, report the effect in physical units, and check wind-wave-height error for degradation. Mask annotation should earn its cost through better slope estimation or a reliable foam measurement in its own right.

## 2. Shore video → mean wave period and crest timing

The present V-JEPA 2 protocol already asks whether ordered frames help over repeated or shuffled frames, whether all 16-second windows help over one, and whether a brightness spectrum helps peak period. Its spectrum averages power over selected blocks. That retains periodicity while discarding which crest crossed which part of the image. CDIP’s spectral zero-crossing period, Tm02 = √(m₀/m₂), is present for all 453 packaged clips. A human can independently mark visible crest arrivals on a fixed transect. Buscombe et al. reported 0.41 s period RMSE on their close-range IR dataset. Kim et al. studied average wave-height classes with ADCP references. Kamagata et al. proposed a five-output shore-video model. Each result belongs to its input, label and split. [Optical wave gauging](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70208193) · [Kim et al.](https://www.kriso.re.kr/sciwatch/handle/2021.sw.kriso/9712) · [Kamagata et al., preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11998)

**Model and experiment.** For each stable camera orientation, mark two or three water-only transects and stack their pixel intensities through time. A propagating crest traces a ridge in this space–time image. Compare a deterministic ridge/peak detector, a small 2-D CNN trained to predict crest-passage probabilities, and the existing frozen video probe with a Tm02 head. Establish crest reference times by independently annotating a subset across sites, tide and breaking intensity. Match predicted and annotated arrivals one-to-one at a tolerance set from annotator disagreement. Report event precision/recall, inter-arrival MAE in seconds, then clip-level buoy-period RMSE for all arms including the existing brightness-spectrum ridge and a no-video hindcast. Ablate time order and test on a new camera orientation. Timing is meaningful before spatial calibration. Conversion of crest speed to m/s needs calibrated ground coordinates and synchronized frames. Andriolo et al. demonstrate that dependency in a webcam celerity study. [WebCOOS design](../docs/experiments/webcoos_video.md) · [Andriolo et al., calibrated coastal webcam](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17132274)

**Decision.** Report local inter-crest timing and offshore spectral period as two endpoints. Currituck is about 9.2 km from its reference buoy, Jennette about 18.6 km, and Oak Island about 28.9 km. Refraction, breaking and missed faint crests can separate those endpoints. A win against annotated arrivals with little change in offshore RMSE would still yield a local instrument. Keep both Oak Island views and shared CDIP records in the same fold, block multi-day events for a sensitivity analysis, and show the three site-held-out results separately once each has adequate date coverage. [Camera and buoy inventory](../docs/datasets/webcoos.md)

## 3. Buoy spectra → height by frequency band and directional spread

**Make the spectral head earn its resolution.** S3 already outputs normalized wind-sea energy across 46 bins. A simpler competitor would predict nonnegative integrated variances in a few fixed frequency bands from pooled image features. Choose boundaries on the instrument grid before scoring. Compare a training-mean shape, a wind-conditioned spectral shape, a small dictionary of nonnegative spectra learned only on training buoys, and S3's 46-bin output aggregated to identical bands. Fit weights for the dictionary with a small image head. Train on absolute band variances, and report equivalent band height (four times the square root of band variance) in metres alongside error in normalized shape. A model can assign the right total energy to the wrong periods. Separate the amount from its distribution in the evaluation. For total-sea bands, recover reference densities from the raw spectra, since the existing shape columns only contain the wind-sea partition. [Repository spectral estimators](../src/wave_analysis/processing/spectra.py) · [NDBC wave spectra](https://www.ndbc.noaa.gov/faq/wavespectra.shtml)

For a stronger fusion experiment, allow the image head to correct the wave model's energy in individual bands and derive total height and energy period from the resulting spectrum. Use a positive correction and a common frequency range with known coverage. The baseline is exactly the same correction model with the camera withheld. Full ERA5 or WW3 spectra matched to the buoy windows are an additional acquisition and alignment step. The locally available ERA5 bulk moments alone cannot uniquely determine band energy. Mooneyham et al.'s SWRL Net demonstrated learned spectral corrections using WW3 and buoy observations. Its offshore forecast inputs and lead times differ from the camera nowcast proposed here. [SWRL Net](https://doi.org/10.1175/WAF-D-19-0254.1)

**A directional output suited to a rotating buoy.** Estimate the directional width of the wind-sea band. The magnitude of its first directional moment is invariant to a common rotation, making it a plausible target for strips without reliable north headings. Form the energy-weighted first vector from NDBC's r₁(f) and α₁(f), then compute width as √(2 × (1 − |R₁|)) in radians and report degrees. Aggregate energy and vector components over the selected frequency and time support before taking the magnitude. Track valid directional-energy coverage, mask bins with missing coefficients and flag low band energy. This definition includes changes of mean direction across frequencies. Keep it distinct from spread at the spectral peak. The package's 40,038 mean directions indicate a substantial source of directional records; coverage of quality-controlled wind-band widths needs its own count. [Kuik et al., directional buoy analysis](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/phoc/18/7/1520-0485_1988_018_1020_amftra_2_0_co_2.xml) · [Existing directional moment code](../src/wave_analysis/processing/spectra.py)

Try a frozen image embedding plus a bounded scalar head for |R₁|. Compare with wind-only, the training-fold median, and the wave model where a definition-matched spread is available. Score width MAE in degrees and dispersion by buoy, with separate checks in narrow and broad seas. Permute view order as a control. For S3's absolute mean direction, add a sun/time/position-only comparator and score low-resultant or conflicting seas separately. The branch's caption audit found the printed bearing fixed at 79 stations and a different fixed value at the remaining station. The absolute-direction task must establish how much orientation the pixels supply.

## 4. Local breakers → breaking type and individual height

For a paper on **what is happening at the beach**, use labels from the breaker itself. Yin et al. released 9,000 labeled 30-second clips from 15 cameras across six sites, grouped into spilling, plunging and surging by the prominent event. Start with their site-organized release and compare a frozen video probe with a frame CNN plus GRU on identical held-out sites. Match camera IDs and UTC windows against WebCOOS before assigning training or evaluation footage from either source. Evaluate macro-F1 and the site-by-class confusion matrix. Site-specific class absence and the public set's selection for visible breakers matter when moving to continuous video, where a no-event detector also needs an evaluation. [Yin et al., dataset and label definitions](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-06005-5)

For **individual wave height and period**, the local Duck IR/lidar release has 1,782 tracked waves across 53 runs over two days, with trough-to-crest height, trough-to-trough period and 1,674 manual types. Only 37 manual labels are plunging onsets. Confirm which tracks have matching image or IR-mask inputs before fitting anything. Use the lidar values as targets. Compare an IR-mask transect model with a compact CNN on matched thermal observations. Hold entire runs and then an entire day out, report per-wave height MAE in metres and period MAE in seconds, and treat the two-day result as a method check. A larger cross-site campaign would be needed for transfer. The local thermal and Yin label taxonomies differ, so use distinct task heads. [Duck data and instrument details](https://hdl.handle.net/1773/46586) · [Local archive inventory](../docs/datasets/external_research_datasets.md)

For absolute wavelength, celerity or crest elevation, the local ship stereo archive offers rectified radiance and reconstructed elevation on the same grid. Compare a space–time spectral estimate with a small learned radiance model against stereo-derived quantities on held-out bursts. Keep overlapping five-minute and full-capture versions of the same burst together, and propagate reconstruction masks. The delivered radiance has already used stereo geometry in rectification, so performance describes that instrument setup. For a future maximum-wave-height study, define a fixed record duration and derive maxima from quality-controlled displacement or co-located elevation. An offshore buoy's largest wave in a half hour cannot identify the particular breaker filmed at the beach. [Ship stereo release](https://hdl.handle.net/1773/38314) · [CDIP products and record windows](../docs/datasets/cdip.md)
