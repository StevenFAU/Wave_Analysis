# Give Each Wave Measurement Its Signal

The project improves on existing research fastest by no longer asking one camera model for every wave parameter: train separate models, each on the measurement its input has been shown to carry, and bring in a second, non-camera signal for the rest. The deciding principle for every choice below is **information per independent site**: a test ranks high when it asks an input for something it physically sees, is judged against the strongest no-camera baseline at buoys or dates the model never saw, and runs on data already held or openly archived for the cameras' own dates; the newness of a network counts for nothing. The project's own pre-registered bars supply the map. The buoy camera is a wind-sea instrument (wind-wave height error **0.260 m against ERA5's 0.343 m** across 79 buoys) and improves a wave model only when fused as wind-sea energy (**0.267 against 0.288 m** on total height, met out of fold, confirmation due 2026-11-27); it loses to ERA5 on total height, peak period and direction, and no learned shore-video probe beats climatology on peak period. Buoy stills should therefore learn wind-sea height and period, surface slope, wind speed and energy by frequency band; shore video should learn period from minutes-long physical signals and individual-wave statistics from buoy displacement records; swell must come from a wave model or from a new partner, and the most promising partner found is coastal seismic noise, which is open, continuous and archived for **1,727 of 1,733 days** of the Waimea camera record. Against the four target papers the project is ahead of Choi 2020, ahead of Kamagata 2026 on buoy cameras but not yet on like-for-like shore video, behind Kim 2023 on snapshots, and holds a real-data null for Yang 2026; none of the four papers' own networks has been trained on the project's data, and that re-run is a prerequisite for any "improves on" sentence. The published record contains no leakage-free, instrument-referenced learned baseline for wind-sea height, surface slope, wind speed from sea imagery, band-resolved spectra or held-out-date shore period, so rigorous first benchmarks are open there, while rip currents, runup and video bathymetry are already benchmarked and should be left alone. Three free upgrades come before any new network: individual-wave labels from CDIP displacement and zero-crossing files, a stronger model-only bar built from NOAA GFS-Wave buoy-point partitions, and the re-run of the four papers' architectures. Five 2026 *Ocean Engineering* papers that cite the same targets could not be read and must be read as PDFs before any claim of priority.

**How to read the evidence tags.** Every claim carries the level at which its source was checked on 2026-11-19. **PROJ** is a scored result or document in this repository (the branch is named where the newest version is not on `main`). **FT** means the full text was read. **ABS** means abstract, landing page or catalogue record only. **SNIP** means a search-result summary or a secondary citation only, unverified until the paper is opened. **META** means title and venue only, with no result known. **API** means read directly from a provider's server that day (THREDDS file headers, station services, directory listings, model cards). **EST** marks an estimate or arithmetic by the note-takers or this report, not a published or measured number. Demonstrated results are stated as facts with their tag; proposals are called proposals. RMSE is the typical miss in the stated unit; R² is the share of the true ups and downs an estimate reproduces (1 is perfect, 0 is no better than the average); a 95 % interval that excludes zero means the difference would survive drawing other buoys or dates.

## Where the project stands: the camera is a wind-sea instrument, and the newest results sit on unmerged branches

### What the repository holds

The distinctive holding is the NDBC buoy-camera archive: **41,091 packaged daylight and twilight six-view strips at 79 buoys**, 2026-09-23 to 2026-11-14, each labelled by the wave sensor on the same hull, with later packages adding wind-wave and swell heights, the buoy's own wind, periods, mean direction, a 46-bin wind-sea spectral shape, band-limited surface slope and four fixed energy bands. NDBC deletes the images after about 72 hours and never transmits raw buoy motion, so the project's archive is the only copy and the horizon in the image is the only attitude proxy ([buoy experiment log](../docs/experiments/ndbc_buoycam_sea_state.md), PROJ, newest on branch `claude/trusting-lalande-4a4e8b`; [NDBC wave FAQ](https://www.ndbc.noaa.gov/faq/wave.shtml), API). On shore the project holds 241,229 WebCOOS stills from eight North Carolina and Florida cameras, about 217,000 ten-minute time-exposure (`timex`) and brightest-pixel (`brt`) images that **no experiment has touched**, and a growing sample of ten-minute, 20-fps video clips (1,282 packaged on 20 local dates on the morning of 2026-11-19, of about 4,800 planned), all paired to CDIP Waverider buoys 9 to 29 km offshore; the label is therefore the incident sea, not the breakers in view ([WebCOOS datasheet](../docs/datasets/webcoos.md), PROJ; [video experiment](../docs/experiments/webcoos_video.md), PROJ, newest on branch `claude/video-whole-clip-491c90`). The 2009-2013 Waimea Bay archive adds 17,854 hourly images paired with CDIP 106 at 6.3 km ([Waimea datasheet](../docs/datasets/pacioos_beachcam.md), PROJ). ERA5 and the USACE WIS hindcast serve as model baselines and never as labels; WIS ends 2025-12-31, so 2026 work has only ERA5. Four small external sets sit on disk for method checks: ship stereo with platform motion, Duck thermal infrared with lidar, Antarctic stereo, and the Yin et al. breaker-type clips ([external datasets](../docs/datasets/external_research_datasets.md), PROJ).

### What the models and the protocol are

Three model families have been scored. The buoy model is a ConvNeXt-Tiny that reads each of the six views, mixes them with a one-layer transformer and regresses total, wind-wave and swell height; its best version starts from DINOv3-distilled weights and is tuned gently, and later stages add a distribution head with buoy-level conformal intervals and heads for period, spectral shape and direction. The shore-video model is a frozen V-JEPA 2 ViT-L read by an attentive probe over 16-second windows, averaged over every window of a clip, beside a label-free pixel-brightness spectrum. Fusion is late and tabular: camera outputs are combined with ERA5 and buoy wind by ridge regression or by a gated "partition swap" that replaces the model's wind-sea energy with the camera's. Every comparison is a "bar" committed before its runs, decided by a paired bootstrap over whole buoys, station groups or dates, with three-seed averages; grouped cross-validation over 43 station groups (buoys within 300 km chained together) now lets all 79 buoys act once as unseen buoys ([buoy experiment log](../docs/experiments/ndbc_buoycam_sea_state.md), PROJ). Reference re-implementations of all four target papers exist as code (`Choi2020Regressor`, `Kim2023CNNBiLSTM`, `Yang2026Fusion`, `SlowFastWaveHead`), yet a search of every script and config on `main` and all branches found **no training run that uses them**; comparison so far computes each paper's metric from the project's own model ([model notes](../docs/methodology/models.md), PROJ).

### What the experiments have shown, in plain terms

At 12 buoys the model never saw, the best strip model misses significant wave height by **0.440 m** typically (R² 0.82); over all 79 buoys out of fold the figure is 0.532 m, pulled up by the US West Coast fold (0.70 m), where swell dominates. ERA5 at the same buoys misses by 0.267 m and 0.292 m, so **the camera alone loses on total height**. Split the sea into its two parts and the picture reverses for one of them: on wind-wave height the camera misses by 0.260 m against ERA5's 0.343 m out of fold (better by about 0.09 m, interval 0.07 to 0.11), and by 0.215 against 0.341 m at the sealed buoys. Fused as wind-sea energy with ERA5 and buoy wind, the camera lowers total-height error from 0.288 to **0.267 m** (difference −0.021 m, interval −0.031 to −0.012) and wind-wave error from 0.293 to 0.240 m at 77 buoys; this is the first fusion bar met, out of fold, and it awaits confirmation on strips archived after the package ([buoy experiment log](../docs/experiments/ndbc_buoycam_sea_state.md), PROJ). The same pattern holds for period: the camera beats ERA5 on wind-sea peak period (0.69 against 0.93 s) and wind-sea energy period (0.55 against 0.75 s), a result recorded as "found, not confirmed", and loses badly on total peak period (3.44 against 1.70 s), energy period (2.40 against 0.67 s) and mean direction (76° against 14°). A control that sees only sun position, location and time scores 61.6° on direction, better than the camera's 75.8°, because no strip carries a compass heading. Training period, shape and direction heads jointly with height made height worse by 0.086 m. Adding horizon roll and pitch as inputs made height worse by 0.038 m. Swapping backbones moved height by about 0.01 m once each had a suitable recipe.

On shore, the Currituck camera adds a modest, consistent amount to a hindcast (macro-F1 gains of 0.04 to 0.06 on held-out years) but does not beat WIS alone on a held-out year, and Waimea image statistics add nothing once WIS is present ([Currituck experiment](../docs/experiments/currituck_sea_state.md), PROJ; [Waimea datasheet](../docs/datasets/pacioos_beachcam.md), PROJ). The interim video run on 992 clips and 18 dates found that motion inside a 16-second window adds nothing for height (ordered, frozen-frame and shuffled-frame inputs all score 0.533 to 0.535 m), that averaging every window of a clip helps by 2 % (0.513 against 0.524 m, interval below zero), and that **no learned probe beats climatology on peak period** (2.86 against 2.72 s); the label-free brightness spectrum does (2.46 s), although the bar for adding it to the video probe was not met on all clips (interval −0.74 to +0.02 s) and clears zero only on clips with at least 15 windows ([video experiment](../docs/experiments/webcoos_video.md), PROJ). The reading across all of this: every bar that asked the camera for the wind sea, or for more looks at one record, was met; every bar that asked for total height alone, swell-driven period, direction or a better encoder failed or was level.

Three readings follow. First, the published accuracy bar for camera height and period is set by protocols the project already treats as leaky, and where the literature itself changed protocol the numbers fell: Buscombe's period error quadruples outside the calibration range, the ship sea-state group dropped from 88 % to F1 47 % on a new test set, and a course report that reproduces Buscombe-like errors on a surf camera (0.17 m, 0.4 s) explains only 51 % and 21 % of the variance ([Egan report](https://galenegan.github.io/files/final_report.pdf), FT, not peer reviewed). A paired "random split against held-out days" result on the project's own shore data would quantify that inflation for wave height for the first time. Second, where splits are honest the learned method lands at about the classical method's error (0.37 m learned depth on a held-out year against 0.38 m for cBathy at the same site), and for period the non-learned single-camera methods are the baseline a reviewer will expect. Third, **no published work applies DINOv2/v3, V-JEPA 2 or a comparable encoder to wave height, period, direction or spectra with instrument labels and a leakage-free split**; the only video-foundation-model wave papers are the two Kamagata preprints, which have zero citations ([citation record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/ARXIV:2607.11998?fields=title,citationCount), API). Beating Kamagata therefore carries little weight by itself. The real competitors are Buscombe 2020, the non-learned period methods, the ship sea-state group, and a cluster of 2026 *Ocean Engineering* papers that cite Choi, Kim and Xu together and could not be read (listed in the last section). No claim to have beaten any of the four targets was found, and no public benchmark or leaderboard exists for camera wave height, period, direction or spectra; the rip-current community's path from dataset paper to challenge to a unified benchmark with video-level intervals ([RipVIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01128), FT) is the template if the project releases one.

## Data not yet held: individual-wave truth is a download away, in-view truth is a request away

### Richer truth for imagery already on disk

The largest gain needs no new imagery. **CDIP serves raw x, y, z displacement for every deployment of every buoy the shore cameras are paired with**, in per-deployment files the project has not downloaded, and also a ready-made zero-crossing product with one row per wave (height, period, direction, crest and trough displacement, running H_1/3 and H_1/10), free of restrictions ([CDIP archive](https://thredds.cdip.ucsd.edu/thredds/catalog/cdip/archive/433p1/catalog.html), API; [zero-crossing file header](https://thredds.cdip.ucsd.edu/thredds/dodsC/cdip/zcross/realtime/433p1_d14_zc.nc.html), API). That gives every existing clip and still a time-domain significant height, mean individual height (Kim's actual target), zero-crossing period, H_1/10, wave counts and group statistics, and it lets Kamagata's five outputs be trained against an instrument instead of untraceable labels. The pairing is statistical, not wave by wave, because the buoys sit 6 to 29 km from the cameras; a buoy's largest wave in half an hour cannot identify a breaker filmed at the beach. One caution from the repository: every displacement window matched so far carries the provider's "not evaluated" quality flag (`docs/experiments/measurement_checkin.md` on branch `claude/wave-measurements-review`, PROJ).

### Other sensors that carry wave information at the project's sites

**Seismic noise is the strongest find.** Ocean waves interacting near a coast shake the ground at twice the wave frequency, so the secondary microseism's spectral peak reads out swell period directly and its amplitude tracks wave energy over a coastal area, day and night, from a source that is not a wave model. A random forest on coastal broadband noise reached a mean absolute error of 0.14 to 0.18 m against two wave buoys on a later-months test at fixed stations in Sicily ([Moschella et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feart.2020.00114/full), FT), seismic-derived height correlates with local waves at r = 0.93 on daily averages in California ([Ardhuin et al. 2012](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00083/19443), ABS), and a 2021 abstract reports a recurrent network doing it on the US East Coast with no numbers ([Bolrão et al. 2021](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU21/EGU21-14837.html), ABS). Open stations with data verified in the archive sit **25 km from Waimea Bay** (IU.KIP, 1,727 of 1,733 days at least 90 % complete across 2009-02 to 2013-10) and **14 km from the Oak Island cameras** (N4.Y60A, 1,681 of 1,764 days from 2022-01 to 2026-10) ([KIP availability](https://service.earthscope.org/mustang/measurements/1/query?metric=percent_availability&network=IU&station=KIP&channel=BHZ&location=00&timewindow=2009-02-01,2013-10-31&format=text), API; [Y60A availability](https://service.earthscope.org/mustang/measurements/1/query?metric=percent_availability&network=N4&station=Y60A&channel=HHZ&location=00&timewindow=2022-01-01,2026-10-31&format=text), API). The station 8 km from Waimea is mostly absent from the archive for the camera years, and the nearest permanent broadband station to Duck and Jennette's Pier is 86 to 90 km away on the mainland. No published pairing of seismic noise with wave imagery was found. The honest expectation is noisier than the Sicilian figures: an open-coast station also hears distant storms, and the mapping is station-specific.

The second find is a nearshore proxy that already exists at Duck. NOAA's tide gauge on the pier publishes every six minutes the standard deviation of 181 one-second water levels, and a NOAA report shows that this single number tracks wave height at the pier to **0.14 m RMS** with one coefficient ([NOAA Tech. Rep. NOS CO-OPS 075](https://www.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/publications/NOAA_Tech_075_Microwave_Water_Level_2014_Final.pdf), FT). It is height only, one site, and it reads swell better than chop, the opposite bias to the camera's. Satellites cannot label shore-camera frames but accumulate over 79 buoys: roughly 150 daylight altimeter passes within 50 km per buoy-year (EST, from orbit geometry, not a tally), and the CFOSAT SWIM instrument resolves exactly the 70 to 500 m swell band the camera misses, with partition period and direction errors of about 0.9 s and 20° against NDBC ([SWIM validation](https://essopenarchive.org/doi/full/10.1002/essoar.10506895.1), SNIP; [CFOSAT handbook](https://www.aviso.altimetry.fr/fileadmin/documents/data/tools/CFOSAT_L2PBOX_handbook_SALP.pdf), FT). SWIM and Sentinel-1 pass near dawn and dusk, so they serve as an independent check on the buoy's and model's swell labels, a year-two evaluation asset, not as partners for the image. At Waimea a daily human surf observation (1968 to 2020, stated uncertainty 10 to 15 %, CC0) and archived forecast text cover the camera years ([Goddard-Caldwell record](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/goddard-caldwell-daily-visual-surf-observation-database-daily-visual-surf-observation-2020), ABS), which makes Waimea the one site where image, buoy, seismometer, human observation and forecast text coexist. Surf infrasound at 1 to 5 Hz tracks breaking height ([Garcés et al. 2003](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Garces_etal2003.pdf), SNIP), but the only sensor near a project camera is a citizen unit about 5 km from Masonboro Inlet, and whether WebCOOS clips carry an audio track is unknown; inspecting the clips already on disk answers that in a minute.

Not worth pursuing now: HF radar (a station shares Jennette's Pier but publishes currents only, and long-range units see waves mainly in high seas; [radar poster](https://ooifb.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/2024_HFR_SMAB_Pioneer_Array_final.pdf), FT); CYGNSS reflectometry (25 km cells, wave height at 0.43 m against NDBC, about the camera's own error and the same wind-roughness physics, mission reported to be ending; [SCAWaveNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00701), ABS); scatterometer winds; North Carolina forecast text; and the excellent Duck fibre-optic, X-band and drifter experiments of 2021 to 2022, which precede the public camera record. Platform motion as a co-input now has two independent real-data nulls, the project's buoys and a three-vessel radar thesis ([TU Delft 2026](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:29d1170b-30b0-4e46-b86e-0ac5c259f891), ABS), against two simulation gains; that disagreement is a reportable finding and a reason to stop spending on it.

## Raise the model-only bar, and change the signal before the encoder

### Wave-model baselines and fusion partners beyond ERA5 and WIS

The cheapest large upgrade over ERA5 bulk parameters is public. NOAA's GFS-Wave publishes, for each NDBC buoy position, an hourly bulletin of total height plus up to six tracked wave systems, each with height, peak period, direction and a flag for local wind generation, and the same station set sits in NOAA's open-data bucket (verified for one 2025 date; the autumn 2026 dates still need checking) ([bulletin sample](https://nomads.ncep.noaa.gov/pub/data/nccf/com/gfs/prod/gfs.20261119/00/wave/station/bulls.t00z/gfswave.41025.bull), API; [archive listing](https://noaa-gfs-bdp-pds.s3.amazonaws.com/?list-type=2&prefix=gfs.20250115/00/wave/station/&delimiter=/), API). Those wave systems come from spectral partitioning at the buoy point, closer to NDBC's definition than ERA5's wind-sea and total-swell split, which the project found disagrees with NDBC's (ERA5's swell error exceeds its total error). Copernicus Marine's MFWAM analysis adds an independent model family at 1/12° with wind-sea and two swell partitions ([product page](https://data.marine.copernicus.eu/product/GLOBAL_ANALYSISFORECAST_WAV_001_027/description), FT), and its reanalysis scored 0.33 m against ERA5's 0.37 m at 326 coastal buoys ([Commun. Earth Environ. 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s43247-023-00819-0), SNIP). ERA5's two-dimensional spectra remain retrievable only through a slow archive queue, and ERA6 will not publish before late 2027 ([ERA5 documentation](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/display/CKB/ERA5%3A+data+documentation), FT; [Copernicus, March 2026](https://climate.copernicus.eu/copernicus-climates-era6-reanalysis-production-starts), FT). NOAA's nearshore SWAN system (NWPS) publishes two-dimensional spectra at the Duck, Oregon Inlet and other North Carolina buoys but keeps **only five days online**, and no archive was found ([NWPS listing](https://nomads.ncep.noaa.gov/pub/data/nccf/com/nwps/prod/er.20261119/mhx/00/CG1/), API); like the buoy-camera images, it exists for the project only from the day collection starts.

AI forecasters do not change the baseline. ECMWF's AIFS Single v2 outputs total height, period, direction and six long-period band heights with no partitions; Microsoft's Aurora wave model has partitions but needs ECMWF's own wave analysis as input and is scored against that analysis, not buoys ([AIFS weights](https://huggingface.co/ecmwf/aifs-single-2.0), API; [Aurora](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13063v3), FT). For a nowcast at observation time a forecaster adds nothing the analysis it starts from does not already hold. Time-series foundation models forecast a series from its own past and so cannot set a baseline at an unseen buoy.

The bar that matters is one the literature does not supply. Published buoy-trained correctors cut model error by 10 to 60 %, but they feed the corrector the same buoy's recent observations ([Dawson et al. 2022](https://ai4earthscience.github.io/iclr-2022-workshop/camera_ready/iclr_2022_ai4ess_24.pdf), FT). The fair model-only bar for a camera is a corrector on model features alone (ERA5, GFS-Wave and MFWAM parameters, wind, depth, location), fitted under the same grouped folds with no buoy history, and no published study reports that number for NDBC; it has to be measured in-house. The fusion rule the project found has a long operational pedigree: wave-model assimilation splits the spectrum into partitions, corrects each partition's parameters by optimal interpolation and rebuilds the total ([Voorrips et al. 1997](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Voorrips_etal_JGR1997.pdf), SNIP). The literature-backed refinement is to weight instead of replace. With the project's errors of 0.215 m for the camera and 0.341 m for ERA5 on wind-wave height, and assuming independent, unbiased errors, the camera's weight is about 0.72 and the combined wind-sea error about 0.18 m (EST); correlated errors would shrink that. The only precedent for camera data inside a wave model corrects a boundary condition with video-derived peak period ([Saavedra et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cageo.2023.105407), SNIP), and a NOAA analysis that assimilates webcam visibility inflates the camera's error at low confidence ([Carley et al. 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/experimental-results/article/variational-assimilation-of-web-cameraderived-estimates-of-visibility-for-alaska-aviation/3303F8BEC7EB9BDCF7D412FF11F238D9), FT), the same idea as the project's visibility gate. For the shore cameras, linear theory puts the shoaling change at the reference buoys' 13 to 17 m depth under 10 % (EST), so buoy-to-camera differences come from refraction and sheltering; CDIP's nearshore transformation product covers California only ([Ludka et al. 2019](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/0cp9087z), FT), and a small stationary SWAN look-up table per site is the physics route if one is wanted.

### Perception and physics model families

The model family with real-data support for the project's stuck result is not a new encoder but a different input signal. Every single-camera period estimate that agrees with a reference takes a spectrum of a scalar physical signal over minutes: the vertical displacement of ripple texture in a small box matched a radar's peak period within 3 % on average and 7 % at worst over eleven 20-minute records ([Yu et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/aos/EN/PDF/10.1007/s13131-024-2359-y), FT; the reference is a radar inversion, and all cases are near 6 s). On X-band radar, a 0.7-million-parameter transformer fed only the strongest wavenumber-frequency points of each sequence matched a 3-D CNN on the raw sequence (0.23 against 0.25 m) at about a ninetieth of the compute, and both halved a single-image model's error of 0.51 m ([Spectral Point Transformer](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04581v1), FT, one site). That is the project's own lesson from the other side: one image reads the wind sea, and the gain is on the time axis. No paper applies a dispersion-filtered optical 3-D spectrum to a learned model (SNIP negative).

The other families sort into infrastructure, baseline rows and deferrals. Promptable segmentation is demonstrated for water, sky and land at unseen shores (water IoU 0.97 to 0.99, beating a locally trained segmenter; [vessel-localisation study](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21276v1), FT), which automates horizon lines, surf-zone crops after a camera re-points, and masks for people; nothing applies it to whitecaps or crests. Vision-language models have never been tested on sea state from camera images, and on the nearest benchmark they answer from priors more than pixels: the best model scores 53 against a human 56, and removing the video barely changes its score ([QuantiPhy](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19526), FT). A zero-shot row costs about $1 to $50 by API or one to three GPU-hours locally (EST) and would be the first such baseline in a wave paper; its expected role is quality control, not measurement. Learned single-image calibration gives focal length to about 7 to 14 % but pitch only to 1 to 2° ([GeoCalib](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06704v2), FT), far too coarse at grazing angles where a 0.2° error is an 11 % range error (EST), so pitch and roll must come from the horizon and camera height from site information; that combination is untested on any coastal camera and is the gate to direction and wavelength in physical units. Physics-informed networks and neural operators for waves are demonstrated almost entirely on synthetic data and need inputs the cameras do not supply. No method recovers metric sea-surface elevation from one ordinary camera. No camera wave estimator trained in simulation has a real-data transfer result, and a classical method's frequency bias changed sign between rendered and real clips ([ICPR 2026 slides](https://icpr2026orgteam.github.io/OralPres/1062.pdf), FT), so simulation is safe here only for one question: render the same wind sea with and without swell at the real camera geometry and ask whether any front end can tell them apart. Generative video has no parameter that sets wave height or period. Event, polarimetric and thermal sensors are not in the data streams.

## Four roles for a second signal, and how far the evidence supports each

Everything in this section beyond the cited effect sizes is a proposal; the effect sizes come from other domains, because no cross-sensor learning study with cameras was found for ocean waves.

**Co-input (a second signal at test time).** The project already fuses hindcast partitions and buoy wind. The new co-input worth testing is seismic band energy, because it supplies the swell the camera lacks from outside the wave model. Evidence on fusion design is consistent across domains: decision-level combination is the most robust when an input goes missing but loses accuracy when the inputs are complementary, feature-level fusion collapses in regression when an input vanishes (R² from 0.82 to −1.4 in one Earth-observation benchmark; [Mena et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14297v2.pdf), FT), and a learned quality gate beat ungated fusion by 7 to 13 points when one sensor degraded, with ungated fusion falling below the camera alone in dense fog ([Bijelic et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1902.08913), FT). The project's energy-space partition swap with a visibility gate is already the right skeleton. What it still lacks is a standing table with four columns (both inputs, camera only, model only, camera degraded) and controls that permute the camera's embeddings within a buoy and within a hindcast-state bin, the only control that supports "the camera measures something the model lacks".

**Training-only teacher (privileged information).** A richer record available only in training can improve an image-only student by 1 to 7 points in other fields ([Garcia et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/1806.07110), FT), under two limits that map directly onto the wave spectrum. A teacher whose advantage lies in information the student's input cannot contain yields a no-better or worse student ([Xue et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.06487), FT), and the benefit vanishes as labelled data grow (+4.5 points at 75,000 images, nothing at 200,000; [Lambert et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1805.11614), FT). So distilling swell bins, peak period or direction into the buoy model will not help, and the project already has a matching data point: joint training with period, shape and direction heads cost 0.086 m of height. Camera-visible teachers are the candidates: high-frequency band energy, buoy wind, slope. Three teachers are new in kind. Displacement-derived wave statistics teach the shore model time-domain quantities. A nearshore gauge (the Duck tide-gauge variance, or the FRF lidar gauges) teaches a shore camera the height at the shore instead of at a buoy kilometres away. And the ten-minute video teaches a student that sees only the still, `timex` and `brt` images of the same clip, of which about 1,300 clips have all four inputs and about 108,000 have the three images.

**Self-supervised pretext (no labels).** The best-supported label-free objective for this data is periodicity learning. Methods built for camera heart-rate estimation learn a band-limited periodic signal from unlabelled clips with three frequency-domain losses and reach supervised accuracy ([SiNC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.07944), FT); a contrastive variant cuts frozen-feature frequency error by a quarter to nearly a half against generic self-supervision on four benchmarks but was level on a satellite temperature task, so the advantage is task-dependent ([SimPer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03115), FT). Applied to shore video it needs no buoy, so every clip from every camera trains it, and period is conserved to the shore. The main risk is that seas are often bimodal, and admitting a second peak hurt in the heart-rate work. If self-supervised pretraining on buoy views continues after bar P1, the objective should change before the budget does: use the six simultaneous views of a strip as positives and other days from the same camera as negatives, so camera identity cannot solve the task. Positives that encode a true physical invariance were worth 7.6 points of frozen-feature accuracy in satellite imagery ([Geography-Aware SSL](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.09980), FT); nothing tests this for sea state. Predicting the `timex` and `brt` images from one frame has no precedent that was found; it stays a proposal conditional on the real `timex` image helping as an input.

**Camera-predicted target (the camera as a virtual sensor).** Cameras have been validated as point sensors for wind, temperature and rainfall with useful but sub-instrument errors and poor transfer unless features are normalised per scene: subtracting each clip's mean feature cut wind-speed error on unseen objects from 3.1 to 1.9 m/s and from 9.6 to 1.8 m/s ([Cardona et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1905.13290), FT). For the buoy cameras the natural targets are the anemometer's wind speed, band-limited slope and energy by frequency band, all on the same hull and all trainable today. An image-to-spectrum contrastive embedding is feasible at 41,000 pairs, but the closest scientific analogue shows where its value lies: aligning galaxy images to spectra raised nearest-neighbour read-out by up to 0.19 R² and a trained probe by only 0.00 to 0.03 ([AstroCLIP](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03024v2), FT). Its case is retrieval ("days like this"), not lower error. Language does not help regression: text-prototype read-out trails a linear probe on the same encoder by about 10 points of R² ([EarthAlign](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14624v1), FT). A small any-to-any masked model over image tokens, spectral bins, weather scalars and hindcast tokens is buildable at this scale (one Earth-observation instance trained on 127,155 samples; [Galileo](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09356v3), FT), but the published gain of such pretraining over a model trained from scratch is 1 to 2 points ([TerraMind](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11171v3), FT) and its main risk is learning hindcast-to-buoy while ignoring pixels.

**A cheaper lever than any of these for unseen sites.** Purpose-built domain-generalisation algorithms have not beaten plain training, but methods that look at unlabelled data from the new environment have: 25 unlabelled context samples raised unseen-environment accuracy by 2 to 22 points ([ICRM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.09888), FT), and a regression-specific test-time alignment improved R² where classification-style adaptation diverged ([Adachi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03263v2), FT). Standardising each camera's cached embeddings by that camera's own unlabelled archive costs CPU hours. The caveat is that a camera's mean embedding also encodes its sea climate, so the statistics must be computed within hindcast-state bins.

Tests 4 to 6 ask the buoy camera for things it has been shown to see and yield first benchmarks. Four actions are not tests and should not wait. Start a daily pull of the NWPS spectra and grids for the North Carolina and Hawaii offices, because five-day retention makes every uncollected day unrecoverable. Read the five unread 2026 papers as PDFs. Inspect the container of the clips already on disk for an audio track. Make one small seismic test download to confirm that the availability metrics mean accessible data. Held back deliberately: the Duck tide-gauge teacher, until the camera-to-pier distance is recomputed from the camera's EXIF position; the request to the FRF for Argus pixel stacks or clips against the lidar gauges, which is the single action that would deliver in-view truth at scale; satellite swell partitions as a year-two evaluation asset; sea-state-aware contrastive continuation, until bar P1 is scored; and the image-to-spectrum embedding and any-to-any model, which only matter if test 5 shows usable skill below the wind-sea band.

### What this confirms, extends and changes in the earlier reports

The new findings **confirm** the [architecture report](Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones.md)'s central call that the camera should hand the model a wind sea, now with an out-of-fold bar met and with operational assimilation practice behind the partition rule; they confirm the [measurement report](Wave%20measurements%20to%20learn%20next.md)'s two targets, slope for the buoys and period for the shore, since no learned optical precedent was found for slope and the strongest published period methods are non-learned spectral ones; and they confirm the [backbone report](Vision%20backbones%20for%20wave%20cameras.md)'s line that swell is a data problem, because radar and reflectometry share the camera's dependence on wind roughness. They **extend** the measurement report's suggestion to derive Kim's target from displacement: coverage is now verified for every paired station and a per-wave product already exists, so the step is a download, not a derivation. They extend the architecture report's physics front ends with three signals that have real-data support (texture displacement, a near-field 3-D spectrum, spectral points) and its fusion head with a stronger model-only bar. They extend the [October data-source scan](../docs/literature/data_source_scan_2026-10.md) with sources it did not have: FRF lidar elevation series, USGS timestacks with a nearby ADCP, per-wave CDIP files, the stereo collection that was unreachable then, and the first non-camera partners. They **change** five things. The four-paper goal needs the papers' own networks run on project data, which the earlier reports assumed and the repository has not done. Kamagata's benchmark is the weakest of the four to beat, and Buscombe 2020 plus the unread 2026 cluster matter more. Direction from buoy stills is closed, not pending a caption heading, since the caption carries none and a no-image control wins. Platform motion as a co-input is closed on two real-data nulls. And continued pretraining, if it goes on, should change its objective to same-camera negatives before it changes its scale. Two factual corrections carry over from the repository: the Currituck camera is 9.15 km from its buoy by the image's own GPS position, not the 21.6 to 22.2 km in older documents, and the archive's clips there are 600 s long, not four minutes.

The GFS-Wave archive was verified for one date in January 2025, not for the autumn 2026 label period. The Goddard-Caldwell record's access flag contradicts its licence and the page would not load.

Inside the repository, bar P1, the fusion confirmation, the final video run and bar M-S1 have no scored result yet, so four internal baselines will move within about a week. Sources disagree on SWOT's wave-height accuracy against buoys (the project's table and a 2025 coastal check differ by a factor of two), on the ERA6 release date (the two ECMWF and Copernicus primary pages say end of 2027), and on which CYGNSS satellites remain active. WebCOOS's position on redistributing frames is unrecorded, and raw clips on shared compute need its consent first.

## Conclusion

The question has moved one step past the earlier reports. They asked which network and then how much of each record a model reads; the evidence now says the unit of design is the pairing of a measurement with the signal that carries it. On that view the project's two failed directions are informative rather than discouraging: a still image is a roughness sensor, so it measures the wind sea, slope and wind, and it is in the same physical class as marine radar and satellite reflectometry, which fail the same way without wind; a 16-second window is not a period sensor, but minutes of a scalar physical signal are. The swell that neither sees has an untried, model-independent witness in the ground itself. A system built from a camera for the wind sea and a seismometer for the swell, each read by its own small model and added as energies, would be new in kind, and its first test costs a download.

The standing against the four papers changes meaning accordingly. The durable contribution is not a higher number on their tasks but a set of first benchmarks under a protocol none of them used: unseen buoys, held-out dates, instrument labels, a no-camera baseline strong enough to be worth beating, and controls that show what the pixels add. The papers' own networks, run under that protocol on the project's data, turn "ahead on a harder split" into a measured difference, and the five unread 2026 papers decide whether "first" survives. Both are cheap, and both come before the next training run.
