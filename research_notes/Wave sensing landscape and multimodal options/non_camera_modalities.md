# Non-camera sensing modalities that carry ocean-wave information (survey as of 2026-11-19)

Researcher notes for the report writer. Scope: every non-camera modality that could be paired with the project's cameras (NDBC BuoyCAM on 79 buoys; WebCOOS shore cameras at Currituck/Duck, Jennette's Pier, Oak Island, Masonboro Inlet; Waimea Bay beach cam 2009-2013) as a co-input, a training-only teacher, or an independent label.

## 1. Satellites: altimetry, SWOT, CFOSAT SWIM, Sentinel-1 SAR, Sentinel-2 optical. How many co-locations per year near a fixed site or buoy, and which merged products exist?

### Takeaway
Satellites cannot label individual shore-camera frames, but across 79 buoys they add up: roughly 30 altimeter passes per year per mission come within 50 km of any one site, so the ten-mission Copernicus stream gives on the order of 300 passes per site-year, about half in daylight (my estimate, method under Inferences). The instruments that carry what the camera lacks (swell height, period, direction by partition) are CFOSAT SWIM (wavelengths 70-500 m) and Sentinel-1, but both fly at dawn and dusk local time, so their camera pairs are low-sun frames. SWOT's resolved-swell product only sees waves longer than about 500 m (period above about 18 s), which matters for Hawaii winter swell and almost never for North Carolina.

**SWOT**
- The SWOT Level-3 wind-wave product (`L3_LR_WIND_WAVE`, handbook issue 2.0, October 2026, product version 3.0) derives 2-D wave spectra from the 250-m sea-surface-height images. It "only resolves long waves": wavelengths above about 500 m (about 18 s), down to about 342 m on the diagonal. Spectra are computed on 40 x 40 km boxes with 5-km tiles; swell partitions give a height, wavelength and direction each. From version 3 the product also carries the swath wave-height field on a 2-km grid, taken from the Level-2 version D data. Free for science and commerce under the AVISO+ licence. [FT, handbook] — [AVISO SWOT L3 wind-wave handbook](https://aviso.altimetry.fr/fileadmin/documents/data/tools/hdbk_SWOT_L3_LR_WIND_WAVE.pdf)
- The same handbook states that SWOT's precision "makes it possible to use SWOT to calibrate other sensors (such as SWIM and Sentinel-1 SAR)" that rely on empirical transfer functions or neural networks. This is an agency statement of the teacher idea between sensors. [FT] — [AVISO handbook](https://aviso.altimetry.fr/fileadmin/documents/data/tools/hdbk_SWOT_L3_LR_WIND_WAVE.pdf)
- Bohé et al. (2025, *IEEE TGRS*, doi 10.1109/TGRS.2025.3551605) show kilometre-scale wave-height maps from the KaRIn swath (2 km and 5 km versions), validated against a GPS buoy in the Southern Ocean during the 1-day-repeat phase (31 March to 9 July 2023, 96 days, 8 unusable), airborne lidar, Sentinel-3, SWOT's own nadir altimeter and the ECMWF model; biases between KaRIn and the others are "centimetric". In one extreme case (wave height above 9 m) the 2-km value differed from the buoy by 69 cm and the 5-km value by 24 cm; a simulation showed that wave-group sampling alone explains differences of 35 cm (2 km) and 26 cm (5 km) in that case. [FT, author's accepted version] — [Bohé et al. 2025](https://airsea.ucsd.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2025/04/2025_Bohe_et_al_IEEE.pdf)
- Coastal check: KaRIn wave height against GNSS buoys in Bass Strait had bias about 6 cm and centred RMSE about 21 cm (Hay et al. 2025, as cited by a 2026 preprint). [SEC] — [EGUsphere 2026-3219](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2026-3219/)
- SWOT detects 25-s swell with heights as low as 3 cm and resolves its period and direction (Ardhuin et al. 2024, *GRL*). [SEC] — [Archimer copy](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00905/101689/112658.pdf)
- The project's own table lists SWOT KaRIn at bias 0.11 m and RMSE 0.43 m against buoys. [PD] Not re-verified; the Bass Strait figure above is much better, so the two numbers should not be quoted together without their sources.

**CFOSAT SWIM**
- CFOSAT launched 29 October 2018 and is listed by AVISO under current missions with no end date; it carries SWIM (wave direction, amplitude and wavelength) and a scatterometer. [AB, mission page] — [AVISO CFOSAT](https://www.aviso.altimetry.fr/en/missions/current-missions/cfosat.html)
- Geometry and timing, from the product handbook (issue 1.3, 25 April 2024): directional spectra are built on "off nadir boxes of about 90 x 70 km on each side of the satellite track"; the 10-degree beam is the one distributed; each box gives partitions with height, wavelength and direction. The orbit is sun-synchronous "with an ascending pass at the equator around 7:00", inclination 97.465 degrees, 394 passes per cycle, and "the repeat cycle is 13 days" (same path within 20 km). [FT, handbook] — [CFOSAT L2PBOX handbook](https://www.aviso.altimetry.fr/fileadmin/documents/data/tools/CFOSAT_L2PBOX_handbook_SALP.pdf)
- SWIM resolves directional spectra for wavelengths of about 70-500 m. Partition-level validation against NDBC buoys: RMSE 0.9 s for peak period and 20 degrees for peak direction; partition wave height is weaker (noisy spectra upset the partitioning). An earlier (2021) study found dominant-direction RMSE above 40 degrees. [SEC] — [ESS Open Archive preprint](https://essopenarchive.org/doi/full/10.1002/essoar.10506895.1); [Remote Sens. 13:887](https://0-www-mdpi-com.brum.beds.ac.uk/2072-4292/13/5/887)
- A 2026 *Ocean Dynamics* paper (Canto et al.) validated SWIM against a Southern Ocean mooring, NDBC buoys and the Sofar drifting-buoy archive: wave height and peak direction good, period weaker, the 10-degree beam best, and a neural-network inversion improved mean period. [SEC] — [publication record](https://discover.unhas.ac.id/publications/pub_scopus_105037679101)
- IMOS distributes a CFOSAT SWIM dataset calibrated on NDBC buoys, described as a trial. [SEC] — [researchdata.edu.au](https://researchdata.edu.au/imos-satellite-remote-scatterometer-wave/4247117)

**Sentinel-1 SAR**
- Constellation and orbit per ESA's wiki: sun-synchronous, **local time at ascending node 18:00**, altitude 693 km, 12-day repeat per satellite (6 days with two). Sentinel-1A end of operations planned for end of June 2026; Sentinel-1B ended 2022; Sentinel-1C fully operational since early May 2025; Sentinel-1D launched 4 November 2025, expected fully operational mid-April 2026. Wave mode is "the default mode for acquiring data over open ocean" (20 x 20 km images every 100 km, two alternating incidence angles); "over land and coastal areas: pre-defined mode is IW". [AB, mission page] — [SentiWiki S1 mission](https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s1-mission)
- Since June 2022 the wave-mode ocean product includes total wave height from the neural network of Quach et al., trained on altimeter matches; that network cut RMSE from 0.6 to 0.3 m against altimeters. [SEC] — [SAR-MPC news](https://sar-mpc.eu/about/news); [Quach et al. (Caltech record)](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20200724-100654154)
- DLR's SAR-SeaStaR machine-learning retrieval reports wave-height RMSE of 0.25 m for wave mode and 0.38 m for wide-swath (IW) scenes worldwide. [SEC] — [DLR elib 216451](https://elib.dlr.de/216451/1/JSTARS3672664.pdf)
- **The published count of coastal SAR-buoy matches for US waters:** Owda et al. (2024, *IEEE JSTARS* 17:9415-9428) processed the whole Sentinel-1A/B wide-swath archive 2014-2022 over three US coastal study areas. About 2,900 scenes matched NDBC buoys, giving about 10,500 wave and 5,200 wind matches over 74 buoys; about 6,700 passed filtering and were used; 54 buoys entered the wave-height validation. Sea-state fields were retrieved on a 2.5 x 2.5 km raster (CWAVE_EX, a feature-based method), matched to buoy records within at most 1 hour. Against buoys and the Meteo-France wave model, RMSE was 0.46-0.50 m for wave height and 0.9-1.1 s for the second-moment period. The most-matched buoy was 46011. [FT, partial] — [Owda et al. 2024 (DLR elib)](https://elib.dlr.de/204599/2/Evaluation_of_SAR-Based_Sea_State_Parameters_and_Roughness_Length_Derivation_Over_the_Coastal_Seas_of_the_USA.pdf)
- A CNN study on wide-swath scenes used 1,597 Sentinel-1 images matched to buoys (about 3,330 matches); its error was not captured. [SEC] — [AMiner record](https://aminer.org/pub/5ff680d8d4150a363cb53c03/significant-wave-height-retrieval-from-sentinel-sar-imagery-by-convolutional-neural-network)
- Self-supervised SAR models (WV-Net: linear-probe RMSE 0.50 m against 0.60 m supervised; OceanSAR-2) are already in the project table. [PD]

**Sentinel-2 optical**
- Kudryavtsev et al. (2017, *JGR Oceans*, Part 1) retrieve directional wave spectra from Sentinel-2 sun-glitter brightness gradients; the small time lag between spectral bands resolves the 180-degree direction ambiguity and gives the local dispersion relation; agreement with buoys is called "encouraging" with no numbers in the abstract. [AB] — [Archimer 49280](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00381/49280)
- Wave-based satellite bathymetry uses the same band time lag to get wave celerity and from it depth ("Global Satellite-Based Coastal Bathymetry from Waves", *Remote Sensing* 13:4628). Conventional optical bathymetry studies treat glint and swell as things to screen out. [SEC] — [MDPI 13/22/4628](https://mdpi.com/2072-4292/13/22/4628)

### Inferences
- **Expected altimeter passes near a fixed point (my estimate, not a published number).** For a satellite making n revolutions a day, the yearly number of passes within d km of a point at latitude phi is about 2 n (2d) / (2 pi R cos(phi) sin(psi)) x 365, where psi is the angle between the ground track and the parallel. With d = 50 km this gives about 32 passes a year per mission at 35 N (North Carolina), about 28 at 21.6 N (Oahu), about 38-40 at 45 N; halve it for 25 km. Sun-synchronous missions (Sentinel-3, SARAL, HY-2) and the Jason/Sentinel-6 orbit come out within a pass or two of each other. With the ten missions in the Copernicus stream that is roughly 320 passes per site-year at 35 N, at all hours. Repeat-orbit missions do not spread evenly: a given site is either near a ground track (about 37 passes a year on the 10-day Jason/Sentinel-6 orbit) or never visited by that mission.
- **For the 79 buoy cameras:** buoys report hourly, so every pass has a buoy record within 30 minutes; roughly half fall in daylight. That gives about 150 daylight camera-altimeter pairs per buoy-year, about 12,000 a year across 79 buoys as an upper bound (fewer where the buoy is close to land and the altimeter footprint is contaminated, or at the 25-km radius). Since the camera archive only began in late September 2026, about 8 weeks exist so far: of order 1,500-2,000 daylight pairs.
- **For a shore camera,** the 50-km circle is half land and the altimeter samples water 10-50 km offshore, the same distance class as the CDIP buoys already used as labels. Altimeters therefore add no nearshore information there; their value is as an independent check on the offshore label.
- **SWOT swath:** the same geometry with a 100-km effective swath gives about 32 overflights a year at 35 N and 28 at Oahu (about 1.6-1.9 per 21-day cycle), half in daylight. Science orbit since July 2023.
- **What SWOT's resolved-swell product can and cannot do here.** A 500-m wavelength is an 18-s wave in deep water. North Carolina swell is typically 8-14 s, so SWOT partitions will almost always be empty there; the 2-km wave-height swath is the usable part. Oahu's north shore receives 16-20 s winter swell, so SWOT partitions could label big-swell days, but the Waimea camera archive (2009-2013) predates SWOT (launched December 2022), so this only helps a new Hawaii camera or the Hawaii buoy cameras.
- **SWIM is the best spectral match to the camera's blind spot, with a timing catch.** 70-500 m is about 6.7-18 s in deep water: exactly the swell band the project found the camera cannot see, and a SWIM partition near a buoy is a swell label that does not come from a wave model. Using the same geometry with the 90-km box reach, a site falls inside a SWIM box roughly 60 times a year at 35 N (my estimate). But the orbit crosses at about 07:00 and 19:00 local solar time, so the paired camera frame is a low-sun frame in summer and a dark one for much of the winter. SWIM therefore suits the role of an independent swell label for the buoy record far better than a co-input for the image.
- **Sentinel-1 wide-swath matches per buoy:** Owda's 10,500 matches over 74 buoys and about 8 years is about 18 a year per buoy on average (two satellites until December 2021). With the ascending node at 18:00 local time (descending 06:00), Sentinel-1 has the same dawn-and-dusk problem as SWIM for paired imagery.
- The CCI version 4 wide-swath product already separates swell and wind sea at coastal resolution through 2024; it is the quickest way to count real Sentinel-1 matches at the Outer Banks, Oak Island and Oahu without processing SAR.

### Gaps
- No published per-buoy count of altimeter matches was found in three attempts (Ribal & Young full text blocked; Campos 2023 and a 2025 NOAA poster give only totals); the figures above are my geometry estimate.
- The AVISO status page lists missions but is undated and says nothing about data quality; CFOSAT's safe-hold episode (19-20 October, year not shown) should be checked before relying on recent SWIM data.
- Sentinel-2 overpass local time and how often glint geometry occurs at 34-36 N were not researched; Landsat swell spectra and HY-2 specifics likewise.
- Whether Sentinel-1 wave-mode images are acquired near the Hawaii buoys (open ocean) was not checked; near the mainland coast the mode is wide-swath.

## 2. Shore-based radar: HF radar wave products, marine X-band radar, weather-radar sea clutter. Anything at Duck, Nags Head, Oak Island or Oahu's north shore?

### Takeaway
An HF radar sits on Jennette's Pier itself (station JENN, since 2023), and one ran at Duck from 2003 to March 2023, but the public products from the North Carolina and Hawaii radars are surface currents only; I found no public wave product for either region. HF radar wave measurement was demonstrated at Duck in research campaigns in 1994 and 1999. X-band radar is the modality with real machine-learning precedent (the closest analogue to camera video), and a research X-band radar ran at Duck from October 2021 to August 2022, but its data are available on request only.

### Cited Findings
- North Carolina and Virginia HF radar stations and dates (poster by Updyke, Taylor, Muglia and Seim, OOI Pioneer workshop, September 2024): DUCK (Duck, NC) 2003 to March 2023; **JENN (Jennette's Pier) 2023 to present**; HATY (Cape Hatteras) 2004 to present; OCRA (Ocracoke) 2021 to present; CORE (Core Banks) 2013 to present; LISL (Virginia Beach) 2009 to present. The NC sites are run by UNC under SECOORA. "At present, three long range (5 MHz) SeaSonde stations (LISL, JENN and HATY) monitor surface currents" over the Pioneer array; radial data have 5.8-km range steps and 5-degree bins; hourly current maps on a 6-km grid; real-time data through the national HF radar network. The poster describes currents only. [FT] — [OOI workshop poster](https://ooifb.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/2024_HFR_SMAB_Pioneer_Array_final.pdf)
- SECOORA lists the UNC stations DUCK, HATY and CORE as 5 MHz systems. [SEC] — [SECOORA HF radar page](https://secoora.org/test-hf-radar-page)
- Hawaii: the PacIOOS/University of Hawaii radar product provides eastward and northward surface current only (2-km grid, hourly); contributing Oahu sites are Koko Head, Kakaako, Kalaeloa (Barbers Point), Kaena Point (data before 2019-11-30 excluded) and Kapolei; no wave variables; older data moved to NOAA NCEI in August 2025. [AB, metadata page] — [PacIOOS hfradar_ushi_2km](https://www.pacioos.hawaii.edu/metadata/hfradar_ushi_2km.html)
- How SeaSonde radars get waves: the weaker second-order part of the echo spectrum is fitted with a Pierson-Moskowitz model to give wave height, period and direction. [SEC] — [Frontiers Mar. Sci. 2020, 7:492](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.00492/pdf)
- Working range by radar frequency: the method breaks down when first- and second-order echoes merge, at about 7.4 m wave height for a 13 MHz SeaSonde and about 20 m for a 4-5 MHz long-range unit; shallow water lowers that ceiling, most at low frequencies. At the low end the second-order echo sits near the noise floor; a 12-13 MHz study quotes a usable range of 1 m to 8 m. Long-range units are described as the ones that give wave information when waves are high. No lower limit for 5 MHz was found. [SEC] — [Lipa & Nyden 2005](https://www.codar.com/images/about/2005Lipa_Nyden_wave.pdf); [Lipa et al. 2008, Sensors](https://www.codar.com/images/about/2008Lipa_Sensors.pdf); [Long et al. 2011](https://www.codar.com/images/about/2011Long_J.Sensors.pdf)
- A NOAA Weather Service and IOOS trial (December 2017 to March 2019, 16 months; New Jersey, Puerto Rico, northern California) compared SeaSonde wave heights with NDBC buoys: "HF radar data and buoy data show a clear correlation in magnitude", but the two are not co-located and exact heights "typically do not match". Wave heights were averaged over range cells 6-30 km from the radar. In one quarter hourly availability was about 51 % at the New Jersey sites; forecasters noted a high bias in one period and dropouts when the wind turned offshore. [FT, partial] — [IOOS-NWS white paper, 13 Feb 2020](https://rucool.marine.rutgers.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Significant-Wave-Height-White-paper_20200213.pdf)
- Earlier research radars at Duck: in the DUCK94 experiment an HF radar wave estimate compared with an NDBC buoy 25 km offshore in 30 m of water had a 7-cm bias and RMS error under about 38 cm over wave heights of roughly 0.5-6 m; the SHOWEX experiment ran HF radars at Duck and Corolla from October to December 1999. A 2023 review (Wyatt and Green) finds single-radar wave inversions less accurate than two-radar ones. [SEC] — [Graber et al., Oceanography 10(2)](https://tos.org/oceanography/assets/docs/10-2_graber2.pdf); [SHOWEX project page](https://lucy-wyatt.sites.sheffield.ac.uk/projects/showex); [Wyatt & Green 2023](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/205816/7/remotesensing-15-05536.pdf)
- A NOAA poster gives the community's target for HF radar waves as height within 0.25 m hourly, direction within 10 degrees, period within 0.1 s. This is a goal, not a measured accuracy. [SEC] — [Rutgers AMS poster 2023](https://rucool.marine.rutgers.edu/downloads/presentations/presentations-2023/AMSPoster920_v02.pdf)
- X-band radar at Duck: Helmholtz-Zentrum Hereon ran a coherent-on-receive marine X-band radar at the Field Research Facility for the DUNEX experiment from October 2021 to August 2022; products include image stacks, depth maps and directional wave spectra; processed data go to DUNEX participants on request. [SEC] — [DUNEX radar documentation](https://codebase.helmholtz.cloud/dunexradar/doc); system paper [Horstmann et al. 2021, Sensors 21:7828](https://mdpi.com/1424-8220/21/23/7828)
- USGS released radar water-level time series from three points on the Duck pier (x = 200, 300, 600 m): 5-Hz range and water level, plus hourly mean water level and wave parameters. These are downward-looking range radars, not imaging radars. [SEC] — [USGS data citation 20202](https://cmgds.er.usgs.gov/catalog/cite-view.php?cite=20202)
- Machine learning on X-band image sequences: Kwon et al. 2023 (3-D CNN, RMSE 0.358 m, R 0.970, public data) is in the project table [PD]. A 2026 TU Delft master's thesis trained a Vision Transformer on X-band imagery from three working vessels over several years, labelled by open buoys and ERA5: the best model beats a 0.25-m RMSE target on its development vessel for wave heights up to 3 m; low wind is the consistent failure mode; moving a model to another vessel fails the target. Full text embargoed to 2027-07-05. [AB] — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:29d1170b-30b0-4e46-b86e-0ac5c259f891)
- A 2026 preprint estimates wave height from sparse high-power points of the sea-clutter spectrum with a transformer (0.5-6.3 m, East China Sea). [SEC] — [arXiv:2601.04581](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04581)
- The classical X-band method (signal-to-noise ratio of the image spectrum) correlates poorly with buoy wave height when the wind is not onshore (2022 *IEEE TGRS* study at a coastal radar). Carrasco et al. (2017, *Ocean Science*) get wave height from the Doppler velocity variance of a coherent radar instead, over about 100 days against a buoy. [SEC] — [ProQuest record](https://www.proquest.com/docview/1750365093); [Carrasco et al. 2017](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/13/95/2017/os-13-95-2017.pdf)
- Weather radar: the one study found uses an X-band weather radar in southern France to examine the sea-surface signature for coastal wind retrieval, not wave height (Forget, Saillard and Guérin 2016, *J. Atmos. Oceanic Technol.*). Two searches found no wave-height retrieval from S-band or C-band weather radars such as NEXRAD. [SEC] — [Forget et al. 2016](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Forget_etal_JTECH2016.pdf)
- Commercial WaMoS II accuracy of about 0.30 m is in the project review. [PD]

### Inferences
- JENN shares a pier with a WebCOOS camera, but as a 5 MHz long-range current radar it is a poor wave sensor for ordinary conditions: the wave signal lives in the second-order echo, which at low radar frequencies rises above noise mainly in high seas, and its first usable cell is kilometres offshore (5.8-km range steps). Even if wave files exist they would describe roughly the same offshore water as the CDIP buoy, not the surf zone.
- The Hawaii radars named are on the south and west shores (Koko Head, Kakaako, Kalaeloa, Kapolei) and the north-west tip (Kaena Point); none looks at Waimea.
- X-band radar's failure when the wind is light or offshore is the same physics as the camera's swell blindness: both need short wind roughness to see the long waves. A radar is therefore not a way around the project's central finding; it is a second instrument with the same weakness and an all-weather, night-capable advantage.
- The Hereon radar period (October 2021 to August 2022) overlaps the Duck fibre-optic and drifting-buoy experiments in sections 4 and 6 but precedes most of the WebCOOS record and needs a data request.

### Gaps
- Whether UNC/ECU keep SeaSonde wave files for JENN, DUCK or HATY was not found in two searches; only asking the operators would settle it (not done, as instructed).
- No wave retrieval from operational weather radars was found; I cannot say none exists.
- The Field Research Facility's own radar holdings on its public data server were not checked; the USGS catalogue page refused access so the pier-radar dates are unverified.
- No WERA wave product for US waters was found.

## 3. GNSS reflectometry (CYGNSS, Spire, coastal stations), GNSS buoys, scatterometer and SAR winds, mean-square-slope products

### Takeaway
CYGNSS gives surface slope variance (mean square slope) and wind at 25-km resolution several times a day in the tropics and subtropics, and deep networks turn it into wave height at about 0.43 m RMSE against NDBC buoys: no better than the project's camera (0.44-0.45 m) and mostly a wind-sea measurement, so it duplicates the camera more than it complements it, and the mission is reported to end in December 2026. A single geodetic GNSS antenna on a pier can measure wave height to about 15 cm and water level to about 9 cm, which is the cheap-sensor result worth knowing.

### Cited Findings
- CYGNSS: eight micro-satellites at about 35 degrees inclination. The Level-2 science record version 3.2 gives wind speed and mean square slope at 25 x 25 km; version 3.2 adds a swell correction for low winds; the catalogue record was modified 2026-09-08; one record lists coverage from 2018-08-01 to 2026-03-30. Stated mean revisit differs between records (7 hours in one, 3 hours in another). [SEC, catalogue entries] — [NASA catalogue, CYGNSS L2 v3.2](https://data.nasa.gov/dataset/cygnss-level-2-science-data-record-version-3-2-9c17f)
- CYGNSS status: contact with one satellite (FM-06) was lost on 3 December 2022; a University of Michigan piece says the mission "ends this December" (2026); NASA issued a partnership call in February 2026 to continue data collection; a satellite tracker lists 7 of 8 still in orbit as of July 2026. These sources do not fully agree on what remains active. [SEC] — [WMO OSCAR entry](https://space.oscar.wmo.int/satellites/view/cygnss_8_sats); [U-M article](https://alumni.umich.edu/lifelong-learning/gateway/this-satellite-constellation-transformed-earth-science-by-creatively-tuning-in-to-gps-signals/); [partnership call report](https://www.militaryaerospace.com/sensors/article/55356733/nasa-issues-partnership-call-to-continue-cygnss-satellite-data-collection)
- SCAWaveNet (Zhang et al., arXiv:2507.00701, July 2025) retrieves wave height from four-channel CYGNSS data with an attention network: average RMSE 0.438 m against ERA5 and 0.432 m against NDBC buoys, a few percent better than earlier networks. [AB] — [arXiv:2507.00701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00701)
- A Vision Transformer on the Chinese FY-3E reflectometry data reached RMSE 0.405 m against ERA5 (*Remote Sensing*, September 2024); an April 2026 *Remote Sensing* paper applies multimodal transformer fusion to reflectometry features for wave height. [SEC] — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/06f9d2d1db2d4a08a2b6738a00d72856); [Remote Sens. 18:1351](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-18-01351/article_deploy/remotesensing-18-01351.pdf)
- Spire (commercial reflectometry satellites, data bought by NASA): a University of Michigan evaluation of the Level-2 ocean product (version 0.6.1, August 2022) found wind speeds "compare well" with ERA5 matched within 50 km and 30 minutes and slope variance agrees with co-located CYGNSS; both are limited to below about 11.5 m/s and 0.033 to avoid low signal; daily coverage is lower than CYGNSS; released to scientists under the NASA commercial-data licence. No error figures are given in the summary. [FT, two-page summary] — [NASA CSDA Spire evaluation summary](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2022-11/spire_level2_ocean_products_evaluation_summary_final.pdf)
- Scatterometer winds near the coast: the operational ASCAT coastal product is on a 12.5-km grid and comes to within about 20 km of land (the standard product drops cells within 35 km); a land-corrected version from KNMI (2024) reaches the coastline and yields three times more winds within 20 km of the coast; effective resolution is about 28 km for the coastal product and 17 km for the 6.25-km product. [SEC] — [KNMI coastal product paper](https://cdn.knmi.nl/system/data_center_publications/files/000/067/805/original/paper_coastalproduct_3032_ascat_ao2006.pdf?1495620907); [Remote Sens. 16:2053](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/16/12/2053)
- SAR winds near US buoys: in Owda et al. (2024) Sentinel-1 wind speed had RMSE about 2 m/s and bias 0.78 m/s against buoys. [FT, abstract section] — [Owda et al. 2024](https://elib.dlr.de/204599/2/Evaluation_of_SAR-Based_Sea_State_Parameters_and_Roughness_Length_Derivation_Over_the_Coastal_Seas_of_the_USA.pdf)
- Coastal GNSS interferometric reflectometry: Sepulveda, Cao, Haase and Murphy (2023, *Earth and Space Science* 10:e2022EA002767) ran a standard geodetic antenna on Scripps Pier for one year. Water level: 9.4 cm RMS against the tide gauge after correction (18.2 cm before); astronomical tide 5.9 cm. Wave height: **15 cm RMS** against a wave gauge after a short calibration, found from the elevation angle at which reflections lose coherence. Errors "increase abruptly beyond a fixed limit" in high waves. Using GPS, Galileo and GLONASS together doubles the retrieval rate. Open access. [FT] — [Sepulveda et al. 2023](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10429221)
- Earlier pier work measured wave heights below 0.7 m with a 5.7-cm error standard deviation (Alonso-Arroyo et al. 2014, as cited by Sepulveda et al.). [FT of citing paper] — [Sepulveda et al. 2023](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10429221)
- A 2024 *IEEE GRSL* paper retrieves coastal wave height from multi-constellation signal-to-noise data; no error figure in the abstract. A 2025 Stuttgart thesis shows the method follows mooring wave height and can fill mooring gaps. [SEC] — [AMiner record](https://cn.aminer.org/pub/669f0e4d01d2a3fbfc0b5dca/coastal-significant-wave-height-retrieval-using-ground-based-gnss-interferometric-reflectometry); [Stuttgart thesis](https://elib.uni-stuttgart.de/items/2ccb773e-a52d-4420-b129-e489f88006a4)
- Low-cost GNSS/inertial buoys: Sofar Spotter bulk parameters were within 10 % of a Datawell buoy (Raghukumar et al. 2019, *J. Atmos. Oceanic Technol.* 36(6)); a year-long comparison gave RMSE 0.06 m (wave height), 0.27 s (mean period), 21.9 degrees (peak direction). [AB vendor summary; SEC] — [Sofar summary of Raghukumar et al.](https://www.sofarocean.com/posts/performance-characteristics-of-spotter-a-newly-developed-real-time-wave-measurement-buoy); [Frontiers Mar. Sci. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.966855/full)

### Inferences
- With a 35-degree inclination CYGNSS reaches roughly 38-40 N (the Spire evaluation uses 40 S to 40 N as the overlap region), so it covers all the North Carolina sites, Oahu and the southern NDBC buoys, and none of the New England, Great Lakes, Pacific Northwest or Alaska buoys.
- Mean square slope is what a camera's glitter and texture respond to, so "camera predicts satellite slope variance" is a physically clean cross-sensor target, but at 25 km, with the buoy's own anemometer already on the hull and the mission ending, CYGNSS adds little the project does not have. I rank it low.
- Scatterometer winds stop 20 km or more from land in the standard products and every buoy camera already has an anemometer; they add nothing for this project.
- The pier GNSS result is more interesting for the shore cameras than any satellite: a geodetic antenna gives water level (which the camera needs to interpret the surf zone) and wave height from one cheap sensor, at the pier, continuously. It would have to be installed; I did not find an existing antenna at Jennette's Pier or the Duck pier.
- CYGNSS-to-wave-height networks reach about the same error as the project's camera at unseen buoys. Both are mostly inferring wind sea from roughness, which is a consistency check on the project's central finding.

### Gaps
- Exactly which CYGNSS satellites are still producing data in November 2026 and whether a partner will continue the record is unresolved.
- HY-2 scatterometer products were not researched.
- Whether a continuously operating GNSS reference station with a sea view exists at Duck, Jennette's Pier, Oak Island or Haleiwa was not checked.
- No product was found that reports slope variance at camera scale (metres to a kilometre).

## 4. Seismic microseisms and distributed acoustic sensing: can seismometers recover wave height, period, direction? Which open stations are near the sites?

### Takeaway
This is the strongest finding of the survey. Open, permanent broadband seismometers with data actually in the archive sit 14 km from the Oak Island camera (N4.Y60A: 1,681 of 1,764 days at least 90 % complete from January 2022 to October 2026) and 25 km from Waimea Bay (IU.KIP: 1,727 of 1,733 days at least 90 % complete across the 2009-2013 camera record). Published work recovers wave height from coastal seismic noise to 0.14-0.18 m mean error with a random forest, with period encoded directly in the signal's frequency, and a 2021 abstract reports a recurrent network doing it on the US East Coast. No one appears to have paired seismic noise with camera imagery in a learned model. Separately, a seabed fibre at Duck measured wave height to 0.2 m and period to 1 s over three months in 2021-2022.

### Cited Findings

**What seismic noise carries**
- Ardhuin, Balanche, Stutzmann and Obrebski (2012, *JGR Oceans*): ocean wave spectral level is proportional to seismic spectral level raised to an exponent of about 0.9 (summer, single wind-sea) or 0.7 (winter, coastal reflection). After classifying the noise, seismic-derived wave height correlates with local measured waves at r = 0.93 (daily averages, central California); at stations dominated by distant sources the seismic estimate matches area-averaged satellite or model wave height at r > 0.85. Events with opposing wind-sea and swell give the strongest noise and must be screened. Stations divide into those "mostly sensitive to local sea states" and those integrating a large ocean area. [AB] — [Archimer 19443](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00083/19443)
- Moschella et al. (2020, *Frontiers in Earth Science* 8:114): six coastal broadband stations in eastern Sicily, 2010-2014; features are noise amplitudes in the primary band (13-20 s), secondary band (5-10 s) and short-period secondary band (2.5-5 s); labels are two wave buoys. Random forest beat k-nearest neighbours, linear regression and a support vector machine: **mean absolute error 0.14 +/- 0.02 m** (Catania buoy) and **0.18 +/- 0.05 m** (Cetraro buoy). Split by time: trained January 2010 to August 2014, tested September to December 2014 (same stations, later months). A separate 7-station array located the short-period sources by back-azimuth with median errors of 12 and 21 degrees. [FT] — [Moschella et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feart.2020.00114/full)
- **US East Coast precedent:** Bolrão et al. (EGU General Assembly 2021, abstract EGU21-14837) compared three ways of inferring wave height from seismic data "recorded in the east coast of the United States": two classical empirical methods (Ferretti et al. 2013; Bromirski et al. 1999) and a recurrent neural network. All three work; the network "is able to predict well" the buoy wave height; using several nearby stations beats one. No stations, buoys or error figures are named in the abstract, and I found no full paper. [AB] — [EGU21-14837](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU21/EGU21-14837.html)
- Baranbooei, Bean, Rezaeifar and Donne (2025, *J. Mar. Sci. Eng.* 13:807, "Determining Offshore Ocean Significant Wave Height Using Continuous Land-Recorded Seismic Data"): a neural network on secondary-microseism amplitudes at Irish national network stations, trained on wave height from buoy K4 and a wave model at the buoy position, matches the buoy about 200 km offshore on unseen data. Their 2025 conference abstract says accuracy is excellent for small waves and lower for large ones. No error figure retrieved (publisher refused access twice). [SEC; AB for the conference abstract] — [JMSE 13:807](https://mdpi.com/2077-1312/13/4/807); [EGU25-5217](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU25/EGU25-5217.html)
- A 2020 *Remote Sensing* paper (Cannata et al.) reports a random forest giving "very promising results" for reconstructing wave height in space and time from microseisms. [SEC] — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/1908b34527184ce895261e138764aed9)
- **Direction from one station:** polarisation analysis of a single three-component seismometer gives the direction of arrival of the Rayleigh-wave noise. Seven years of global-network data showed the directions "are robust measurements that point toward the source areas computed from ocean wave models" (Schimmel et al. 2011, *G-cubed*); in Japan, 4-8 s noise points along the track of passing storms while 1-2 s noise points to the nearest sea (Takagi and Obara 2014). Arrays are more accurate than single stations. None of these validates against buoy swell direction. [SEC] — [Schimmel et al. 2011 (Archimer)](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00041/15219); [Takagi & Obara, AGU 2014](https://agu.confex.com/agu/fm14/webprogram/Paper27283.html); [Appl. Sci. 13:918](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/13/2/918)

**Surf-zone seismic work on the Outer Banks**
- Poppeliers (East Carolina University, AGU Fall Meeting 2015): one three-component broadband seismometer about 50 m from the shoreline, recorded for about 10 days. Breaking waves put energy above 5 Hz; in higher seas the spectrum shifts to lower frequency and higher amplitude; "seismic spectral power correlates with ocean wave height and period". [AB] — [AGU FM15 abstract](https://studio.m-anage.com/agu/fm15/webprogram/Paper70395.html)
- The follow-up experiment is archived as FDSN network XZ (2015), "Measuring ambient noise of ocean breakers (Waves-2)", East Carolina University, doi 10.7914/SN/XZ_2015. [AB] — [FDSN network page](https://www.fdsn.org/networks/detail/XZ_2015/)
- Station metadata for XZ 2015: fifteen stations in a line at 36.180 N, 75.750 W, **on the beach at the Duck Field Research Facility**: WV01-WV08 (2015-09-29 to 2015-10-08) and FR01-FR07 (2015-10-08 to 2015-10-16); short-period three-component sensors recorded at 200 samples a second. Archive completeness for WV01: 100 % on 1-7 October 2015. [API] — [EarthScope station query, network XZ](https://service.earthscope.org/fdsnws/station/1/query?network=XZ&starttime=2015-09-01&endtime=2015-11-01&level=station&format=text)

**Open seismic stations nearest each site, and whether the data are actually archived**
Station positions and dates are from the EarthScope station service; completeness is the archive's own daily "percent availability" quality metric. All [API].
- **Oak Island:** N4.Y60A, Bolivia NC (34.0046 N, 78.2163 W), **14 km** from Oak Island and 42 km from Masonboro Inlet. Operating as TA.Y60A from 2013-02-27 and as N4.Y60A from 2015-01-30 with no end date. Broadband at 40 samples/s to March 2019, then 100 samples/s plus a 1 sample/s long-period stream. **Completeness: 97.7 % mean from 2022-01-01 to 2026-10-31, with 1,681 of 1,764 days at least 90 % complete**; 99.8 % mean for 2015-2019. — [station query](https://service.earthscope.org/fdsnws/station/1/query?network=N4&station=Y60A&level=channel&format=text); [availability metric](https://service.earthscope.org/mustang/measurements/1/query?metric=percent_availability&network=N4&station=Y60A&channel=HHZ&location=00&timewindow=2022-01-01,2026-10-31&format=text)

Completeness 99.7 % mean from 2022 to October 2026 (1,754 of 1,764 days at least 90 %). — [availability metric](https://service.earthscope.org/mustang/measurements/1/query?metric=percent_availability&network=N4&station=V61A&channel=HHZ&location=00&timewindow=2022-01-01,2026-10-31&format=text)

(Recent years are worse: 60.4 % mean for 2024 to October 2026.) — [availability metric](https://service.earthscope.org/mustang/measurements/1/query?metric=percent_availability&network=IU&station=KIP&channel=BHZ&location=00&timewindow=2009-02-01,2013-10-31&format=text)
- **Waimea Bay, the closer station that is mostly not in the archive:** PT.OPA, Opana, Oahu (21.6872 N, 158.0097 W), **8 km** from Waimea Bay, Pacific Tsunami Warning Center network, STS-2 broadband listed from 2007-07-13. Nearest unit to the Outer Banks is about 90 km away (Virginia). **Distributed acoustic sensing (fibre)**
- Glover, Wengrove and Holman (2024, *Coastal Engineering* 190:104487): a 1.5-km armoured fibre laid cross-shore at the Duck Field Research Facility from the dune toe to about 15 m depth, recorded **3 November 2021 to 10 February 2022** (552 channels, 3.2-m spacing, 4.8-m gauge length). An empirical transfer function from strain to seabed pressure over 0.04-0.4 Hz gave wave height and peak period "consistent with pressure sensors (typical rmse = 0.2 m and 1 s)" over 0.24-4 m and 3-18 s. The fibre data were also run through the cBathy depth algorithm (normally fed by the Argus cameras, which collect every half hour) and used for wave reflection. Stated advantage: works "in any weather or light". [FT, accepted manuscript] — [Glover et al. 2024](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10588194)
- Smith et al. (2023, *GRL*): a seafloor telecom cable on the Alaskan Arctic shelf calibrated to wave spectra; a later workshop deck shows RMSE 0.10 m for wave height and 0.52 s for period after empirical calibration, and warns that the strain-to-wave correction factor varies "over 10 orders of magnitude between sites" for reasons depth and waves do not explain (cable type, burial, coupling). [FT of slide deck, SAND2024-03173C] — [Smith et al. workshop slides, March 2024](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/2540259)
- Meulé et al. (2024, *Earth and Space Science*): nearshore wave spectra from fibre strain follow linear wave theory, giving spectra at high spatial resolution. A KIT study derives an analytical transfer function and reports strong correlation with a co-located pressure sensor. One search found no learned model on fibre wave data. [SEC] — [KIT repository](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000176818/155895334)

### Inferences
- **Why this pairing fits the project's gap.** The secondary microseism is generated by wave-wave interaction at twice the ocean-wave frequency, so its spectral peak reads out ocean wave period directly (a 5-10 s seismic band is 10-20 s ocean swell), and its amplitude tracks wave energy over a coastal area, day and night. That is the swell height and period information the camera lacks, from a source that is not a wave model. The camera would keep doing what it does well (local wind sea); the seismometer would supply swell.
- **Sample counts are set by the camera, not the seismometer.** Seismic data are continuous, so every frame on a day with data has a matching seismic window. Waimea: 8,935 + 8,919 images (2009-02 to 2013-10) [PD] against IU.KIP on essentially every day (1,727 of 1,733), and against the nearer PT.OPA on about 40 % of days, with the CDIP 106 buoy 6.3 km away as the label [PD]. Oak Island: every WebCOOS still and 10-minute video since 2022 on about 95 % of days against N4.Y60A. The 79 buoy cameras could each be paired with the nearest coastal broadband station, though for buoys far offshore the "local" assumption weakens.
- **IU.KIP sits in the middle of Oahu,** 25 km from the north shore and closer to the south shore. At swell periods the microseism is a regional signal, so this matters less than it would for surf-zone noise above 1 Hz, but summer south swell will also register. The 686 days of PT.OPA data let the project test whether the nearer, north-shore station does better.
- **Honest expectation.** The published 0.14-0.18 m errors come from a time split at fixed stations in a fetch-limited sea; a single station also hears distant storms, so on an open coast the mapping will be noisier, and it will not transfer between stations without retraining (site geology sets the gain). The right first test is cheap: regress CDIP 106 wave height and peak period on IU.KIP band energies for 2009-2013, with a day-block split, before any fusion model.
- The Duck 2015 beach array (XZ) recorded 29 September to 16 October 2015, on the facility's own beach with its wave gauges running. That window includes the offshore passage of Hurricane Joaquin in early October 2015 (background knowledge, not re-verified), so it should contain a large-swell event. There is no public camera imagery for it (WebCOOS starts later; Argus imagery is not public [PD]), so it serves a seismic-to-wave study, not a camera pairing.
- The Raspberry Shake coordinates are published on a roughly 1-km lattice (the latitudes are exact multiples of 1/111 degree), so the "5 km" for AM.R26DE is approximate. Its infrasound channel next to a WebCOOS camera and a CDIP buoy is the only existing in-air acoustic sensor I found near any project site.
- The Duck fibre (November 2021 to February 2022) shows that a seabed fibre resolves wave height and period across the surf zone at 3-m spacing; it does not overlap the WebCOOS Currituck record in a useful way and its data location was not found. Treat it as evidence of what a fibre can do, not as usable data.

### Gaps
- The completeness figures are the archive's quality metrics, not a test download; whether any of these stations is access-restricted was not shown in the metadata I read. One small data request would settle it.
- The East Coast seismic-to-wave-height work exists only as a 2021 abstract with no numbers; no such study was found for Hawaii, and none anywhere fusing seismic with imagery. Absence in my searches is not proof.
- Single-station direction has not been validated against buoy swell direction in anything I found.
- Baranbooei 2025 and Cannata 2020 error figures remain unverified (access refused).
- Raspberry Shake data-use terms and completeness were not checked.

## 5. Acoustics: surf sound in air, infrasound, underwater ambient noise; do public cameras carry audio; audio foundation models

### Takeaway
Surf radiates infrasound at 1-5 Hz whose amplitude tracks breaking-wave height; this was shown in Hawaii in 2002-2003 against the Waimea buoy and again in California in 2025 with video used to match individual breakers, but no one has trained a model on it and none of the published work used a camera as more than a timing aid. Three attempts found no evidence that WebCOOS or other public surf cameras archive audio. Underwater noise measures wind and breaking (bubbles), not swell.

### Cited Findings
- Garcés, Hetzer, Merrifield, Willis and Aucan (2003, *GRL* 30(24), 2264, "Observations of surf infrasound in Hawai'i"): comparison of ocean-buoy data with infrasonic array data from the winter of 2002-2003 "shows a clear relationship between breaking ocean wave height and infrasonic signal levels" in the 1-5 Hz band. The permanent array is the International Monitoring System station I59US near Kailua-Kona on **Hawaii Island**, not on Oahu; the wave buoy is 6.5 km west-north-west of Waimea Bay, Oahu; a portable array about 200 m from a shore was used for comparison, and one figure contrasts signals 200 m and 7.5 km from the coast. No correlation coefficient was found. [SEC, abstract and text fragments in search results; the PDF refused access] — [Garcés et al. 2003](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Garces_etal2003.pdf)
- That infrasound station is in the open seismic archive: IM.I59H1-H4 at 19.59 N, 155.89 W, infrasound channels at 20 samples/s listed from 2010-02-10 with no end. Completeness 97.4 % mean for 2024 to October 2026 (976 of 1,014 days at least 90 %); for 2010-2013 metrics exist for only 315 days. It is **321 km** from Waimea Bay. [API] — [station query IM.I59](https://service.earthscope.org/fdsnws/station/1/query?network=IM&station=I59*&level=channel&format=text); [availability metric](https://service.earthscope.org/mustang/measurements/1/query?metric=percent_availability&network=IM&station=I59H1&channel=BDF&timewindow=2024-01-01,2026-10-31&format=text)
- Garcés et al. (2006, *GRL*, "Infrasound from large surf"): simultaneous infrasound, visual and seabed-pressure observations on Kauai (large swell) and Hawaii Island (small to medium surf) gave "a clear relationship between breaking wave height and low-frequency atmospheric sound amplitudes in the 1-20 Hz" band, and suggest real-time estimates of surf amplitude and period. [SEC] — [Garcés et al. 2006](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Garces_etal_GRL2006.pdf)
- Francoeur and Matoza (2025, *Geophysical Journal International*, doi 10.1093/gji/ggaf317, "Identification of transient seismo-acoustic signals from crashing ocean waves: template matching and location of discrete surf events"): an infrasound array and seismometers on the headland at Coal Oil Point, California, **paired with beach video** to identify which signals are breaking waves (a wave crash was used to align video and infrasound clocks). Surf infrasound arrives as repeated pulses at 1-5 Hz, typically 0.1-0.5 Pa and 1-2 Pa in strong swell; amplitude correlated with significant wave height; array processing put the source on one rock shelf. The relation between video, acoustic and seismic data was "a lot more complex than we initially imagined". The thesis behind it used 10 months of data (September 2022 to July 2023). [SEC, press release; the journal abstract was not reachable] — [UCSB release via EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1096719); [thesis](https://escholarship.org/content/qt27w286rf/qt27w286rf.pdf)
- Suzuki, Imanishi, Sasaki and Sakamoto (2014, *Coastal Engineering Proceedings* 34): an equation estimating significant wave height at a sandy beach from measured sound pressure level and wave period fits two datasets with R = 0.83 and 0.82, better when the data are grouped by period. Microphone placement and band are not given in the abstract. [AB] — [ICCE 2014 poster paper](https://icce-ojs-tamu.tdl.org/icce/article/view/7602)
- Bolin and Åbom measured airborne surf noise in third-octave bands against significant wave height at ten Baltic sites. [SEC] — [Canadian Acoustics article citing it](https://jcaa.caa-aca.ca/index.php/jcaa/article/download/2485/2234/2624)
- Underwater: wind speed is recoverable from 1-20 kHz ambient noise made by breaking waves and bubbles, but the relations are site-dependent; a simple linear model on animal-borne recorders reached about 2 m/s; at a fixed wind speed, sound is 2-3 dB higher while waves are still growing than when fully developed, so wave state modulates the wind-noise relation. A 2008 conference paper reports wave height and mean period from submerged hydrophones in shallow water. [SEC] — [Frontiers Mar. Sci. 2022, 9:983007](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.983007/full); [NOAA repository 67417](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/67417/noaa_67417_DS1.pdf); [OMAE 2008 record](https://www.sciencegate.app/document/10.1115/omae2008-57478)
- NOAA/NPS Ocean Noise Reference Station network: 13 moored hydrophones. Relevant positions: **NRS04 Hawaiian Islands (22.33 N, 157.67 W, about 4,900 m water)**; NRS07 Southeastern US (29.33 N, 77.99 W, 870 m); NRS08 Northeastern US (39.01 N, 67.27 W); NRS06 Gulf (28.25 N, 86.83 W); NRS09 Stellwagen Bank (79 m). The team works in the 10-2,000 Hz band. Raw audio is at the NOAA NCEI passive acoustic archive ("more than 63 cumulative years"). [AB, project page] — [PMEL NRS page](http://www.pmel.noaa.gov/acoustics/noaanps-ocean-noise-reference-station-network/)
- A catalogue entry for the raw audio lists access as "non-public", which conflicts with the open-archive description. [SEC] — [data.gov NRS entry](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/noaa-ocean-noise-reference-station-network-nrs-raw-passive-acoustic-data)
- The ocean observatory mooring off Nags Head (section 7) lists no hydrophone; its only acoustic instrument is a bio-acoustic sonar. [AB] — [OOI Central Surface Mooring page](https://oceanobservatories.org/?p=34272)
- Audio foundation models at sea so far target biology and ships: Perch 2.0 was tested on marine tasks by few-shot transfer, and a 2026 preprint compares pretrained audio models for ship-noise classification. No paper was found applying such embeddings to wind or sea-state regression, and a search for audio-visual or cross-sensor distillation models for waves returned only unrelated work (fish feeding, vessel detection, a patent generating wave sounds from beach images). [SEC] — [arXiv:2512.03219](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03219v1); [arXiv:2601.08358](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08358)
- WebCOOS ingests live streams (RTSP/RTMP, H.264 or H.265), and archives timestamped 10-minute video files and one-minute stills; "by default and by preference, all data in the WebCOOS data system is available publicly". The material found says nothing about audio; its documentation pages returned errors on direct fetch. A search of surf-camera operators (HDOnTap, Surfchex with 50 cameras including North Carolina, Surfline) found no statement that any of them carries or archives sound. [SEC] — [SECOORA 2026 workshop note](https://secoora.org/secoora-co-hosts-the-2026-webcoos-workshop-in-person-in-la-jolla-ca/); [Surfchex listing](https://surfchex.com/llms.txt)
- An existing in-air infrasound sensor near a project camera: Raspberry Shake station AM.R26DE (infrasound and geophone channels, since February 2024) about 5 km from Masonboro Inlet, and AM.R6D00 (infrasound, since January 2025) about 13 km. [API] — [Raspberry Shake station query](https://data.raspberryshake.org/fdsnws/station/1/query?network=AM&station=R26DE&level=channel&format=text)

### Inferences
- Most surf acoustic energy is below 20 Hz, where consumer camera microphones roll off; audible surf sound (hundreds of hertz) exists and was regressed on wave height once (R about 0.83) but is far more exposed to wind noise and people. A "microphone on a surf cam" paper is therefore possible but should expect wind contamination to be the main problem, and the camera itself can flag wind.
- Infrasound measures **breaking** height at the shore, which is the nearshore-transformed quantity the offshore buoys do not give. That makes it a candidate nearshore label, complementary to the seismic swell channel.
- The 2003 Hawaii result is weaker support for an Oahu experiment than its title suggests: the permanent array is on another island 321 km from Waimea, and the Oahu buoy was used as the swell reference. The open I59 record could support an infrasound-to-wave study against Hawaii Island buoys, but there is no project camera there.
- NRS04 is about 87 km from Waimea Bay in 4,900 m of water: a regional wind-noise record, not a surf sensor. NRS07 is about 500 km south of the North Carolina sites. Neither pairs with a project camera.
- The quickest test of "does WebCOOS have audio" is to inspect the streams of the sample video files the project already requested; no web search answered it.

### Gaps
- Garcés 2003/2006 and Francoeur 2025 were not read in full (access refused or not located); correlation coefficients are unverified.
- Whether any public surf camera archives an audio track remains unknown after three attempts.
- No learned regression of wave height from in-air audio or infrasound was found.
- Raspberry Shake infrasound data quality and terms were not checked.

## 6. Inertial and platform motion: ships as wave buoys, smartphones, low-cost buoys, drifters, AIS; which open datasets pair motion with imagery?

### Takeaway
Estimating sea state from a vessel's own motion is a mature research line with a 2026 review, but its data are private and non-standard, and the two studies that fused motion with an imaging sensor disagree: a simulation found a large gain, a real three-vessel thesis found none. Open maritime datasets do pair onboard cameras with inertial sensors, but they were built for obstacle detection in sheltered water and carry no wave measurements; the project's own buoy-camera horizon attitude is, as far as I can tell, the only open-water one with instrument wave labels.

### Cited Findings
- Reviews: Nielsen (2017, *Ocean Engineering*) on the wave-buoy analogy; a 2026 review of machine-learning sea-state estimation from ship motions since 2018 concludes the field has moved from feasibility studies to pipelines using simulated, experimental and in-service data; a systematic review of 77 papers finds nearly 70 % of studies are European and datasets are non-standardised, and calls for open data. [SEC] — [DTU record of Nielsen 2017](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/a-concise-account-of-techniques-available-for-shipboard-sea-state/); [Univ. Rijeka project page](https://airi.uniri.hr/research/machine-learning-for-ship-motion-based-sea-state-estimation/); [TRID 2733030](https://trid.trb.org/View/2733030)
- Nielsen, Iwase and Mounet (2024, *Applied Ocean Research*) compared three learning frameworks and conclude that coverage of the training data decides the result. An NTNU/SINTEF ensemble on research-vessel motions off Norway shows "promising" height and peak period. A spectrum-regularised deep model cut wave-height MAE by 15.53 % against a data-driven baseline, on private simulated data. Using several ships at once improves the estimated spectrum. [SEC] — [NTNU Open](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2827857); [TRID 2782381](https://trid.trb.org/View/2782381)
- **Motion fused with radar images, simulation:** Liu, Wei and Lu (2025, *Frontiers in Remote Sensing*) combine heave and pitch statistics with the X-band radar signal-to-noise feature in a least-squares linear model (not deep learning), on fully simulated data (210 cases). Head seas: radar alone correlation 0.83 and RMSE 1.08 m; fused 0.99 and 0.22 m. The abstract's 0.14 m is the best single wave direction; the range over directions is 0.14-0.26 m. The authors say real-data validation is still needed. [FT] — [Liu et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/remote-sensing/articles/10.3389/frsen.2025.1693042/full)
- **Motion fused with radar images, real vessels:** the 2026 TU Delft thesis (section 2) found that adding six-degree-of-freedom vessel motion to a radar Vision Transformer "does not consistently improve the estimate" and tends to add a positive offset in low seas. [AB] — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:29d1170b-30b0-4e46-b86e-0ac5c259f891)
- **Motion fused with camera images:** Yang et al. 2026 (simulation: image only 0.098 m, with attitude 0.073 m); on the project's real buoys the horizon attitude made wave height worse by 0.038 m. [PD]
- Open datasets with onboard camera plus inertial data, none with wave labels: MaSTr1325 (unmanned surface vessel; images time-synchronised with onboard GPS and inertial unit; built for obstacle segmentation); the Pohang Canal dataset (stereo camera, inertial unit, GPS, lidar and radar in a canal and harbour); a river dataset with stereo polarisation cameras, inertial and GNSS data. A search found no open dataset combining onboard video, inertial data and measured sea state. [SEC] — [MaSTr1325 page](https://box.vicos.si/borja/viamaro/index.html); [Pohang Canal dataset paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.05555); [polarisation-camera rig record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/45Zae3kl)
- Drifting-buoy arrays at Duck: Rainville, Thomson, Moulton and Derakhti (2023, *Earth System Science Data* 15:5135) released the DUNEX microSWIFT dataset: 3-30 October 2021 at the Field Research Facility, 67 missions with 2-50 small GPS-plus-inertial buoys released together through the surf zone; positions, velocities, accelerations, sea-surface elevation, spectra, wave height, and 116,307 individual wave realisations; 1.92 GB on Dryad. [AB, dataset page] — [Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.hx3ffbgk0](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.hx3ffbgk0)
- Public camera imagery from the same experiment, elsewhere on the Outer Banks: USGS ran two cameras at Pea Island National Wildlife Refuge from 18 September to 24 October 2021 (one camera at 2 Hz for 17 minutes each daylight hour; run-up timestacks, snapshots, time-averaged images), released as public domain (doi 10.5066/P14EWDST). No co-located wave instrument is named on the page. [AB] — [USGS CoastCam DUNEX release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/usgs-coastcam-dunex-timestack-imagery-and-coordinate-data)
- A drifter that carries a camera: the SWIFT v4 drifter (University of Washington) lists an inertial unit, a Doppler profiler and a camera with strobe; it measures "directional wave spectra, salinity, water temperature, air temperature, and surface images", with over 20,000 hours of deployments since 2009. Onboard video has been used to confirm breaking. A search found no public collection of the onboard images. [FT, spec sheet; SEC for the image availability] — [SWIFT v4 description](https://apl.uw.edu/project/projects/swift/pdfs/SWIFTv4description.pdf)
- Smartphones: a phone fleet synchronised to 60 microseconds was field-tested as distributed wave sensors (Zhang et al., arXiv:2501.04886; one test on wave motion in ice in Quebec, February 2024, where phone accelerations agreed with a wave buoy). A study of inertial units concludes sensor noise is 3-4 orders of magnitude below the wave signal and that the processing algorithm matters more than the sensor model. [AB; SEC] — [arXiv:2501.04886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04886); [Gyroscopy and Navigation paper](https://journal-vniispk.ru/1573-160X/article/view/284255)
- Spotter accuracy is in section 3; the open Spotter archive (2019 to March 2022) is in the project review. [PD]
- AIS: one search found no study that infers wave height from AIS speed or course changes; the closest are speed-power models that take wave height as an input and AIS speed studies in sea ice. [SEC] — [arXiv:2212.13061](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.13061v1)

### Inferences
- Two independent real-data results (the project's buoys, the Delft vessels) now say that adding platform motion to an imaging model does not help once the imager is good, against two simulation results that say it does. That disagreement is itself a reportable finding and argues against spending more effort on motion as a co-input for the buoy cameras.
- The published claim that "cross-vessel transfer is the main unsolved problem" for radar mirrors the project's unseen-buoy protocol; the project's strict split is ahead of that literature.
- The microSWIFT release is the densest open wave-motion data at any project site, but it is October 2021, before the WebCOOS record, and the facility's own cameras are not public. The Pea Island cameras ran in the same month but about 50 km to the south, so the two do not pair directly.
- A cheap phone or inertial puck in the camera's field of view remains the simplest way to get a surf-zone label where no instrument exists; the literature supports the sensor being adequate.

### Gaps
- OpenMetBuoy, the Scripps directional wave drifters, surfboard inertial studies and quantified in-service accuracy for ship-motion methods were not retrieved.
- Whether SWIFT onboard images can be obtained on request was not asked (no contact, as instructed).
- Which instruments USGS ran beside its Pea Island cameras was not established.

## 7. Water level and pressure: NOAA tide gauges, radar gauges with wave statistics, nearshore pressure arrays, lidar

### Takeaway
NOAA's tide gauge on the Duck pier publishes, every six minutes, the standard deviation of 181 one-second water levels; a NOAA report shows that this single number tracks wave height at the pier to 0.14 m RMS with a microwave sensor. That is a free, continuous, nearshore wave-height proxy inside the camera region and much closer to the surf than the offshore buoys. Off Nags Head, an ocean-observatory mooring in 30 m of water has measured wave spectra and seabed pressure since 2024, 42 km from Jennette's Pier. Oahu's north shore has no NOAA gauge.

### Cited Findings
- Nearest NOAA water-level stations to each site, with the sensors NOAA lists [API]: **Duck 8651370** (0 km from the Duck coordinate; microwave water-level sensor plus a tsunami-rate sensor, wind, pressure); **Oregon Inlet Marina 8652587** (13 km from Jennette's Pier; microwave); **Wrightsville Beach 8658163** (4 km from Masonboro Inlet; acoustic sensor); Wilmington 8658120 (38 km from Oak Island, on the river); Springmaid Pier SC 8661070 (79 km from Oak Island; microwave). For Waimea Bay the nearest are Pearl Harbor (32 km), Mokuoloe (37 km) and Honolulu (43 km), all on other shores. — [CO-OPS station metadata API](https://api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/mdapi/prod/webapi/stations.json?type=waterlevels)
- The public six-minute water-level product returns the level and its standard deviation ("s"): at Duck on 2026-11-01 00:00 UTC, level 0.439 m and s 0.112 m; at Wrightsville Beach at the same time s 0.024 m. A one-minute product is also served at Duck. [API, sample records] — [CO-OPS data API, Duck](https://api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/api/prod/datagetter?begin_date=20261101%2000:00&end_date=20261101%2000:30&station=8651370&product=water_level&datum=MLLW&units=metric&time_zone=gmt&format=json)
- Finer than six minutes: NOAA serves preliminary one-minute water levels online, and NCEI archives one-minute and 10- or 15-second tsunami-rate data from the same gauges. No public one-second product was found. [SEC] — [NCEI tide gauge data](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/natural-hazards/tsunamis-earthquakes-volcanoes/tsunamis/tide-gauge-data); [CO-OPS web services](https://tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/web_services_info.html)
- NOAA Technical Report NOS CO-OPS 075 (Park, Heitsenrether and Sweet, June 2014): the six-minute product's standard deviation is computed from 181 samples at 1 Hz. At Duck over 24 days in April 2012, a one-coefficient model (wave height = alpha x standard deviation) against the hourly wave height from a seabed Nortek gauge gave alpha = 6.53 with RMS residual **0.14 m** for the microwave sensor and alpha = 11.08 with **0.21 m** for the acoustic sensor (the ideal is 4). The relation fails to reach significance when waves are low. At Duck a standard deviation of 7-20 cm corresponds to 1 m of wave height. The acoustic sensor's protective well filters wave motion; the microwave sensor "captures water level variability with higher fidelity when waves are present". [FT] — [NOAA Tech. Rep. NOS CO-OPS 075](https://www.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/publications/NOAA_Tech_075_Microwave_Water_Level_2014_Final.pdf)
- Fiorentino, Heitsenrether and Krug (2019, *Frontiers in Marine Science* 6:586) computed wave statistics from 1-Hz radar gauges on the Duck pier (about 0.5 km offshore, 6-7 m depth) against a seabed gauge at 11.4 m depth, 0.8 km away. Wave height RMSE by sensor: 8.1, 15.5 and 16.4 cm in 2017; 14.0 and 19.2 cm in 2018; correlations 0.93-0.98. Mean period RMSE 0.63-1.75 s. The pulse radar NOAA uses operationally could not resolve wind-wave energy at 0.2 Hz and above and showed spurious low-frequency energy. "NOAA CO-OPS does not maintain any of its own operational wave measurement systems." [FT] — [Fiorentino et al. 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2019.00586/full)
- **Ocean Observatories Initiative, Pioneer array off Nags Head** (relocated there in 2024; installation completed 17 April 2024 [SEC]): the Central Surface Mooring (CP10CNSM) is at 35.95 N, 75.125 W in about 30 m of water, **42 km** from Jennette's Pier and 62 km from Duck (my distances). Its buoy carries a surface wave spectra sensor, a motion pack and bulk meteorology; its seabed node carries a seafloor pressure sensor and a current profiler. The Northern and Southern surface moorings (about 100 m depth) also carry wave sensors; a catalogue entry lists Northern-mooring wave spectra from May 2025 to April 2026. Data are open through the OOI data explorer. [AB for the instrument list; SEC for dates] — [OOI Central Surface Mooring page](https://oceanobservatories.org/?p=34272); [data.gov entry, Northern mooring wave spectra](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/ooi-coastal-pioneer-mab-northern-surface-mooring-surface-buoy-surface-wave-spectra)
- At Jennette's Pier itself: a NOAA-partnered ocean observing buoy was placed "just off the pier" in late 2024 at the pier's wave-energy test site (about 11 m depth, 35 deg 54.6' N, 75 deg 35.5' W). It reports wind, humidity, air and sea temperature, salinity and currents through NOAA stations 8652226 and qr0601; **waves are not among the listed measurements**. A Sofar Spotter (SPOT-0560) was deployed at the pier around October 2020 for a wave-energy competition; how long it ran is not stated. [AB] — [Coastal Studies Institute article, 7 Jan 2025](https://coastalstudiesinstitute.org/new-buoy-at-jennettes-pier-provides-info-for-all); [UNC Charlotte post, Oct 2020](https://coefs.charlotte.edu/wbwillia/2020/10/realtime-wave-conditions-at-jennettes-pier/)
- USGS radar water-level series at three positions on the Duck pier (5 Hz) are in section 2. The Duck seabed gauges record raw pressure at 2 Hz for 107 minutes every other hour (as described in Glover et al. 2024). The facility's lidar wave-gauge array and pressure arrays are in the project review. [FT for the gauge sampling; PD] — [Glover et al. 2024](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10588194)
- A geodetic GNSS antenna on a pier gives water level to 9.4 cm and wave height to 15 cm (section 3). [FT] — [Sepulveda et al. 2023](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10429221)

### Inferences
- The Duck gauge's six-minute standard deviation is a wave-height proxy at the end of the pier, in 6-7 m of water, 240 values a day, with a published calibration against the facility's wave gauge. For shore cameras in the Duck area it is a label measured after most of the shoaling and refraction that separates the camera from the offshore buoy (the project's stated worry about offshore labels), and it can be re-calibrated at any time against the facility's seabed gauges, which are public.
- It is a wave-height-only proxy: 181 seconds of 1-Hz data smoothed by the sensor footprint carries no usable period or direction, and the operational pulse radar under-reads wind sea above 0.2 Hz. It will read swell better than chop, which is the opposite bias to the camera's and therefore useful.
- Wrightsville Beach has an acoustic sensor in a well; its sample standard deviation (2-3 cm) is far smaller than Duck's and the report shows acoustic wells mute the wave signal, so expect a weaker proxy there. Oregon Inlet Marina is inside the inlet and sheltered. So the usable nearshore proxy among the project sites is Duck alone.
- The same statistic exists at every ocean-pier NOAA gauge in the country, so a method built at Duck generalises to other pier cameras.
- The OOI central mooring gives Jennette's Pier a second directional-wave reference with seabed pressure and meteorology on the same platform from 2024, farther out than the CDIP buoy; useful as an incident-wave check, not as a nearshore label. The Jennette's Pier Spotter, if its 2020-2022 record can be found, would be the only wave instrument within the camera's own view.

### Gaps
- Whether NOAA will release raw 1-Hz gauge series (which would allow spectra and infragravity energy) was not established; nothing public was found.
- The archive and dates of the Jennette's Pier Spotter record were not found.
- PacIOOS nearshore sensors on Oahu's north shore were not checked.
- The start date of the microwave sensor's operational record at Duck was not found; the report shows it running in 2012.

## 8. Human and text observations: ship observers, forecast text, lifeguard and surf reports, crowd-sourced photos; has language been used as a weak label for imagery?

### Takeaway
A daily human surf observation for Oahu's north shore exists for 1968-2020, made at Sunset Point and at Waimea Bay itself on big days, with a stated uncertainty of 10-15 % and a public-domain licence; it overlaps the entire 2009-2013 Waimea camera archive. Weather Service surf forecasts for the exact North Carolina beaches and for Oahu are archived as text back to at least 2009. I found no published work that uses surf reports or forecast text as labels for wave imagery.

### Cited Findings
- Goddard-Caldwell database (NOAA NCEI): daily visual surf observations for Oahu, 1968-08-01 to 2020-12-10; Larry Goddard to September 1987, Patrick Caldwell from September 1987. The value is the daily maximum, taken as the upper end of the reported height range, "roughly equivalent to H1/10", on the Hawaii scale, which under-states breaker face height by as much as one half. North-shore values are mostly from Sunset Point; on extreme days (above 15 feet on that scale) they are taken at **Waimea Bay**. South shore is usually Ala Moana; west and east are lower quality. Sources are Honolulu lifeguards, the Surf News Network and experienced surfers, cross-checked. Comparison with buoy 51001 (1981-2002) gives an uncertainty of 10-15 % of the surf height. Licence CC0; one metadata field says "non-public", contradicting the licence. [AB, federal catalogue record; the NCEI page itself timed out] — [data.gov record](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/goddard-caldwell-daily-visual-surf-observation-database-daily-visual-surf-observation-2020); [NCEI landing page](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/archive/accession/Goddard-Caldwell_Database)
- Caldwell and Aucan (2007, *Journal of Coastal Research* 23(5)) give an empirical formula from offshore buoy wave height and period (a Waverider about 5 km north-west of Waimea Bay) to north-shore surf height, defined as the mean of the highest tenth of breakers at the high-refraction spots. [SEC] — [BioOne record](https://bioone.org/journals/journal-of-coastal-research/volume-23/issue-5/04-0397R.1/An-Empirical-Method-for-Estimating-Surf-Heights-from-Deepwater-Significant/10.2112/04-0397R.1.full)

- Weather Service Surf Zone Forecast text, Newport/Morehead City office (product SRFMHX): zone "Northern Outer Banks, including the beaches of Duck, Southern Shores, Kitty Hawk, Kill Devil Hills, and Nags Head". Seasonal: on 31 October 2026 it was "discontinued until April, 2027". A summer example (15 July 2026): "Rip Current Risk: Moderate. Surf Height: Around 2 feet", with wind, weather and Duck Pier tides. [API] — [IEM text archive, SRFILM](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/afos/retrieve.py?pil=SRFILM&limit=1&fmt=text)

- Ship observers: Gulev et al. (2003, *JGR*) analysed visual wave reports from voluntary observing ships for 1958-1997; random observational uncertainty is within 20 % of the mean for most locations; small seas are overestimated and periods underestimated; other work finds high waves underestimated (a fair-weather bias) and large scatter around correct averages. [SEC] — [Gulev et al. 2003](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Gulev_etal_JGR2003.pdf); [Grigorieva et al. 2022](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Grigorieva_etal_ESS2022.pdf)
- Crowd-sourced photos: an Army Corps evaluation of the CoastSnap citizen-photo programme found its shorelines match survey shorelines to under 10 m RMSE in low to moderate waves and up to 18 m in high waves (better when run-up is included); the photos give shoreline and qualitative storm information, not wave height. [SEC] — [ERDC technical report](https://erdc-library.erdc.dren.mil/jspui/handle/11681/47568)
- Language as supervision for ocean imagery: the nearest precedent is SeaCLIP, which uses CLIP as a supervisory signal for seagrass image patches; "Orca" applies a large language model to buoy wave-height series, not images. A search for vision-language models or surf reports as weak labels for wave imagery found nothing. [SEC] — [arXiv:2303.00973](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00973); [arXiv:2407.20053](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.20053v1)
- Observer-labelled imagery already in the project table: bridge-camera Beaufort classes (Vorkapic 2024, Pobar 2026). Surfline's human and camera reports are unvalidated and not open. [PD]

### Inferences
- **Waimea is the one site where image, human observation, forecast text, a buoy 6.3 km away and an archived seismometer all exist for the same years (2009-2013).** About 1,700 days of camera coverage each carry one Goddard-Caldwell observation, two or more forecast issuances, hourly buoy spectra and continuous seismic data. The human number describes breaking surf at the shore (what the beach camera actually shows), while the buoy describes offshore conditions; that difference is the nearshore transformation the project wants to learn.
- Caveats on the human label: one value a day (the daily maximum), usually for Sunset Point (about 5 km from Waimea) on a local scale that needs converting to face height, with 10-15 % uncertainty. It is a daily, weak label, not a per-image one.
- Forecast text is a forecast, written with wave-model guidance: using it as a label leaks the wave model into the target. Its legitimate uses are (i) a language-side input or contrastive target for a vision-language experiment (does an image embedding align with "small north-northwest swell fading"?), and (ii) a baseline: is a camera better than the forecaster's morning number against the buoy?
- North Carolina forecast text exists only April-October and at one-foot granularity ("around 2 feet") for a whole county's beaches. It is too coarse for regression and misses winter storms; I rank it low.
- Ship observers' 20 % uncertainty with a fair-weather bias gives a human baseline for "how well does a trained eye estimate sea state", a useful sentence for a paper, not a data source for these sites.

### Gaps
- The Goddard-Caldwell file format and the "non-public" flag need a direct look at NCEI; the page would not load in two attempts.
- No lifeguard observation dataset for North Carolina was found in one search; Surfline's human reports were not researched further and I know of no open archive.
- No precedent was found for text or reports as weak labels for wave imagery; this is an absence in two searches, not a proof.

## 9. Precedent by role: (a) co-input fused with camera imagery, (b) teacher or label source for a camera model, (c) a target the camera predicts

### Takeaway
Fusion with cameras has been tried only with inertial attitude and weather variables. The well-established pattern is one remote sensor labelled by another (altimeter labels for SAR, buoy or reanalysis labels for radar, reflectometry and seismic). No published work was found that fuses a camera with seismic, acoustic, infrasound, HF radar, reflectometry, tide-gauge or satellite wave data, or that trains a camera to predict another sensor's quantity other than wave-radar height.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Co-input fused with imagery**
- Camera + platform attitude: Yang et al. 2026, simulation, RMSE 0.098 m (image) to 0.073 m (image + attitude); the project's real-buoy test found no gain (+0.038 m worse). [PD]
- Camera + wind, temperature, humidity, pressure, tide: Xu et al. 2024, one site, random split: MAE 0.04 m and 8.5 % error with the extra inputs; image-only much weaker. [PD]
- Camera + ERA5 + buoy wind (the project): together they cut the wind-wave error of ERA5-plus-wind by 30 %. [PD]
- Camera + infrasound + seismic: Francoeur and Matoza 2025 used video to identify and time breaking waves in the infrasound record; no learned fusion. [SEC] — [UCSB release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1096719)
- Camera-derived bathymetry next to fibre-derived bathymetry: Glover et al. 2024 ran the camera algorithm (cBathy) on fibre data and compared with the camera result; a comparison, not a fusion. [FT] — [Glover et al. 2024](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10588194)
- Radar images + ship motion: simulated gain from RMSE 1.08 m to 0.22 m (Liu et al. 2025) [FT] — [Liu et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/remote-sensing/articles/10.3389/frsen.2025.1693042/full); no consistent gain on three real vessels (TU Delft 2026) [AB] — [TU Delft](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:29d1170b-30b0-4e46-b86e-0ac5c259f891)
- Radar images + wind: SWH-WindNet. [PD]
- Lidar + thermal infrared at breaking onset: Carini et al. 2021. [PD]
- Several seismic stations fused for wave height: more stations beat one on the US East Coast (Bolrão et al. 2021). [AB] — [EGU21-14837](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU21/EGU21-14837.html)
- Reflectometry features fused across channels with multimodal transformers (single sensor, several feature types): *Remote Sensing*, April 2026. [SEC] — [Remote Sens. 18:1351](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-18-01351/article_deploy/remotesensing-18-01351.pdf)

**(b) One sensor as teacher or label for another**
- Altimeter wave height labels train the Sentinel-1 wave-mode network (0.6 to 0.3 m RMSE against altimeters), operational since June 2022. [SEC] — [SAR-MPC](https://sar-mpc.eu/about/news)
- Altimeter matches label the linear probe on the self-supervised SAR model WV-Net (0.50 m against 0.60 m). [SEC] — [arXiv:2406.18765](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18765v1)
- ERA5 and NDBC buoys label CYGNSS networks (0.438 m and 0.432 m). [AB] — [arXiv:2507.00701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00701)
- Open buoys and ERA5 label a ship-radar Vision Transformer. [AB] — [TU Delft](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:29d1170b-30b0-4e46-b86e-0ac5c259f891)
- Buoys label seismic-noise models (0.14-0.18 m). [FT] — [Moschella et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feart.2020.00114/full)
- Pressure gauges calibrate fibre strain (0.2 m, 1 s). [FT] — [Glover et al. 2024](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10588194)
- A seabed wave gauge calibrates tide-gauge variance into wave height (0.14 m). [FT] — [NOAA Tech. Rep. 075](https://www.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/publications/NOAA_Tech_075_Microwave_Water_Level_2014_Final.pdf)
- SWOT is proposed by its own handbook as the calibrator for SWIM and Sentinel-1 retrievals. [FT] — [AVISO handbook](https://aviso.altimetry.fr/fileadmin/documents/data/tools/hdbk_SWOT_L3_LR_WIND_WAVE.pdf)
- For cameras specifically: shipboard wave radar labelled Choi et al.'s ship-camera model (RMSE 0.133 m); human observers labelled the bridge-camera Beaufort sets; timestack and optical-flow pseudo-labels trained Kamagata's period model. [PD]

**(c) A target the camera predicts**
- Camera to wave-radar wave height: Choi et al. 2020 ship part. [PD]
- Nothing found for camera to slope variance, camera to seismic or acoustic level, camera to tide-gauge variance, or camera to SAR/SWIM swell partition. A search for audio-visual or cross-sensor distillation models for waves returned nothing relevant. [SEC, negative result] — [nearest hit: acoustic-visual vessel detection](https://trid.trb.org/View/2754090)

### Inferences
- The untested roles are specific and nameable, which is what a novelty claim needs: (i) seismic or infrasound as a **co-input** that supplies swell to a camera model; (ii) a nearshore sensor (tide-gauge variance, pier radar, infrasound) as a **training-only teacher** so a camera learns the nearshore-transformed height that offshore buoy labels cannot give it; (iii) the camera **predicting** a second sensor's reading (for example microseism band energy) as a self-supervised pretext task on unlabelled video.
- The only fusion with real-data evidence either way is platform motion, and it is negative on real data in two independent studies. A paper proposing a new co-input should lead with a real-data ablation at unseen sites for that reason.
- The cross-sensor teacher pattern is standard practice for satellites, so a reviewer will accept it for cameras; what would be new is the choice of teacher.

### Gaps
- These absences rest on roughly twenty searches; HF-radar-plus-camera work was not searched specifically.
- Quantified gains for most fusion papers are single-site or simulated; none uses an unseen-site split.

## 10. Which three to five pairings look most promising and novel for these exact sites, and what would the co-located sample count be?

### Takeaway
Start with seismic noise as the swell channel beside the camera, tested first at Waimea Bay with the Kipapa station, because it is the only modality that is open, verified to be in the archive for the full camera periods at two project sites, physically carries the period and swell energy the camera cannot see, and has not been paired with imagery. The deciding principle: prefer modalities that are continuous (so the camera sets the sample count), independent of the wave model, and already archived for the camera's own years.

### Cited Findings
The facts each pairing rests on, by number:

1. **Camera + coastal seismometer (swell height and period).** IU.KIP is 25 km from Waimea Bay with 1,727 of 1,733 days at least 90 % complete across 2009-02 to 2013-10; PT.OPA at 8 km has 686 such days on its one archived channel; N4.Y60A is 14 km from Oak Island with 1,681 of 1,764 days from 2022-01 to 2026-10; seismic-noise models reach 0.14-0.18 m mean absolute error for wave height against buoys in a time split. [API; FT] — [IU.KIP availability](https://service.earthscope.org/mustang/measurements/1/query?metric=percent_availability&network=IU&station=KIP&channel=BHZ&location=00&timewindow=2009-02-01,2013-10-31&format=text); [N4.Y60A availability](https://service.earthscope.org/mustang/measurements/1/query?metric=percent_availability&network=N4&station=Y60A&channel=HHZ&location=00&timewindow=2022-01-01,2026-10-31&format=text); [Moschella et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feart.2020.00114/full)
2. **Camera + pier tide-gauge variance as a nearshore teacher (Duck).** The Duck gauge's six-minute standard deviation gives wave height to 0.14 m RMS with one coefficient; the series is public through the NOAA API. [FT; API] — [NOAA Tech. Rep. 075](https://www.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/publications/NOAA_Tech_075_Microwave_Water_Level_2014_Final.pdf); [CO-OPS API](https://api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/api/prod/datagetter?begin_date=20261101%2000:00&end_date=20261101%2000:30&station=8651370&product=water_level&datum=MLLW&units=metric&time_zone=gmt&format=json)
3. **Buoy cameras + satellite swell partitions as an independent label.** CFOSAT SWIM partitions (height, peak period, peak direction; 70-500 m wavelengths; 90 x 70 km boxes either side of the track; period RMSE 0.9 s and direction RMSE 20 degrees against NDBC) are served within 3 hours; the ten-mission altimeter stream gives along-track wave height at 7 km; the CCI version 4 Sentinel-1 wide-swath product separates swell and wind sea to 2024. [FT; SEC; AB] — [CMEMS PUM](https://documentation.marine.copernicus.eu/PUM/CMEMS-WAVE-PUM-014-001-003-009.pdf); [CFOSAT handbook](https://www.aviso.altimetry.fr/fileadmin/documents/data/tools/CFOSAT_L2PBOX_handbook_SALP.pdf); [SWIM partition validation](https://essopenarchive.org/doi/full/10.1002/essoar.10506895.1); [CEDA 9675ee26](https://catalogue.ceda.ac.uk/uuid/9675ee262e79485daa00a146f1300bf3/)
4. **Waimea image + human surf observation + forecast text.** Daily observations 1968-2020 with 10-15 % uncertainty and a CC0 licence, taken at Waimea Bay on the biggest days; Honolulu surf forecast text archived for the 2009, 2010 and 2012 dates checked. [AB; API] — [data.gov Goddard-Caldwell record](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/goddard-caldwell-daily-visual-surf-observation-database-daily-visual-surf-observation-2020); [IEM AFOS list](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/api/1/nws/afos/list.json?pil=SRFHFO&date=2010-01-15)
5. **Camera + infrasound of breaking surf.** Surf infrasound at 1-5 Hz tracks breaking-wave height (Hawaii 2002-2003; California 2025 with video); a citizen infrasound sensor has run about 5 km from Masonboro Inlet since February 2024. [SEC; API] — [Garcés et al. 2003](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Garces_etal2003.pdf); [UCSB release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1096719); [Raspberry Shake query](https://data.raspberryshake.org/fdsnws/station/1/query?network=AM&station=R26DE&level=channel&format=text)

### Inferences

**Ranked plan and realistic sample counts**

1. **Seismic swell channel + camera. Do this first.**
   - *Waimea Bay (2009-2013):* about 17,850 hourly daylight images from two cameras [PD], on about 1,700 camera days, essentially all of which (1,727 of 1,733 calendar days) have IU.KIP data (25 km), labelled by the CDIP 106 buoy 6.3 km away [PD]. About 40 % of those days (686) also have the nearer PT.OPA (8 km), enough to test whether proximity to the north shore matters. Effective independent samples are closer to the number of days than the number of images, because hourly frames within a swell event are correlated; split by blocks of days.
   - *Oak Island (2022-2026):* every WebCOOS still and 10-minute video on about 95 % of days (1,681 of 1,764) against N4.Y60A (14 km), labels from CDIP 200 at 24.5 km [PD]. Count is whatever the camera archive holds.
   - *Masonboro Inlet:* N4.Y60A at 42 km plus the citizen geophone at about 5 km (since 2024).
   - *Duck and Jennette's Pier:* weakest case, nearest permanent broadband 86-90 km inland (but 99.7 % complete).
   - *Experiment shape:* (i) seismic-only regression of buoy wave height and peak period (the published method; new for Hawaii, and for the US East Coast beyond one abstract); (ii) camera-only; (iii) both; (iv) both plus ERA5/WIS, to ask whether seismic replaces what the wave model supplies. Success would mean a camera-plus-seismometer system that recovers swell without a wave model. Risk: an open-coast station hears distant storms and other shores, and the mapping is station-specific.
   - *Why it is novel:* no published pairing of seismic noise with wave imagery was found; both sensors are cheap and widespread, and the seismic data are free.

2. **Tide-gauge variance as a nearshore teacher at Duck.** 240 six-minute values a day, every day, at the pier end; pairs with any shore camera within a few kilometres of the pier and with the facility's public seabed gauges for calibration. Use it as a training-only target (the camera learns "height at the pier", not "height at a buoy 22 km offshore") and test whether that improves agreement with the facility's nearshore gauges. Count: every daylight frame; for a 10-minute video cadence, one or two gauge values per clip. Limit: wave height only, one site, and the Currituck camera is north of the pier, so the camera-to-pier distance must be checked before committing.

3. **Satellite swell labels for the 79 buoy cameras.** Expect about 150 daylight altimeter pairs per buoy-year within 50 km (about 12,000 a year across the network, upper bound; roughly 1,500-2,000 exist for the first eight weeks of the archive), about 15 daylight SWOT swath overflights per buoy-year, about 60 SWIM box overflights per buoy-year (all near 07:00 or 19:00 local time), and Sentinel-1 wide-swath matches of the order of 18 per buoy-year by the published US count (06:00 or 18:00). Because the swell-resolving satellites pass at dawn and dusk, use them to check the **buoy and model** swell labels rather than as partners for the image: an instrument-based check on swell height, period and direction that is independent of both the buoy and the wave model, which matters for the claim "the camera is blind to swell". It grows slowly; it is a year-two evaluation asset, not a training set.

4. **The Waimea five-source set (image, buoy, seismometer, daily human observation, forecast text).** About 1,700 days. Two questions only this set can answer: does a camera at the beach track the human-observed breaking height better than the offshore buoy does (nearshore transformation), and does forecast language align with image embeddings (a first vision-language test for sea state). It is the natural home for pairing 1 and adds a human baseline to the paper for free.

5. **Infrasound or audio of breaking surf (exploratory).** If WebCOOS video files carry an audio track, every 10-minute clip is already an audio-visual sample and a wind-robust "breaking loudness" feature can be tested at once. If not, the only existing sensor near a project camera is the citizen infrasound unit near Masonboro Inlet (since February 2024; about 2.7 years of overlap with the camera and CDIP 150), location known only to about a kilometre. High novelty, uncertain data; check the video container before investing.

**Not recommended now:** HF radar (no public wave product at these sites; first cell kilometres offshore; long-range units see waves mainly in high seas), CYGNSS (25 km, duplicates the camera's wind-sea skill at similar error, mission ending), scatterometer winds (stop 20 km from land; buoys have anemometers), platform motion as a co-input (negative on real data twice), North Carolina forecast text (seasonal, one-foot steps, model-derived), the Duck fibre, X-band and drifter experiments of 2021-2022 (excellent sensors, but before the public camera record and partly on request only).

**What would change this ranking:** a failed test download from IU.KIP for 2009-2013 or N4.Y60A for 2022-2026 would drop pairing 1 at that site; finding that the Duck-area camera is too far from the pier would drop pairing 2; discovering an audio track in WebCOOS video would raise pairing 5 to second place. **What changed during this work:** the Waimea seismic pairing was first written around PT.OPA (8 km); the archive check showed most of its data are missing, so the plan now rests on IU.KIP (25 km), which is complete.

### Gaps
- All sample counts for WebCOOS sites depend on the project's own archive size, which I did not read; the counts for satellites are geometric estimates, not tallies.
- The seismic pairing has not been tried anywhere I could find, so there is no published effect size for the fusion; the first seismic-only regression at Waimea is the go/no-go test.
- Three direct checks this read-only pass could not make: a test download of the seismic data, the Goddard-Caldwell files and access flag at NCEI, and the presence of audio in WebCOOS video files.
- Still unresearched: Sentinel-2 glint frequency at these latitudes; HY-2 scatterometer products; SeaSonde wave files held by the North Carolina radar operators; lifeguard records in North Carolina; the Jennette's Pier Spotter archive.
