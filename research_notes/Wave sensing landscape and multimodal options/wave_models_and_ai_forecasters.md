## 1. Reanalyses, hindcasts and operational analyses: accuracy against buoys and access (partitions, 2-D spectra, latency)

### Takeaway
The cheapest large upgrade over ERA5 bulk parameters is already public and archived: NOAA GFS-Wave point output at NDBC buoy positions (hourly bulletins with up to six wave systems, plus 2-D spectra) sits in the NOAA open-data bucket for 2025, and NWPS (SWAN) publishes 2-D spectra at the Duck, Oregon Inlet, Frying Pan and other NC buoys but keeps only five days online. ERA6 will not help before late 2027; ERA5 2-D spectra remain MARS-only through `reanalysis-era5-complete`.

### Cited Findings

**ERA5 / ERA6 (ECMWF)**
- ERA5 waves run on a 0.36° reduced lat/lon grid (EDA 1.0°). The 2-D spectrum parameter is shortName `2dfd`, paramId 140251 (the "251.140" of MARS notation; the documentation does not use "d2fd"): 24 directions (15° steps from 7.5°) by 30 frequencies (0.03453 Hz, ratio 1.1), stored as log10 of spectral density, missing over land, hourly for stream `wave`. It is "available in MARS only", reachable through the CDS API dataset `reanalysis-era5-complete` (slow queue). ERA5T is `expver=0005`. [F] — [ERA5 data documentation](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/display/CKB/ERA5%3A+data+documentation)
- ERA5 partition parameters on CDS: wind sea (`shww`, `mdww`, `mpww`), total swell (`shts`, `mdts`, `mpts`), and three swell partitions (`swh1-3`, `mwd1-3`, `mwp1-3`). [F] — [ERA5 data documentation](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/display/CKB/ERA5%3A+data+documentation)
- The same page mentions altimeter wave-height parameters as the assimilated wave data and does not mention buoys; a secondary source states NDBC buoys are not assimilated into ERA5, so they are an independent check. [F]/[S] — [ERA5 data documentation](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/display/CKB/ERA5%3A+data+documentation); [MDPI Atmosphere 16:1345](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4433/16/12/1345)
- **ERA6 (changed in 2026):** production was announced 6 March 2026 on IFS Cycle 49r2 (ERA5: 41r2). Atmosphere and ocean waves run at 14 km (TCo799) against 40 km for ERA5 waves, with a coupled NEMO4-SI3 ocean at 0.25°. The first streams (2007 onward) are to be published "towards the end of 2027", the first four decades "by early 2028"; ERA6T latency is "TBC". [F] — [Copernicus, 6 Mar 2026](https://climate.copernicus.eu/copernicus-climates-era6-reanalysis-production-starts); [ECMWF Newsletter 188](https://www.ecmwf.int/node/38351)
- Conflict: third-party pages returned by search put the first ERA6 release at "end of 2026" or "September 2026"; the two ECMWF/Copernicus primary pages both say end of 2027. [S] — [EMD wiki](https://help.emd.dk/mediawiki/index.php/ERA6); [Vortex blog](https://vortexfdc.com/blog/transitioning-from-era5-to-era6/)
- A separate ECMWF research hindcast exists: "Tco1279 CY50R1 wave hindcast based on ERA5 forcing 2026 for AIFS training" (experiment `j5rf`, class `rd`, DOI 10.21957/j4p5-jf97): standalone ecWAM CY50R1 with altimeter assimilation, about 9 km, 36 directions x 36 frequencies, 6-hourly output, retrievable with the ECMWF Web API (`dataset: research`). The page shows 2026 only and does not mention spectra or a licence. [F] — [ECMWF experiment j5rf](https://apps.ecmwf.int/ifs-experiments/rd/j5rf/)

**ECMWF operational (IFS wave) open data**
- Open data (CC-BY-4.0) carries IFS wave fields `swh`, `mwd`, `mwp`, `pp1d`, `mp2`, `cdww`, bathymetry, and six period-band heights `h1012`, `h1214`, `h1417`, `h1721`, `h2125`, `h2530` (significant height of waves with periods 10-12 s ... 25-30 s). No swell/wind-sea partitions and no spectra. Resolution 0.25°, with 0.1° added for IFS from 20 October 2026. The portal keeps "the most recent 12 forecast runs"; mirrors on AWS, Azure and Google. [F] — [ECMWF open data](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/datasets/open-data)
- The AWS mirror (`ecmwf-forecasts`, eu-central-1) gives no archive start date and "may retain older data conventions/versions over time". [F] — [AWS registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/ecmwf-forecasts/)
- ECMWF's whole real-time catalogue became CC-BY-4.0 with no information cost on 1 October 2025; delivery of the full-resolution set can carry service charges, and the MARS archive is listed separately as needing a licence. [S] — [ECMWF open-data status](https://www.ecmwf.int/node/29328); [Service charges](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/display/DAC/Service+Charges%3A+From+01+July+2024)

**Copernicus Marine (MFWAM)**
- Global analysis and forecast `GLOBAL_ANALYSISFORECAST_WAV_001_027`: MFWAM, 1/12° (0.083°), listed hourly (overview text says 3-hourly instantaneous fields), total sea plus wind-wave, primary-swell and secondary-swell height/period/direction and Stokes drift; coverage 1 Nov 2022 to 29 Nov 2026 (daily analyses, 10-day forecasts, updated 00 and 12 UTC). No 2-D spectra distributed. [F] — [CMEMS product page](https://data.marine.copernicus.eu/product/GLOBAL_ANALYSISFORECAST_WAV_001_027/description)

No 2-D spectra distributed. [F] — [CMEMS WAVERYS page](https://data.marine.copernicus.eu/product/GLOBAL_MULTIYEAR_WAV_001_032/description)

### Inferences
- Product matrix for the project's label period (2025-2026) at or near NDBC buoys: **partitions and 2-D spectra at the buoy point** from GFS-Wave (archived, real time), NWPS (subset of buoys, 5-day retention), WIS (annual, held to 2025-12-31) and ERA5 (`2dfd`, slow MARS queue); **gridded partitions only** from ERA5 on CDS, CMEMS 1/12° analysis and WAVERYS; **no partitions** from ECMWF open data, AIFS or PacIOOS SWAN.
- GFS-Wave bulletins are the low-effort item: about 49 MB per cycle, so one year of four cycles is roughly 70 GB, and only the first six hourly rows of each cycle are needed for a nowcast series. The spectra tarball is about 1.7 GB per cycle (about 2.5 TB a year), so spectra should be pulled only for selected cycles.
- The bulletin's wave systems come from the same kind of spectral partitioning as NDBC-style partitions computed from a spectrum, and the `*` flag marks the wind sea; this is closer to a wave-system definition than ERA5's wind-sea/total-swell split, which the project found disagrees with NDBC's ([P] `docs/literature/benchmark_targets.md`).
- NWPS spectra at 44056 and 44100 (Duck) and 44095 (Oregon Inlet) are the only operational nearshore SWAN spectra found for the NC Outer Banks. With five days of retention and no archive found, they exist for the project only from the day collection starts.
- ECMWF open-data period-band heights (10-30 s) are a free, real-time swell-energy breakdown; the energy below 10 s follows as swh² minus the sum of band heights squared (assuming band energies add). On the US East Coast much swell is shorter than 10 s, so the split is cruder than a partition there.
- Published like-for-like accuracy by sea-state partition against NDBC was not found for any product; the project's own numbers (ERA5 wind-wave 0.341 m, total 0.267 m [P]) are the best available for that question.

## 2. AI models that output ocean waves (2023-2026)

### Takeaway
Two AI systems matter and neither replaces a spectral model for this project: Aurora 0.25° Wave is the only public model with wind-sea and swell partitions but needs ECMWF's wave analysis (with partitions) as input, and ECMWF AIFS Single v2 (operational 12 May 2026, CC BY 4.0 weights) outputs only total height, period, direction and six long-period band heights. Neither outputs 2-D spectra, and both are forecast models whose starting point is an analysis the project could use directly.

### Cited Findings
- **Aurora 0.25° Wave (Microsoft):** fine-tuned from Aurora 0.25° Pretrained on HRES-WAM analysis for 2016-2021, evaluated on 2022, at 0.25°. Wave variables: `swh`, `mwd`, `mwp`, `pp1d`, wind sea (`shww`, `mdww`, `mpww`), total swell (`shts`, `mdts`, `mpts`), first and second swell partitions (`swh1/2`, `mwd1/2`, `mwp1/2`), plus neutral winds. [F] — [Aurora models](https://microsoft.github.io/aurora/models.html); [arXiv 2405.13063v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13063v3)
- Aurora wave skill is against the HRES-WAM analysis, not buoys: "within 20% RMSE" of HRES-WAM on 96 % of targets, matching or beating it on 86 % of wave variables across lead times and 91 % at three days; `pp1d` is the weak variable. Missing values over land and sea ice are handled with density channels. [F] — [arXiv 2405.13063v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13063v3)
- Aurora inputs and cost: optimal only with HRES T0 meteorology plus HRES-WAM analysis waves; the worked example pulls waves from ECMWF MARS (class `od`, stream `wave`, type `an`; ECMWF account) and runs two 6-hour steps. "Approximately 40 GB of GPU memory" for 0.25° inference. Hugging Face tags the weights MIT; the Azure catalogue also lists MIT; the repository's LICENSE.txt was not opened. [F]/[S] — [wave example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/aurora/main/docs/example_wave.ipynb); [usage](https://microsoft.github.io/aurora/usage.html); [HF microsoft/aurora](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/aurora); [Azure catalogue](https://ai.azure.com/catalog/models/Aurora)
- **ECMWF AIFS waves (changed in 2026):** AIFS Single v2 was implemented 12 May 2026 and outputs 11 wave fields: `swh`, `mwd`, `mwp`, `cdww`, bathymetry and band heights `h1012` ... `h2530`. ECMWF calls it its "first operational data-driven wave forecasts"; 0.25°, four runs a day, to 360 h. Weights CC BY 4.0 (checkpoint 994 MB), run with `anemoi-inference` from open-data initial conditions; inference memory is not stated. [F] — [AIFS Single Wave Set XII](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/datasets/aifs-forecast-aifs-single-wave-set-xii-aifs-single-wave); [HF ecmwf/aifs-single-2.0](https://huggingface.co/ecmwf/aifs-single-2.0)
- AIFS wave training used an ERA5-forced ecWAM hindcast for 1979-2023; ECMWF reports "a gain of roughly one forecast day" for medium-range H_s over the operational system (blog, 21 Aug 2025). The AIFS Marine prototype (blog, 12 Oct 2026) adds surface ocean and sea ice and cuts H_s RMSE against altimeters by about 10 % relative to ecWAM for May-August 2024. No buoy numbers, no spectra, no partitions. [F] — [AIFS waves blog](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/aifs-blog/2025/representing-ocean-wind-waves-ecmwfs-aifs); [AIFS Marine blog](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/aifs-blog/2026/bringing-surface-ocean-aifs)
- **OceanCastNet** (Zhang et al., JAMES 17, 2025; arXiv v4 2 Nov 2025): global wave forecaster from wind and wave fields predicting H_s, mean period and mean direction to 228 h; better than operational ECWAM at 24 NDBC stations against 10. Code or weights not stated on the arXiv page. [A] — [arXiv 2406.03848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03848)
- Other dedicated wave learners, bulk H_s only: a rolling global model from current H_s and ERA5 winds (GMD, Aug 2025), framed as a surrogate; GWSM4C with a nearshore variant (RMSE 0.161 m, r 0.951 in untrained areas) and the WaveUformer post-processor (2026); a residual U-Net correcting WW3 forecasts (2026 preprint, not peer reviewed). [S] — [GMD 18:5101](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/18/5101/2025/); [GWSM4C-NS](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2024.1437043/epub); [WaveUformer](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1732870/pdf); [preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202606.0758/v1)
- A neural emulator of the exact nonlinear wave-wave interaction term, coupled online into WAVEWATCH III (JGR ML and Computation, 2026), cut integrated spectral error by about 5 % and mean H_s RMSE from roughly 0.17 to 0.07 (DOE summary; units and setting not read). This is ML inside the physics model, not a forecaster. [S] — [DOE highlight](https://www.energy.gov/science/ber/articles/improving-how-models-predict-ocean-waves-ai)
- NVIDIA FourCastNet 3 lists 72 surface and pressure-level atmospheric variables; no wave variable was seen. [S] — [NGC model card](https://catalog.ngc.nvidia.com/orgs/nvidia/earth-2/models/fourcastnet3/0.1.0)

### Inferences
- For a nowcast at observation time, an AI forecaster adds nothing the analysis it starts from does not already hold. Aurora Wave is worth running only if the project turns to forecasting; its input (HRES-WAM analysis with partitions) is itself a better baseline than ERA5 and is the harder thing to obtain.
- One H200 (141 GB) runs either model: Aurora 0.25° needs about 40 GB; the AIFS checkpoint is under 1 GB of weights.
- AIFS and IFS band heights give a swell breakdown but no wind sea; they cannot stand in for the partition the camera is compared with.
- Both systems learn from analyses that assimilate altimeter H_s, so they inherit the analysis's wind-sea errors; a camera wind-sea observation is independent of all of them.

### Gaps
- Not verified (search budget ended): whether FuXi/FuXi-Ocean, Pangu-Weather derivatives, GraphCast/WeatherNext 2, CorrDiff, or OceanNet/Orca-type ocean models output surface waves. No evidence of wave outputs was found for any of them in the searches that did run.
- "WaveCastNet" was not confirmed as an ocean-wave model; no ocean-wave paper of that name surfaced.
- Aurora supplementary Section C.5 (wave post-processing) and the fine-tuning cost for waves were not read; the Aurora repository LICENSE.txt was not opened.
- No AI model with 2-D spectral output or published weights for spectra was found.

## 3. ML correction of wave-model output with observations (the "model-only" bar)

### Takeaway
Published buoy-trained correctors report 10-60 % error cuts, but nearly all feed the corrector recent observations from the same buoy, which is assimilation of the target itself. Evidence for transfer to points with no instrument is thin and mostly unquantified, so the fair model-only bar for the camera is a cross-buoy corrector with no buoy lags, and it has to be built in-house.

### Cited Findings
- **SWRL Net** (Mooneyham, Crosby, Kumar, Hutchinson; Weather and Forecasting 35:2445-2460, 2020, doi:10.1175/WAF-D-19-0254.1): a fully convolutional network correcting WW3 frequency-directional forecasts up to 24 h ahead from recent WW3 output and buoy observations, trained on 10 years at three US West Coast buoys. H_s RMSE fell 23-50 % in the first 6 h and 10-20 % after; mean direction 28-54 %; mean period 20-56 %; sea bands (5-10 s) improved more than swell (12-20 s). [S] (AMS page returned 403; bibliographic record [F]) — [CSDMS record](https://csdms.colorado.edu/wiki/Reference:Reference-018641); [abstract mirror](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/swrl-net-a-spectral-residual-deep-learning-model-for-improving-short-term-wave-forecasts/812578279746699265-139)
- Follow-up (Dawson, Reneau, Hutchinson, Crosby; ICLR 2022 AI for Earth Sciences workshop): the input is a (2τh + τp) x 5 x F tensor of energy and four directional moments per frequency (0.04-0.25 Hz), using 6-12 h of buoy history to correct the next 12-24 h. Seven West Coast buoys; error reductions of 10-60 %; diminishing returns beyond 5 years of training. Cross-buoy transfer: a model pretrained on another buoy and applied without fine-tuning roughly halved WW3's mean squared error at four new buoys (for example Port Orford 0.195 to 0.094-0.098, Crescent City 0.263 to 0.100-0.113), with one failure (Columbia River 0.211 to 0.120 from one source buoy). On real archived forecasts errors were much higher and the authors ask for evaluation on "other coastal regions, such as the U. S. East coast". [F] — [Dawson et al. 2022](https://ai4earthscience.github.io/iclr-2022-workshop/camera_ready/iclr_2022_ai4ess_24.pdf)
- Campos et al. (NCEP Office Note 490, 2017; journal version Ocean Modelling 149, 2020): neural-network ensemble averaging and bias correction of the NCEP wave ensemble at two buoys improved the 5-day forecast by 64 % in bias, 29 % in RMSE and scatter index, 11 % in correlation; a pilot, sensitive to random seeds. [A] — [NOAA repository](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/16085)
- Ellenson et al. (Coastal Engineering, 2020): bagged regression trees learned error patterns of 24-h WW3 H_s forecasts at buoys off California-Oregon; applying predicted deviations lowered RMSE, bias and scatter index (no percentages in the snippet). [S] — [record](https://www.aminer.org/pub/5e5e19da93d709897ce8cbba/an-application-of-a-machine-learning-algorithm-to-determine-and-describe-error)
- Zhang et al. (Ocean Modelling 187, 2024): a trajectory-GRU network corrected gridded ECMWF-IFS H_s forecasts in the western North Pacific at 0-240 h; MAE fell 13-46 % in spring and 14-39 % in winter. [A] — [arXiv 2311.15001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15001)
- Gradient-boosted trees on the model-minus-buoy H_s residual cut errors at all tested sites (JMSE 2024); MLP and LSTM post-processing improved ERA5 hourly H_s at two buoys (NOAA report); GAM and XGBoost corrected a Tyrrhenian Sea model with altimeter and buoy data (2025). None reports held-out-buoy tests. [S] — [JMSE 12:1573](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/12/9/1573); [NOAA report](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/55796); [PoliTO record](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/3007521)
- Spatial transfer: an EGU 2026 abstract calibrates quantile mapping of ERA5 H_s, T_p and wind on some North Sea buoys (2018-2019; sub-areas of 6 and 18 points), interpolates the correction factors and tests with leave-one-out and leave-two-out; no numbers in the abstract. [A] — [EGU26-10234](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-10234.html)
- Copernicus Marine (May 2026) describes networks that correct wind and current forcing before the wave model, improving storm H_s and period against buoys off Galicia. [S] — [Copernicus Marine news](https://marine.copernicus.eu/news/how-artificial-intelligence-powering-next-generation-operational-marine-products)

### Inferences
- The project's partition-aware fusion gain (0.288 to 0.267 m, about 7 % [P]) is small beside the 10-60 % these correctors report, but they are not the same test: the published correctors see the buoy's own last hours, and the project's protocol holds whole buoys out.
- The model-only bar that any camera fusion must beat is therefore: gradient boosting or a small network on model features only (ERA5 and GFS-Wave total and partition parameters, wind, depth, distance to coast, location), fitted under the same grouped folds, with no buoy lags. A quantile-mapping variant covers the simplest end. No published study reports this number for NDBC, so it must be measured.
- SWRL Net's cross-buoy result suggests model error structure is partly shared between sites, which supports training one corrector across the 79 buoys.
- The spectral input format (energy plus a1, b1, a2, b2 per frequency) matches what NDBC and CDIP buoys report, so it is the natural common format for model and buoy spectra.

### Gaps
- No full-text read of Mooneyham et al. 2020, Ellenson et al. 2020 or Campos et al. 2020.
- No study was found comparing quantile mapping, gradient boosting and neural correctors on the same wave data with held-out sites.
- No published correction of ERA5 partition parameters (wind sea, swell) against buoy partitions was found.

## 4. Nearshore transformation: physics, learned surrogates, CDIP MOP and bathymetry

### Takeaway
CDIP MOP is, on all evidence found, a California-only system, so no MOP lines exist for North Carolina, Florida or Hawaii. At the reference buoys' depths (13-17 m) shoaling changes wave height by under 10 %, so buoy-to-camera differences come from refraction, sheltering and alongshore variation, which a small stationary SWAN run on CUDEM bathymetry (or a look-up table built from it) can supply.

### Cited Findings
- MOP: "wave Monitoring and Prediction (MOP) system for the California coastline"; a "linear spectral refraction model initialized with 2-D spectral estimates from multiple Datawell directional buoys", swell (0.04-0.08 Hz) from deep-water buoys and sea (0.09-0.5 Hz) from shelf-break buoys; points in 10 m depth every 100 m alongshore; output H_s, T_p, mean period, directions, radiation stresses and E, a1, b1, a2, b2 per frequency. Validated with 20 m buoys: "significant skill at most validation sites, but prediction errors for individual swell or sea events can be large"; low bias; only fair where island shadowing and poor bathymetry matter. [F] — [Ludka et al., Scientific Data 2019](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/0cp9087z)
- CDIP's MOP v1.1 documentation lists `CA_v1.1_transect_definitions` and `R_CA_transformation_coeffs` files, and the THREDDS model catalogue holds only MOP_alongshore, MOP_grids, MOP_validation, R_CA_coefficients and misc. No other state appears. One California dataset counts 11,594 alongshore sites. [F]/[L]/[S] — [MOP v1.1 docs](https://cdip.ucsd.edu/MOP_v1.1/); [CDIP THREDDS models](https://thredds.cdip.ucsd.edu/thredds/catalog/cdip/model/catalog.html); [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/6946105)
- O'Reilly et al. 2016 (Coastal Engineering 116:118-132) describe the method as a "non-stationary, linear, spectral refraction wave model" driven by offshore buoys, better for monthly or annual climate than for single events. [S] — [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2016.06.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2016.06.005)
- Learned surrogates of SWAN: a DeepONet for a steady-state Duck, NC case maps boundary waves and wind to H_s and radiation-stress gradients with relative L2 error 1.91 % for H_s and a four-orders-of-magnitude speed-up (Cai et al., Ocean Engineering 367, 2026). DELWAVE reproduces SWAN H_s at Adriatic points with MAE 5-10 cm (GMD 2024). A random forest reproduced SWAN spatial fields 100 times faster (Chen et al. 2021). An earlier framework forced SWAN with offshore buoy data and trained ML on the output (2017). [A]/[S] — [arXiv 2604.06433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06433); [DELWAVE](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/17/4705/2024/); [Chen et al.](https://researchportal.plymouth.ac.uk/en/publications/using-machine-learning-to-derive-spatial-wave-data-a-case-study-f/); [arXiv 1709.08725](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.08725)
- Operational physics transforms covering the project's coasts: NWPS (SWAN, 5 km to 200 m, hourly, with ESTOFS water levels and RTOFS currents) for MHX, AKQ, ILM, MFL, MLB, JAX and HFO; PacIOOS SWAN at 500 m for Oahu. [F] — [EMC NWPS](https://www.emc.ncep.noaa.gov/emc/pages/numerical_forecast_systems/nwps.php); [PacIOOS](https://www.pacioos.hawaii.edu/metadata/swan_oahu.html)
- NRL's SWANFAR is a variational (adjoint) assimilation system for SWAN that controls the boundary spectrum; its spectra "compare well to measured spectral data at Duck, North Carolina", with later runs at the Duck 17 m Waverider and 11 m AWAC. [S] — [Orzech et al. 2013](https://www7320.nrlssc.navy.mil/pubs/2013/orzech1-2013.pdf); [NRL 2016](https://www7320.nrlssc.navy.mil/pubs/2016/orzech-2016.pdf)
- Bathymetry: NOAA NCEI CUDEM tiles are 1/9 arc-second (about 3 m) topobathy at the coast, coarsening to 1/3 arc-second offshore, public domain, on THREDDS; Hawaii has its own CUDEM set at 1/9, 1/3 and 3 arc-seconds. State tile coverage for NC and FL was not checked. [S] — [CUDEM metadata](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/metadata/geoportal/rest/metadata/item/gov.noaa.ngdc.mgg.dem:199919/html); [Amante et al. 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/6/1702)

### Inferences
- Own calculation (linear theory, normal incidence, no refraction or dissipation): the shoaling coefficient relative to deep water is 0.91-1.05 at 13-17 m depth for periods of 4-14 s. Moving to 5 m it is 0.92-1.02 for 4-8 s but 1.11-1.28 for 10-14 s; at 3 m, 1.24-1.44 for 10-14 s. So a buoy label in 13-17 m is close to valid for a camera viewing water of similar depth, and grows increasingly wrong for long swell if the camera's footprint is the inner surf zone.
- A physics transform can make the buoy label valid at the camera in two ways: (a) a stationary SWAN run per sea state on a 50-200 m grid with CUDEM bathymetry and the buoy or WIS spectrum on the boundary, pre-computed as a look-up table over (height, period, direction, tide), giving the label at the camera footprint; (b) the same output as a prior input to the camera model. The DeepONet and DELWAVE results show such a table can be replaced by a network at no loss that matters, but a table is enough for a handful of sites.
- With 9-29 km between camera and buoy, alongshore wind-sea differences (fetch, local wind) are not fixed by a boundary-forced transform; NWPS gridded fields, which include local wind forcing, are the only product found that addresses that, and only prospectively.
- The US Wave hindcast (200 m nearshore, 1979-2010) cannot label 2025 data but can train a site surrogate or supply long-term buoy-to-shore transfer statistics.

### Gaps
- XBeach, CMS-Wave and the USACE Duck model test bed were not researched; no surrogate that maps offshore 2-D spectra plus bathymetry plus tide to breaking height was found.
- MOP coverage outside California is unconfirmed by a direct CDIP statement; the conclusion rests on file names, catalogue contents and every description saying "California".
- No published accuracy for NWPS against NC nearshore buoys was found.
- O'Reilly et al. 2016 was not read in full (ScienceDirect).

## 5. Data assimilation and fusion of partial wave observations; camera precedents

### Takeaway
Operational wave assimilation already works the way the project's fusion does: split the spectrum into partitions, correct each partition's mean parameters by optimal interpolation, and rebuild the spectrum. Camera-derived wave data has been assimilated into a wave model once (video peak period into SWAN, 2023), and web-camera visibility is assimilated in a NOAA surface analysis; no precedent was found for camera wave height or wind sea in any wave or weather model.

### Cited Findings
- OI-P scheme (Voorrips et al. 1997; Hasselmann et al. 1997): the spectrum is projected onto a few mean parameters per partition (swell, wind sea), model and observed partitions are cross-assigned, and the parameters are updated by optimal interpolation, which keeps cost low. For altimeters, H_s is analysed by optimal interpolation and the spectrum is then corrected "using empirical laws and assumptions" according to the sea/swell ratio. [S] — [Voorrips et al. 1997](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Voorrips_etal_JGR1997.pdf); [Aouf et al. 2006](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Aouf_etal_JTECH2006.pdf); [ECMWF 2012](https://ecmwf.int/sites/default/files/elibrary/2012/14796-latest-developments-wave-data-assimilation.pdf)
- SAR and CFOSAT: MFWAM assimilates partition wavenumbers from SWIM operationally "since 2nd February 2021"; reported gains are H_s bias reduction (Southern Ocean), a slight T_p improvement and better direction, without headline percentages; retrieval of partition energy was still being improved. [S] — [OSTST 2023](https://ostst.aviso.altimetry.fr/fileadmin/user_upload/OSTST2023/Presentations/CFO2023-On_the_assimilation_of_reprocessed_SWIM_wave_data_in_the_wave_model_MFWAM.pdf); [OSTST 2020](https://meetings.aviso.altimetry.fr/fileadmin/user_upload/tx_ausyclsseminar/files/OSTST2020_LAouf_SO_cfosat.pdf)
- Sofar (Houghton et al., GRL 2022): wave spectra from over 600 Spotter buoys assimilated "via optimal interpolation to update model spectra to best fit observations"; "improvements in forecast skill of significant wave height of 38%" and up to 45 % for other bulk parameters. Follow-up (Houghton et al., Ocean Modelling 2023): a Local Ensemble Transform Kalman Filter on H_s from Spotters and altimeters gave "reduced forecast errors out to 2.5 days" and more realistic updates than optimal interpolation. [A] — [Dryad record](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.d2547d84v); [Sofar note](https://www.sofarocean.com/posts/ensemble-based-data-assimilation-of-significant-wave-height-from-sofar-spotters-and-satellite-altimeters-with-a-global-operational-wave-model)
- Altimeter limits: assimilating altimeter H_s does not always help because it carries no spectral detail; ERA5 and WAVERYS use forward-only assimilation of altimeter data that are not jointly calibrated across missions. [S] — [GMD 18:5101](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/18/5101/2025/); [ESSD 18:6841](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/18/6841/2026/)
- **Camera into a wave model:** Saavedra et al. (Computers and Geosciences, 2023) estimate peak period from time-stack video and assimilate it by correcting the nested SWAN model's boundary conditions (successive corrections, synthetic spectra replacing the prescribed boundary spectra). [S] (publisher and mirrors blocked) — [doi:10.1016/j.cageo.2023.105407](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cageo.2023.105407); [repository record](https://repository.udem.edu.co/handle/11407/7887)
- Video in nearshore assimilation otherwise targets bathymetry: Beach Wizard assimilates video-derived roller dissipation and radar celerity (Van Dongeren et al. 2008); an ensemble system coupling SWAN and ROMS assimilates optical wave celerity and currents every half hour. Video-derived directional spectra at Hasaki, Japan agreed with SWAN in shallow water. [S] — [Van Dongeren et al. 2008](https://research.engr.oregonstate.edu/haller/inc/Publications/VanDongeren_etal_CoastEng_2008.pdf); [ICCE paper](https://icce-ojs-tamu.tdl.org/icce/article/download/6725/pdf/28552); [Hasaki study](https://journals.utm.my/jurnalteknologi/article/download/4651/3239/13000)
- **Camera into a weather analysis:** Carley et al. (Experimental Results 2, e14, 2021) assimilate visibility estimated from FAA web cameras (edge detection against a clear-day reference; 320 camera sites, 154 without a nearby METAR) into NOAA's 2DVar Real Time Mesoscale Analysis over Alaska, with camera errors inflated by up to 1.4 times at low confidence; detection of low-visibility classes improved with slightly more false alarms. DWD ran experiments assimilating camera-derived cloud information into ICON-D2 (2022 abstract). [F]/[S] — [Carley et al. 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/experimental-results/article/variational-assimilation-of-web-cameraderived-estimates-of-visibility-for-alaska-aviation/3303F8BEC7EB9BDCF7D412FF11F238D9); [EMS2022-39](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EMS2022/EMS2022-39.html)
- Learned assimilation (weather, not waves): score-based assimilation with a diffusion model trained on HRRR analyses, conditioned on 40 stations, gave about 10 % lower RMSE at left-out stations but under-dispersed ensembles (Manshausen et al.). DiffDA conditions a GraphCast-based diffusion model on sparse observations at inference; FlowDA (2026) uses flow matching; Latent-EnSF argues masked-interpolation approaches fall behind interpolation after a few steps; DABench benchmarks AI assimilation. [A]/[S] — [arXiv 2406.16947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16947); [DiffDA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.05932); [FlowDA](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06800); [Latent-EnSF](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.00127); [DABench](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11438v2)
- Learned reconstruction for waves: AUWave (Ocean Engineering 350, 2026) rebuilds regional H_s fields around Hawaii from sparse NDBC buoys and ERA5 with a station encoder and attention U-Net, error lowest near observation sites; no numbers in the abstract. [A] — [arXiv 2509.19384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19384)

### Inferences
- The project's "replace ERA5's wind-sea energy with the camera's" is a hard-replacement special case of partition assimilation. The literature-backed refinement is to weight instead of replace: update only the wind-sea partition's energy by optimal interpolation with weights from out-of-fold error variances, keep the swell partitions, and sum energies for the total.
- Indicative size (assuming unbiased, independent errors, using [P] RMSEs of 0.215 m for the camera and 0.341 m for ERA5 wind-wave height): camera weight 0.72 and a combined wind-sea error near 0.18 m. Correlated errors would shrink that gain; the project's measured 30 % cut with wind added is consistent with it.
- Feeding a single-point, wind-sea-only observation into a running spectral model is poorly supported: every operational precedent has spectra or many H_s observations, and the one camera precedent corrects a boundary condition. For a point nowcast, statistical partition fusion gets the benefit without a model run.
- Carley et al. give the template for camera observation errors: a per-site confidence index that inflates the error, which maps onto the project's planned gate for dusk, haze and glare.

### Gaps
- No precedent found for assimilating camera- or webcam-derived wave height, wind sea or mean-square slope into any wave or weather model; none found for mean-square-slope (GNSS-R) assimilation into a wave model either. HF radar: one 4D-Var study of HF Doppler spectra surfaced but was not read ([Sensors 21:7811](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/21/23/7811/html)).
- The standard argument that wind-sea corrections decay within hours unless the wind is also corrected was not sourced this session; treat it as unverified background.
- Voorrips 1997, Aouf 2006 and the ECMWF 2012 slides could not be opened (403 or image-only PDF); Saavedra 2023's site and quantitative gain were not obtained.
- No learned or score-based assimilation applied to a spectral wave model was found.

## 6. Spectrum-level learning: representing, reconstructing and calibrating wave spectra

### Takeaway
The working representation in published learning systems is the buoy-native one: energy plus four directional moments per frequency band, corrected as a residual on a physics prior. Parametric priors (JONSWAP for the wind sea) fused with observed swell are the closest published design to the project's fusion, while learned 2-D reconstruction is reported as fragile outside its training seas. No diffusion model on wave spectra was found.

### Cited Findings
- SWRL Net treats the spectrum as a 5 x F image (e, a1, b1, a2, b2 by frequency), predicts residuals added to the WW3 spectrum, and constrains outputs with ReLU (energy) and tanh (moments). [F] — [Dawson et al. 2022](https://ai4earthscience.github.io/iclr-2022-workshop/camera_ready/iclr_2022_ai4ess_24.pdf)
- Gao and Jiang (JGR ML and Computation, 2025): a physics-guided network predicts single-point directional spectra from two wind inputs, a high-resolution local field for the wind sea and a low-resolution, large-area, long-history field for swell, without predefined spectral shapes; evaluated on ERA5 spectra offshore and hindcast spectra at the coast. No metrics in the abstract. [A] — [doi:10.1029/2024JH000492](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024JH000492)
- Wang et al. (Remote Sensing 2026): a learned wind-sea height sets a JONSWAP prior spectrum, fused with the SAR-observed swell spectrum by a U-Net; H_s RMSE 0.40 m against ERA5, buoy validation missing. [P]/[S] — [doi:10.3390/rs18111703](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18111703)
- Afham and Chandrasena (preprint): residual learning on an analytical Fourier-expansion reconstruction from the 1-D spectrum and directional moments, with energy and moment-consistency losses; ML models "are sensitive to domain shift, whereas analytical methods demonstrate great robustness". [S] — [engrXiv 6980](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/view/6980)
- Inverse problems from other sensors: a CNN maps marine-radar image spectra to directional spectra, beating the modulation-transfer-function method on synthetic data (Ludeno et al., JMSE 2024). [S] — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/07e43c9bd85349b0a604f0fb2897887d)
- Diffusion for sparse-observation field reconstruction exists for general fields (Zhuang et al.) but not for wave spectra. [S] — [arXiv 2409.00230](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00230v2)
- Spectral grids in hand or on offer: ERA5 24 x 30 [F]; ECMWF CY50R1 hindcast 36 x 36 [F]; GFS-Wave bulletins list up to six systems as (H_s, T_p, direction) triples [L]. — [ERA5 doc](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/display/CKB/ERA5%3A+data+documentation); [j5rf](https://apps.ecmwf.int/ifs-experiments/rd/j5rf/); [bulletin](https://nomads.ncep.noaa.gov/pub/data/nccf/com/gfs/prod/gfs.20261119/00/wave/station/bulls.t00z/gfswave.41025.bull)

### Inferences
- For the fusion head, "partition tokens" are already available without any spectra download: each GFS-Wave bulletin row is a short set of (height, period, direction, wind-sea flag) triples, a natural variable-length input for a small set or attention model, with the camera's wind-sea estimate as one more token.
- Where spectra are used, reducing model 2-D spectra to (e, a1, b1, a2, b2) per frequency puts ERA5, WIS, NWPS and NDBC/CDIP data on one footing and avoids comparing a model's directional resolution with a buoy's.
- Calibrated uncertainty for the fused estimate is best obtained outside the spectrum model (seed ensembles plus conformal or quantile calibration under grouped folds, as the project already plans [P]); nothing in the spectrum literature found offers calibrated intervals.
- Afham's result argues for keeping a physics or parametric prior and learning only the residual, especially since the test is at unseen buoys.

### Gaps
- No work found on transformers over spectral bins, diffusion on wave spectra, or uncertainty calibration of learned spectra.
- No published method found for reconstructing a full directional spectrum from a wind-sea-only observation plus a model swell spectrum other than Wang et al. 2026.
- Gao and Jiang's metrics and Wang et al.'s architecture were not read in full this session.

## 7. Time-series and tabular foundation models for short-horizon nowcasting of buoy parameters

### Takeaway
Among the time-series models, only Chronos-2 and TimesFM 2.5 are both current and commercially open (Apache-2.0); Chronos-2 alone accepts past-only covariates, which is what lagged camera and model features are. The single wave-height study found puts zero-shot Chronos mid-table against trained baselines. Tabular models after TabPFN v2 carry non-commercial weights.

### Cited Findings
- Chronos-2 (Amazon): Apache-2.0, 120 M parameters, encoder-only; univariate, multivariate, and covariate-informed forecasting with past-only and known-future real or categorical covariates; context to 8,192 steps, horizon to 1,024; paper dated 17 Oct 2025. [F] — [HF amazon/chronos-2](https://huggingface.co/amazon/chronos-2)
- Chronos-2's report lists it as the only model of its comparison set handling univariate, multivariate, past-only, known and categorical covariates; TabPFN-TS handles known covariates only; Moirai 2.0, TimesFM 2.5 and TiRex are univariate there. [S] — [arXiv 2510.15821](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15821v1)
- TimesFM 2.5 (Google): Apache-2.0, 200 M parameters. A secondary source says TimesFM 3.0 moved to a non-commercial licence. [F]/[S] — [HF google/timesfm-2.5-200m-pytorch](https://huggingface.co/google/timesfm-2.5-200m-pytorch); [blog](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/timesfm-licensing-commercial-use)
- Moirai 2.0 (Salesforce): CC BY-NC 4.0, "for research purposes only", 11.4 M parameters (small). [F] — [HF Salesforce/moirai-2.0-R-small](https://huggingface.co/Salesforce/moirai-2.0-R-small)
- TabPFN (Prior Labs): v2 weights are Apache 2.0 with an attribution requirement (10,000 rows, 500 columns). Weights for 2.5 (Nov 2025), 2.6 (Apr 2026), 3 (May 2026) and 3.5 (Sep 2026; up to 1,000,000 rows) are non-commercial; code is Apache 2.0. Non-commercial use covers internal experimentation. [F] — [Prior Labs models](https://docs.priorlabs.ai/models)
- TabPFN-TS: Apache-2.0 code, v1.3.0 (15 Sep 2026) defaults to TabPFN-3.5 weights; uses known-future covariates, not past-only or static covariates. [F] — [tabpfn-time-series](https://github.com/PriorLabs/tabpfn-time-series)
- TabICLv2 (ICML 2026; checkpoints dated 12 Feb 2026) adds regression; pre-trained on 300 to 48,000 rows, benchmarked to 100,000; "permissive license" (exact licence file not read). TabDPT v1.3 (8 Sep 2026): Apache-2.0 code, probabilistic regression; weights licence not stated on the repository page. [F] — [TabICL](https://github.com/soda-inria/tabicl); [TabDPT](https://github.com/layer6ai-labs/TabDPT)
- Geophysical evidence: Zhai et al. (Ocean Engineering 341, 2025) test Chronos on buoy H_s at 1-24 h and 1-120 h; zero-shot holds "median performance (rank 4/12)" against trained models; a search summary of the full text gives zero-shot at 93.6-95.1 % of fine-tuned accuracy and fine-tuned about 1 % better RMSE than PatchTST, with site-to-site variability. [A]/[S] — [arXiv 2504.16834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16834)
- Covariate behaviour: TabPFN-TS captured simple target-covariate relationships better than Chronos-2 at short horizons (ICLR 2026); in an Oct 2026 load-forecasting benchmark, fine-tuned Chronos-2 led with exact or mildly noisy future covariates, but a conventional model was more robust under severe covariate noise. [S] — [arXiv 2605.12200](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.12200); [arXiv 2610.07232](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.07232v1)

### Inferences
- These models forecast a series from its own past. At an unseen buoy or a shore camera there is no target history, so they cannot set the project's main baseline; they apply only to a buoy-in-the-loop nowcast (persistence-plus), which is a different claim.
- Where a buoy history exists, Chronos-2 with the wave model and camera as past covariates is the one configuration worth a test, because it is the only open model that accepts past-only covariates.
- As a combiner on tabular out-of-fold features, TabPFN v2 or TabICLv2 can sit beside ridge and gradient boosting; newer TabPFN weights are fine for research but not for any commercial follow-on.

### Gaps
- No evaluation found of TimesFM, Moirai, Lag-Llama or the tabular models on wave or other buoy series; Lag-Llama's licence was not verified.
- No test found of any of these models with camera- or model-derived covariates for sea state.
- Zhai et al.'s station list is inconsistent between sources (the abstract says three Northwest Pacific zones; a summary names five NDBC stations).

## 8. Which model-side upgrades most raise the baseline, and which fusion formulation is best supported

### Takeaway
Raise the baseline with three things in this order: GFS-Wave buoy-point partitions from the public archive, ERA5 2-D spectra partitioned with the same code as the buoy spectra, and a cross-buoy learned corrector stacked over ERA5, GFS-Wave and MFWAM with no buoy lags. Fuse the camera as an observation of the wind-sea partition's energy, weighted by out-of-fold error variances and gated on image quality, leaving the model's swell untouched.

### Cited Findings
- GFS-Wave publishes hourly per-buoy wave systems (H_s, T_p, direction, wind-sea flag) and 2-D spectra, archived for 2025 in a public bucket. [L] — [bulletin sample](https://nomads.ncep.noaa.gov/pub/data/nccf/com/gfs/prod/gfs.20261119/00/wave/station/bulls.t00z/gfswave.41025.bull); [S3 listing](https://noaa-gfs-bdp-pds.s3.amazonaws.com/?list-type=2&prefix=gfs.20250115/00/wave/station/&delimiter=/)
- ERA5 2-D spectra are retrievable only through `reanalysis-era5-complete`; ERA6 is not due before late 2027. [F] — [ERA5 doc](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/display/CKB/ERA5%3A+data+documentation); [ECMWF Newsletter 188](https://www.ecmwf.int/node/38351)
- MFWAM products give an independent model family with wind-sea and two swell partitions at 1/12° and SAR spectral assimilation; WAVERYS scored 0.33 m against ERA5's 0.37 m at coastal buoys. [F]/[S] — [CMEMS analysis](https://data.marine.copernicus.eu/product/GLOBAL_ANALYSISFORECAST_WAV_001_027/description); [Commun. Earth Environ. 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s43247-023-00819-0)
- Buoy-trained correctors cut model error 10-60 % when given the buoy's recent data, and pretrained correctors transfer across buoys. [F] — [Dawson et al. 2022](https://ai4earthscience.github.io/iclr-2022-workshop/camera_ready/iclr_2022_ai4ess_24.pdf)
- NWPS provides SWAN 2-D spectra at Duck-area and other NC buoys with five-day retention. [L] — [mhx CG1](https://nomads.ncep.noaa.gov/pub/data/nccf/com/nwps/prod/er.20261119/mhx/00/CG1/)
- Partition-wise optimal interpolation is the established way to assimilate partial spectral information; the only camera-to-wave-model precedent corrects a boundary condition. [S] — [Voorrips et al. 1997](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Voorrips_etal_JGR1997.pdf); [Saavedra et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cageo.2023.105407)
- Aurora Wave needs ECMWF wave analyses as input and 40 GB of GPU memory; AIFS outputs no partitions. [F] — [Aurora usage](https://microsoft.github.io/aurora/usage.html); [HF AIFS](https://huggingface.co/ecmwf/aifs-single-2.0)

### Inferences
Deciding principle: an upgrade ranks by whether it covers the 2025-2026 label period at the 79 buoys and NC sites, gives the wind-sea/swell split on the buoy's own definition, and costs days rather than weeks.

| Rank | Upgrade | Expected value | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | GFS-Wave bulletins at buoy points from `noaa-gfs-bdp-pds` (first hours of each cycle) | High: wave-system partitions at the exact buoy, hourly, for the label year; second model family beside ERA5 | Low: about 70 GB a year of small tarballs, text parsing |
| 2 | ERA5 `2dfd` spectra, partitioned with one code path shared with NDBC/CDIP and WIS spectra | High for the fusion: removes the partition-definition mismatch that makes ERA5's swell error exceed its total error | Medium: slow MARS queue; decoder already prepared |
| 3 | Cross-buoy learned corrector and multi-model stack (ERA5 + GFS-Wave + MFWAM 1/12°), no buoy lags, grouped folds | High: this is the true model-only bar; published gains are with buoy lags, so expect less | Low-medium: CPU days; CMEMS needs a free account |
| 4 | Start collecting NWPS spectra and CG1 grids for MHX, AKQ, ILM (and HFO) now | Medium, prospective only: the one SWAN nearshore product at the NC shore sites | Low: a daily pull, about 1 MB per spectrum point and 26-94 MB per grid |
| 5 | Stationary SWAN look-up table on CUDEM bathymetry per shore camera | Medium: carries the buoy label or model spectrum to the camera footprint; matters most for long swell in shallow views | Medium: one to two weeks per site set-up |
| 6 | ECMWF open-data band heights; `j5rf` 9 km hindcast | Low-medium: free swell bands above 10 s; possible higher-resolution ERA5 successor if its span covers the labels | Low to check, unknown to obtain |
| 7 | Aurora Wave, AIFS, learned assimilation, time-series foundation models | Low for a nowcast baseline; relevant only if the project moves to forecasting or buoy-in-the-loop nowcasts | Medium-high |

- Fusion formulation: treat the camera's wind-sea height as an observation of the model's wind-sea partition energy. Analysis energy = background + K x (camera − background), with K from out-of-fold error variances (about 0.7 on current numbers) and reduced by an image-quality gate; swell partitions pass through; total H_s is the root of summed partition energies; a small residual learner on top handles what the linear rule misses. This is OI-P restricted to one partition, matches Wang et al.'s wind-sea prior plus observed swell, and reduces to the project's current replacement rule when K = 1.
- The model whose wind-sea partition is the background should be the one whose partition definition matches the labels best, which rank 1 and rank 2 are meant to settle; with ERA5's own split the fusion inherits ERA5's swell error.
- What would change the order: if GFS-Wave's analysis-hour partitions prove no better than ERA5's at the 79 buoys, rank 2 moves first; if an NWPS archive turns up, rank 4 moves to the top for the shore cameras.

### Gaps
- No published head-to-head of ERA5, GFS-Wave and MFWAM partition accuracy at NDBC buoys exists to confirm the ranking in advance; ranks 1-3 are justified by access and definition, not by a measured gain.
- The expected size of the model-only corrector's gain at unseen buoys is unknown; the literature gives only same-buoy figures.
- Licence wording for WIS and NWPS data was not found on the pages read (US government products; NOAA open-data terms were confirmed only for the GFS bucket).
