# Multimodal and crossmodal learning methods for camera-based wave measurement

Scope: learning strategies that use a second modality as co-input, as training-only supervision, or as a self-supervised pretext; precedents in geoscience, remote sensing and other sensor domains; and translation into experiments on the project's paired data. Current as of 2026-11-19. Builds on, and does not repeat, the earlier notes on heads, uncertainty and late-fusion combiners ([heads_uncertainty_fusion.md](../Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones/heads_uncertainty_fusion.md)) and the report [Wave camera architectures beyond backbones](../../reports/Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones.md).

## Q1. Contrastive cross-modal pretraining (CLIP-style) between imagery and a non-image signal: precedents, data volumes, and whether it beats supervised regression at tens of thousands of labels

### Takeaway
Image↔signal contrastive alignment works at the project's data scale (successful precedents used 50k–550k pairs and batch sizes from 16 to 8,000), and the closest scientific analogue, galaxy image↔spectrum alignment on 198k pairs, matched or beat supervised baselines from frozen embeddings. Its table also shows where the gain is: large for nearest-neighbour retrieval, 0.00–0.03 R² for a trained probe on top of an already self-supervised image encoder. When the paired signal *is* the regression target, published gains over a plain supervised loss are small with tens of thousands of labels; the benefit shows up in low-label, transfer and retrieval settings. No work was found that aligns optical sea-surface imagery with wave spectra, wind, radar or model fields: the ocean precedents (WV-Net, OceanSAR-1) are image-only self-supervision on SAR.

### Inferences
- **Feasibility is not the question; added value is.** 41k strip↔spectrum pairs and 241k still↔hindcast pairs sit inside the range that worked (50k–550k), and with a pretrained, gently tuned image encoder plus a small spectrum encoder, batch sizes of a few hundred to a few thousand fit on one H200. SatCLIP and IMU2CLIP both trained only the new-modality side against a fixed image side, which is the cheap recipe.
- **Expect little in-distribution regression gain.** When the "other modality" is the label (spectrum, partition heights), InfoNCE is a weaker, noisier form of supervision than regression; RnC's +0.09 °C on 35k webcam images and ConVIRT's shrinking margin at full labels are the honest reference points. The published wins are in (a) few labels, (b) transfer / unseen targets, (c) retrieval. The project's problem is (b), but none of these papers tests *unseen-site* transfer of a sensor regression, so the gain there is an open empirical question.
- **What AstroCLIP's table adds.** The project's continued DINOv3 training corresponds to the "before alignment" row. AstroCLIP says two things about going further. For a *trained probe*, alignment to spectra bought 0.00–0.03 R² over the self-supervised image encoder, which matches the small-gain expectation above. For *retrieval*, it bought a great deal (up to +0.19 R² under nearest-neighbour read-out), because alignment reorganises the embedding so that neighbours share physical properties. So the case for an image↔spectrum embedding here rests on the retrieval head and the query interface, not on lower regression error. The spectrum-side embedding may shed some information the image cannot match (swell, in this project's case), which is harmless because only the image side is deployed. Because the spectrum is also the source of the labels, the alignment is best run as one arm of the privileged-supervision experiment (Q2) on identical folds, not as a separate programme.
- **Where contrastive alignment gives something regression cannot (proposals):**
  1. *Spectrum retrieval as a non-parametric head*: embed image and 46-bin spectrum (+ r1, r2, α1, α2) in one space; at test time retrieve the k nearest training spectra and average them. This yields a full spectrum with realistic shape and a "days like this" interface for free.
  2. *Alignment to fields that exist at every camera*: ERA5 and WIS exist at any coastal camera, buoy or not. Aligning stills to hindcast state (wind vector, wind-sea and swell partitions) needs no buoy, so it can use cameras that have no instrument and therefore increases the number of independent sites, the project's binding constraint. This needs additional data: stills from further WebCOOS (or other public) cameras and the ERA5/WIS extracts at them.
  3. *Physical-descriptor third modality* (SeisBind-style): align image, spectrum, and a short vector (wind speed, wind-sea Hs, swell Hs, Tp) so that text-free numeric queries work.
- **Leakage rule specific to cross-modal contrastive pretraining:** pairs carry label information, so every pair from a test buoy, test camera or test period must be excluded from pretraining, unlike image-only SSL where transductive use of unlabeled test images can be declared.
- Novelty: an image↔wave-spectrum embedding from optical cameras appears to be new; the nearest precedents are AstroCLIP (different science) and image-only SAR SSL.

### Gaps
- AstroCLIP's alignment epochs and compute were not retrieved (appendix beyond the portion read); its redshift results are in figures only.
- GeoCLIP (image↔GPS) was not read. Remote-sensing image–text models are covered at abstract level only (RemoteCLIP, GeoRSCLIP; see Q7).

## Q2. Learning using privileged information and cross-modal distillation: training-only signals to improve an image-only student

### Takeaway
Training with a modality that is absent at test time has produced gains of roughly 1–7 accuracy points in RGB-D, video and low-data image classification, and +0.15 IoU in a 2026 SAR-only wildfire study. Two conditions limit it. The privileged signal must carry information the student's input also contains; when the teacher's advantage is modality-specific, a better teacher gives a no-better or worse student. And the gain shrinks to nothing as labelled data grow. For this project that predicts gains on the wind-sea band and wind, none on swell, and most benefit while independent buoys are few.

### Cited Findings
- Generalized distillation unifies Hinton-style distillation with Vapnik's learning using privileged information: a teacher trained on the privileged representation supplies soft targets to a student that sees only the regular input (Lopez-Paz, Bottou, Schölkopf, Vapnik, ICLR 2016). [ABS] — [arXiv 1511.03643](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03643)
- Modality hallucination: Garcia, Morerio & Murino (ECCV 2018) train an RGB "hallucination" stream to mimic a depth stream's features and predictions. On NTU RGB+D (cross-subject / cross-view): RGB-only 66.52 / 71.39 %; depth teacher 70.44 / 75.16 %; two-stream RGB+depth 79.73 / 81.43 %; RGB-only student at test 73.42 / 77.21 %. The student gains +6.9 / +5.8 points over RGB-only and recovers about half the gap to the true two-stream model. Loss: hard labels + temperature-softened teacher labels + Euclidean distance between feature maps (α = 0.5, T = 10). [FT] — [arXiv 1806.07110](https://arxiv.org/html/1806.07110)
- Video motion as privileged information for an appearance student: MARS (CVPR 2019) trains an RGB 3-D CNN to mimic optical-flow-stream features; single-stream accuracy 72.7 % vs 72.0 % RGB and 65.6 % flow on Kinetics (+0.7) [ABS] — [NAVER LABS publication page](https://europe.naverlabs.com/research/publications/mars-motion-augmented-rgb-stream-for-action-recognition/); 80.1 % vs 73.5 % on HMDB51 split 1 (+6.6), with MIT-licensed code and models [SNIP] — [CVPR 2019](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Crasto_MARS_Motion-Augmented_RGB_Stream_for_Action_Recognition_CVPR_2019_paper.html)
- **Privileged information helps with few samples and stops helping with many** (Lambert, Sener & Savarese, CVPR 2018; the privileged signal is object localisation, available only in training). ImageNet single-crop top-1 without → with privileged information: 37.85 → 42.30 % at 75k training images; 55.99 → 55.51 % at 200k; 66.66 → 66.77 % at 600k. At 75k their method led the comparison (multi-task with masks 40.48, modality hallucination 37.66). At 200k plain multi-task learning with the privileged signal as an auxiliary target was best (56.32) and their method was mid-table. The paper's tables disagree slightly on its own 200k figure (55.51 vs 55.20). [FT] — [ar5iv 1805.11614](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1805.11614)
- **Multi-task meteorological targets from outdoor images, measured** (Chu, Liang & Ho, Atmosphere 2021; 53,378 webcam images from 9 cameras; 90/10 split *within* each camera). One network predicting four properties against four single-task networks, RMSE in the paper's units: wind speed 4.35 → 3.89 (−11 %), humidity 11.60 → 10.45, visibility 1.44 → 1.41, temperature 2.80 → 2.90 (slightly worse). The authors themselves note that multi-task training does not always help. [FT-pdf] — [Atmosphere 12, 584 (PDF)](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/atmosphere/atmosphere-12-00584/article_deploy/atmosphere-12-00584.pdf)
- Geoscience precedent, 2026: Crossfire (NeurIPS 2026 workshop on representation learning for Earth observation, not main track) uses Sentinel-2 optical only in training and Sentinel-1 SAR alone at inference, via multiscale feature hallucination and dual decoder supervision. Mean per-tile IoU 0.568 vs 0.416 for the best of nine SAR-only baselines on a held-out 2025 Canadian fire season (500 tiles); 0.602 on 16 fires from 2026 with no fine-tuning. [ABS] — [NeurIPS 2026 virtual](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/157090)
- Remote-sensing hallucination networks for missing optical/SAR exist (DH-ADNet, IEEE TGRS 2021; Kumar et al. 2019), and later work reports that hallucination can fail to transfer knowledge under complex SAR backgrounds and that plain distillation turns into an ill-posed imitation problem when a modality is missing. [SNIP] — [arXiv 1908.10559](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10559.pdf); [arXiv 2601.13502](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.13502)
- **Failure mode, quantified** (Modality Focusing Hypothesis, Xue et al., ICLR 2023): on AV-MNIST a multimodal teacher that is +7.04 points more accurate than a unimodal teacher yields a student 0.37 points *worse* (69.73 vs 70.10 %; no-KD 68.36 %); on NYU Depth V2 a teacher +4.64 mIoU better yields a student 0.22 worse (47.78 vs 48.00). With a teacher restricted to modality-*specific* features, RAVDESS student accuracy collapses to 42.66 % vs 77.22 % (regular KD) and 78.28 % (modality-general teacher). The paper's hypothesis: distillation performance depends on γ, the proportion of *modality-general* decisive features in the teacher. Sharing parameters across modalities in the teacher raised the NYU student from 46.89 to 47.93 mIoU. [FT] — [ar5iv 2206.06487](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.06487)
- Auxiliary-output pretraining: MultiMAE predicts pseudo-labelled depth and segmentation alongside RGB and transfers competitively or better whether or not the extra modalities are present downstream. [ABS] — [arXiv 2204.01678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01678)
- Predicting a privileged modality and feeding it back ("thinking in modalities"): TerraMind generates land-cover tokens from the input and re-uses them, raising Sen1Floods11 mIoU from 81.06 to 83.65 (S-1) and 89.70 to 91.14 (S-2); crop mapping 41.87 → 42.74. [FT] — [TerraMind](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11171v3)
- Earlier project notes found no study showing that multi-output training improves wave *height* for image inputs, and one LSTM study where adding Tp and wind as inputs did not improve Hs. [PROJ] — [heads_uncertainty_fusion.md](../Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones/heads_uncertainty_fusion.md)

### Inferences
- **Map the hypothesis onto the wave spectrum.** Modality-general content between a sea-surface image and the buoy record is the part the camera can see: the high-frequency (wind-sea) bins, wind speed and direction, steepness / mean-square slope, whitecap coverage. Swell energy, peak period and swell direction are modality-specific to the buoy. The Modality Focusing result therefore predicts: distilling the *full* spectrum or a teacher that leans on swell will not help and may hurt; distilling only the camera-visible band should help the wind-sea output and leave total Hs unchanged.
- **A direct measurement of γ is available for free**: train the image model with a 46-bin spectral head and plot test R² per frequency bin at unseen buoys. That "visibility curve" shows which bins are modality-general and sets the cut-off for any privileged loss. It is the cheapest decisive experiment in this section and also the first learned optical spectral head (the earlier notes found none).
- **Candidate privileged signals, by expected usefulness (proposals):**
  1. Buoy wind speed/gust/direction and the wind-sea partition as auxiliary regression targets (multi-task, uncertainty-weighted). Wind is physically the driver of what the camera sees.
  2. Bins above the wind-sea/swell separation frequency, as a normalised shape plus an energy scale.
  3. A hallucination loss in which the image student mimics the embedding of a teacher that sees image + wind + high-frequency spectrum (teacher with shared trunk, per the NYU result).
  4. Shore sites: the ten-minute video (pixel-intensity spectrum, V-JEPA 2 record embedding) as privileged information for a student that sees only the still + time-exposure + brightest image of the same clip. ~1,300 clips have all four; ~108k have the three images. Period information is probably video-specific (low γ), height and breaking extent probably shared (high γ).
  5. WIS 2-D spectrum at shore sites as a privileged target for direction, gated on the visibility curve.
- **What the two second-pass results change.** Lambert's table says to expect the benefit while effective sample size is small. The project has 41k strips but 55 training buoys, and unseen-buoy error is governed by the number of buoys, so it sits nearer the low-data end; the benefit should fade as buoys are added. Chu's table says to judge each auxiliary head separately and check the primary target for harm (wind speed improved 11 %, temperature worsened 4 %).
- **Failure checks to pre-register:** student total-Hs error must not rise; the gain must appear on wind-sea height (currently 0.215 m against ERA5's 0.341 m [PROJ]) at unseen buoys under grouped folds; and a control student distilled from a *label-shuffled* teacher must show no gain.

### Gaps
- Hoffman, Gupta & Darrell (CVPR 2016, "Learning with Side Information through Modality Hallucination") was not re-read; the CVF page returned 403. Canonical, cited by Garcia et al.
- No precedent found for spectrum-as-privileged-information in any wave-sensing paper, nor for video-teacher → time-exposure-student distillation in coastal imaging.
- No controlled study found of auxiliary met-ocean targets improving an image regression at unseen sites.
- MARS's HMDB51 figure is from a search summary; its Kinetics figures and Lambert's and Chu's tables were read in the second pass.
- No wave-measurement paper was found in which a camera model is supervised by a second instrument acting as teacher (radar, lidar, stereo).

## Q3. Masked multimodal modelling and any-to-any models: can images, spectra, met variables and hindcast fields share one masked model?

### Takeaway
Yes in principle, and the tokenisation problem is solved: recent any-to-any models quantise images with learned codebooks, spectra with a 1-D convolutional tokenizer, and scalars with parameter-free quantile bins, then train one masked-token transformer. The smallest successful Earth-observation instance (Galileo) used 127k training instances and 0.8–85 M parameters, which is the project's scale. Reusing published weights is not sensible (they are built for satellite bands or sky surveys); reusing the recipe on cached image tokens is.

### Cited Findings
- 4M (NeurIPS 2023 spotlight): every modality is mapped to discrete tokens; one encoder–decoder is trained to predict a random subset of tokens from another random subset; any modality can condition any other. 4M-21 (2024) extends to "tens" of modalities including feature maps, image metadata and colour palettes. [ABS] — [arXiv 2312.06647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06647); [arXiv 2406.09406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09406). Code Apache-2.0; **weights under Apple's separate "Sample Code" licence**; tokenizers and a guide for training new-modality tokenizers are released. [CARD] — [GitHub apple/ml-4m](https://github.com/apple/ml-4m)
- MultiMAE (2022): masks across patches *and* modalities; needs only pseudo-labels for the extra modalities. [ABS] — [arXiv 2204.01678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01678)
- **AION-1** (NeurIPS 2025; astronomy; the closest template for image + spectrum + scalars):
  - Spectra: resampled to a fixed 8,704-point grid, encoded by a ConvNeXt-V2 to 273 latent positions, quantised with lookup-free quantisation into 1,024 codes.
  - Scalars: each value mapped through its empirical CDF and quantised into 1,024 equal-quantile bins, no learned parameters.
  - Images: ResNet autoencoder, 16× downsampling, finite-scalar quantisation (~4,096 codes).
  - Objective: 4M-style masked token prediction, 256 input / 128 output tokens; models of 300 M, 800 M, 3 B parameters trained on >200 M observations with 64–288 H100s for 1.5–3.5 days.
  - Results: frozen embeddings reach galaxy stellar-mass R² 0.96 with photometry+image+spectrum vs 0.85 for a supervised spectrum ConvNeXt; morphology 87.2 % vs 80.0 % (EfficientNet-B3) and 71.4 % (DINOv2). The text claims R² ≈ 0.5 with 10²–10³ labels where supervised baselines are near zero (figure only; not verified numerically). Cross-modal generation (e.g. low-resolution → high-resolution spectra) is shown qualitatively, and the authors caution that samples are not calibrated posteriors.
  - [FT, first 100k characters] — [arXiv 2510.17960](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17960). Code MIT; base (300 M) weights on Hugging Face, weight licence not stated on the repository page. [CARD] — [GitHub](https://github.com/PolymathicAI/AION)
- **TerraMind** (IBM–ESA, ICCV 2025): any-to-any over Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, DEM, land cover, NDVI, captions and coordinates; image-like modalities tokenised by FSQ autoencoders (16k codes), coordinates rounded to 0.25° and written as special text tokens; dual token- and pixel-level inputs; ~9 M samples; 32 A100s, about 9,216 GPU-hours for the base model (the paper states both 6 and 12 days). PANGAEA average mIoU 59.57 (L) / 58.35 (B) vs 57.58 for a U-Net trained from scratch, i.e. +0.8 to +2.0 points over no pretraining. No weather modality. Apache-2.0. [FT]; licence [CARD] — [arXiv 2504.11171](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11171v3); [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/ibm-esa-geospatial/TerraMind-1.0-base)
- **Galileo** (NASA Harvest, 2025): one ViT over space-time (S-1, S-2), space (elevation, land cover), time (ERA5 precipitation and temperature, TerraClimate, VIIRS) and static (population, location) inputs; time series and statics are *linearly projected* to tokens with channel-group, month and position embeddings; masked modelling in latent space with global + local losses. Trained on **127,155 instances**; ViT-Nano 0.8 M parameters / 200 GPU-hours, Tiny 5.3 M / 259, Base 85 M / 573. **Removing ERA5 from pretraining did not hurt** (MADOS 67.79 → 68.10, EuroSat 91.0 → 91.2); removing VIIRS or TerraClimate cost up to 10 points. [FT] — [arXiv 2502.09356](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09356v3). Repository licence MIT; weights are in the repository (nano) and on Hugging Face with no separate weight licence stated. [CARD] — [GitHub](https://github.com/nasaharvest/galileo)
- AnySat (arXiv Dec 2024, v3 May 2025): JEPA with scale-adaptive encoders trained on five multimodal datasets, 11 sensors. [ABS] — [arXiv 2412.14123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14123). Code MIT; weights on Hugging Face, weight licence not stated. [CARD] — [GitHub](https://github.com/gastruc/AnySat)
- DOFA (2024): one backbone for five sensors via a wavelength-conditioned dynamic hypernetwork. [ABS] — [arXiv 2403.15356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15356). Code MIT; weights on Hugging Face, weight licence not stated. [CARD] — [GitHub](https://github.com/zhu-xlab/DOFA)
- Prithvi WxC (2024): 2.3 B-parameter weather/climate model on 160 MERRA-2 variables with a mixed masked-reconstruction + forecasting objective. [ABS] — [arXiv 2409.13598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13598). Model-card licence CDLA-Permissive-2.0. [CARD] — [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/ibm-nasa-geospatial/Prithvi-WxC-1.0-2300M)
- Clay: code Apache-2.0; official documentation lists weights under OpenRAIL-M while several secondary sources say Apache — conflicting. [SNIP] — [Clay model card](https://huggingface.co/made-with-clay/Clay)
- MVAE (Wu & Goodman, 2018): a product-of-experts inference network with sub-sampled training lets one model infer from any subset of modalities. [ABS] — [arXiv 1802.05335](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05335)

### Inferences
- **A "sea-state any-to-any" model is buildable from existing pieces (proposal).** Tokens: (i) cached patch tokens from the project's frozen or continued DINOv3 / V-JEPA 2 encoders, passed through a linear projection (Galileo-style continuous inputs avoid training an image tokenizer); (ii) the 46-bin spectrum as 46 quantile-binned scalar tokens (AION recipe) or 8–12 band tokens; (iii) r1, r2, α1 (as sin/cos), α2 per band; (iv) wind speed, gust, direction, air–sea temperature difference, pressure; (v) ERA5 and WIS partition parameters; (vi) for shore sites, tide level and sun elevation. Train a 5–20 M-parameter transformer to predict random masked subsets with categorical cross-entropy on scalar tokens. At test time any missing block (swell partition, direction, wind) is read out conditionally on whatever is present.
- **What the published evidence does and does not support.** It supports: tokenising spectra and scalars this way; training at ~10⁵ instances with small models (Galileo); a single model serving any modality subset (MVAE, 4M). It does *not* support an expectation of large accuracy gains over a tuned supervised model at this scale: TerraMind beats a from-scratch U-Net by 1–2 mIoU points after ~9,000 GPU-hours, and Galileo's own ablation shows a coarse met modality contributing nothing to image tasks.
- **Main risk: the model will learn hindcast → buoy and ignore pixels.** With ERA5/WIS tokens present, most of the swell and total-Hs variance is explained without the image. Masking schedules must often hide all hindcast tokens, and evaluation must report image-only, hindcast-only and joint conditionals separately (see Q9).
- **Reuse verdict.** None of the EO or astronomy weights transfers to oblique sea-surface photographs or to wave spectra; licences also vary (4M weights restricted; AION weight licence unclear). Reuse the *recipes* (AION scalar/spectrum tokenisation, Galileo projections, 4M masking), which are unencumbered.

### Gaps
- No any-to-any or masked multimodal model over ocean-wave spectra plus imagery was found.
- data2vec and OmniMAE were not re-read this pass; they add no evidence on small scientific datasets that the items above do not already give.
- AION's low-data numbers are in a figure and were not extracted; only the first 100k characters of the AION, TerraMind and Galileo pages were read.
- Galileo, DOFA and AnySat publish code under MIT but state no separate licence for their weights; confirm before any reuse.

## Q4. Fusion under missing or unreliable modalities: what has beaten simple late fusion, and by how much

### Takeaway
No fusion design wins everywhere. In Earth-observation tests with sensors removed at inference, decision-level ensembles were by far the most robust but lost accuracy when the views were complementary; feature-level fusion was slightly better with all inputs and failed badly (negative R²) in regression when an input vanished. Learned quality gating has beaten late fusion by 7–13 AP points across clear and adverse weather in driving, with the largest margin in dense fog. Feature-wise conditioning (FiLM-style) has matched but not beaten a well-built late fusion. For this project the physically structured decision fusion already found (camera wind sea + hindcast swell, added as energies) is the right skeleton; the additions with evidence behind them are a quality gate, training with modality dropout, and reporting under each missing-input case.

### Cited Findings
- **Missing sensors in EO, regression and classification** (Mena et al., IGARSS 2024; four datasets; optical, radar, weather, static views). Values are accuracy (CropHarvest) or R² (LFMC fuel moisture, CYP crop yield): [FT-pdf] — [arXiv 2403.14297](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14297v2.pdf)

  | Method | CropHarvest-binary: all views → optical missing → only radar left | CYP yield R²: all → optical missing → weather missing | LFMC R²: all → optical missing |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Input concatenation (impute) | 0.847 → 0.717 → 0.554 | 0.823 → −1.435 → 0.710 | 0.717 → 0.060 |
  | Feature fusion, gated (ignore missing) | 0.849 → 0.797 → 0.700 | 0.823 → 0.193 → −7.534 | 0.737 → 0.138 |
  | Decision ensemble, average (ignore missing) | 0.828 → 0.792 → 0.740 | 0.768 → 0.593 → 0.823 | 0.312 → 0.243 |

  The authors conclude the ensemble is most robust, missing-view scenarios are considerably harder in regression than in classification, and the optical view is the most critical. Note the ensemble's cost: 0.768 vs 0.823 with all views on yield, and 0.312 vs 0.737 on fuel moisture, where views are complementary rather than redundant.
- A follow-up applies sensor dropout at the decision level and reports consistent gains with one sensor missing, one sensor available, and all sensors present (DSensD+, IEEE Access 2025). [SNIP] — [DFKI record](https://www.dfki.de/web/forschung/projekte-publikationen/publikation/16093)
- **Missing modality in multimodal transformers** (Ma et al., CVPR 2022): with only 30 % of test text present, ViLT falls below the image-only model on MM-IMDb (F1 31.2 vs 35.0) and Food-101 (65.9 vs 71.5 %); the better fusion depth (early vs late) is dataset-dependent; multi-task training plus a searched fusion policy restores MM-IMDb to 41.8 (30 % text) and 37.3 (10 % text). The paper's unimodal figures are not fully consistent between sections. [FT] — [ar5iv 2204.05454](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.05454)
- ModDrop (2015): randomly dropping whole modalities during fusion training keeps the classifier usable with any subset of inputs. [ABS] — [arXiv 1501.00102](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.00102)
- **Learned quality gating under a degraded sensor** (Bijelic et al., CVPR 2020; camera, lidar, radar, gated NIR; trained on clear weather only). Car detection AP, moderate difficulty: [FT; the paper's own "9.69 %" margin could not be matched to its table] — [ar5iv 1902.08913](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1902.08913)

  | Condition | Entropy-steered fusion | Late fusion | Early concat | Image only |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Clear | 85.57 | 78.02 | 76.62 | 75.75 |
  | Light fog | 87.99 | 78.65 | 78.24 | 78.52 |
  | Dense fog | 81.49 | 68.46 | 69.16 | 78.25 |
  | Snow/rain | 83.71 | 75.28 | 73.65 | 74.38 |

  In dense fog both ungated fusions fall 9–10 points *below* the camera alone; the gated model, which multiplies each sensor's features by a weight derived from its local entropy, stays above every single sensor.
- **Conditioning the image network on a co-input vs late fusion** (Geo-Aware Networks, ICCV-W 2019; iNaturalist with geolocation; 645k training images). Top-1: image only 70.1 %; late fusion ("post-processing") 79.0 %; customised additive feature modulation 78.2 %; off-the-shelf FiLM 72.5 %; Bayesian prior 69.4 %. On a small MobileNetV2, feature modulation was best (72.2 % vs 70.7 % late fusion vs 59.6 % image only). [FT] — [ar5iv 1906.01737](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.01737)
- Dynamic, uncertainty-aware late fusion has a generalisation-bound argument and reported robustness gains on noisy multimodal benchmarks (QMF, ICML 2023); no numbers in the abstract. [ABS] — [arXiv 2306.02050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02050)
- **Naively trained joint networks can lose to their best single input, and re-weighting the per-modality losses fixes it.** On Kinetics, late fusion by concatenation scored 1.2–3.8 points *below* the best single modality in all four combinations tested (e.g. audio + RGB 71.4 % vs RGB alone 72.6 %); Gradient-Blending, which weights each modality's loss by how fast it overfits, scored 0.5–4.2 points above the best single modality (Wang, Tran & Feiszli, CVPR 2020). [FT] — [ar5iv 1905.12681](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.12681)
- Mixture-of-experts fusion built for arbitrary missing-modality combinations (FuseMoE, NeurIPS 2024; Flex-MoE, 2024) reports gains of 7.6 % and 11.07 % over the strongest prior fusion baselines on two clinical datasets, but neither lists a plain late-fusion or decision-ensemble baseline, so they do not answer the late-fusion question. [SNIP] — [arXiv 2410.08245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08245); [arXiv 2402.03226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03226)
- Product-of-experts inference handles any subset of modalities with shared parameters (MVAE). [ABS] — [arXiv 1802.05335](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05335)
- From the earlier project notes (not repeated here): DAFT/FiLM gave only modest gains over concatenation in MRI+tabular; late fusion and concatenation are hard to beat across image+tabular benchmarks; a residual on an analytical spectral prior beat pure ML in-domain but lost to the analytical method under domain shift; Wang et al. 2026 fuse a learned wind-sea prior with an observed swell spectrum. [PROJ] — [heads_uncertainty_fusion.md](../Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones/heads_uncertainty_fusion.md)
- Project results: camera wind-sea height error 0.215 m vs ERA5 0.341 m, but ERA5 + wind + camera total Hs 0.258 m vs 0.259 m without the camera; a physics-aware "partition swap" was the first fusion to help. [PROJ] — [project report](../../reports/Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones.md); assignment context for the partition-swap outcome

### Inferences
- **The partition swap is a decision-level fusion of complementary experts, which is the case where plain ensemble averaging is weakest (Mena's LFMC row) and structured combination is needed.** Adding energies per partition (Hs² = wind-sea² + swell²) is the correct combination rule for complementary views and is automatically robust: with no camera, the wind-sea term falls back to the hindcast.
- **Three additions with evidence behind them (proposals):**
  1. *Quality gate.* A scalar g ∈ [0, 1] per image from cheap image statistics (entropy, brightness, sun elevation, horizon-detection confidence) or from the camera head's predicted variance; fused wind-sea energy = g·camera + (1 − g)·hindcast. The fog result says ungated fusion can drop below the better single sensor when one input degrades; the camera at dusk or in haze is exactly that case.
  2. *Precision weighting per partition (Gaussian product of experts).* If camera and hindcast each give a calibrated mean and variance for wind-sea energy, the optimal linear combination weights by inverse variance, provided their errors are uncorrelated. Both errors plausibly correlate with wind speed, so measure the error correlation on out-of-fold predictions before trusting the formula.
  3. *Modality dropout when training any joint model*, and a standing evaluation table with four columns: both inputs, camera only, hindcast only, camera degraded (night/fog subset).
- **Partition tokens.** Representing each hindcast partition (wind sea, swell 1, swell 2: height, period, direction) as a token that cross-attends to image tokens is a natural architecture, but nothing in the evidence above says cross-attention beats the energy-space decision rule; treat it as an ablation of proposal 1–3, not the main bet. The Geo-Aware result (off-the-shelf FiLM +2.4 points vs +8.9 for late fusion) is the cautionary number for conditioning the image encoder on wind or hindcast.
- **Residual on a physical prior** remains the safest joint model: predict the correction to hindcast wind-sea energy, shrink it by the gate.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head of gated, product-of-experts, cross-attention and FiLM fusion exists for imagery + reanalysis in geoscience (unchanged from the earlier notes).
- No study found of camera + hindcast fusion under camera degradation for any ocean variable.
- Mixture-of-experts fusion papers were seen only through search summaries and do not compare against late fusion.
- The inverse-variance statement is textbook estimation theory and is given here as reasoning, not as a cited empirical result.

## Q5. Self-supervised objectives that use physical structure instead of labels

### Takeaway
The best-supported label-free objective for this data is periodicity learning from video: methods built for camera heart-rate estimation learn a band-limited periodic signal from unlabeled clips and reach supervised accuracy, and their priors (finite bandwidth, sparse spectrum, frequency equivariance under speed change) transfer directly to gravity waves. Sea-state-aware contrastive positives (simultaneous views, weather-matched sites) have indirect support. Learning the delay between two sensors as a self-supervised target has precedents in audio and in a 2026 multi-sensor workshop paper, but none between an offshore instrument and a shore camera. Predicting time-exposure images from a single frame and tide/sun-geometry invariance have no precedent that was found; they are proposals.

### Cited Findings
- **SiNC** (Speth et al., CVPR 2023): learns the pulse waveform from unlabeled face video with three frequency-domain losses and no contrastive pairs: (i) penalise spectral power outside a physical band (0.66–3 Hz); (ii) penalise in-band power away from the spectral peak (sparsity); (iii) push the batch-summed spectrum towards uniform over the band (anti-collapse). Speed augmentation resamples clips by c ∈ [0.6, 1.4] and scales the band limits with c. Results (MAE, beats/min): UBFC 0.59 vs 0.55 supervised and 0.64 Contrast-Phys; PURE 0.61 vs 0.99 supervised; DDPM 5.87 vs 3.96 supervised and 9.70 Contrast-Phys. All three losses are needed (band loss alone 3.08; all three 0.59). Without augmentation PURE MAE was 33.9. Training on a dataset with no physiological labels at all worked (HKBU-MARs → UBFC 1.08), but failed on heavily compressed video (CelebV-HQ). Allowing a second harmonic in the sparsity loss hurt (PURE 3.29). Batch size 20. [FT] — [ar5iv 2303.07944](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.07944); code [GitHub](https://github.com/CVRL/SiNC-rPPG)
- SiNC+ (2024) applies the same losses to respiration by changing the band limits and adapts a model to one subject using that subject's unlabeled video. The authors present this as evidence that the method carries over to other band-limited, quasi-periodic signals. [ABS] — [arXiv 2404.13449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13449)
- **SimPer** (ICLR 2023 oral): contrastive learning where views are the same clip at different playback speeds (periodicity-variant) and the similarity measure is periodic. Frozen-feature frequency MAE vs best of SimCLR / MoCo v2 / BYOL / CVRL: SCAMPS 14.45 vs 26.15; UBFC 8.78 vs 11.75; PURE 13.97 vs 19.27; Countix 2.06 vs 2.81. Fine-tuned vs supervised: SCAMPS 3.27 vs 3.61; UBFC 4.24 vs 5.13; Countix 1.33 vs 1.50. With 5 % of labels: 4.02 vs 12.24 supervised. Training with a gap in the frequency range: unseen-frequency MAE 0.02 vs 1.74 supervised (synthetic RotatingDigits). **On the satellite land-surface-temperature forecasting task SimPer's frozen-feature result was level with other SSL (4.84 vs 4.88), so the advantage is not universal.** [FT-pdf] — [arXiv 2210.03115](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03115)
- RepNet (CVPR 2020): class-agnostic period estimation through a temporal self-similarity matrix, trained entirely on synthetic repetitions made by looping unlabeled clips. Later counters report lower error on RepCount (ESCounts, ACCV 2024: MAE 0.38 → 0.21) but rely on exemplars or language models. [SNIP] — [arXiv 2006.15418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15418); [arXiv 2403.18074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18074v2)
- **Synchronisation as a pretext** (nearest analogue to buoy–camera time-lag): learning whether audio and video are in sync, with curriculum-chosen negatives and a contrastive loss, improved action recognition by +19.9 points on UCF101 and +17.7 on HMDB51 over training from scratch (Korbar, Tran & Torresani, 2018). [ABS] — [arXiv 1807.00230](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.00230)
- **Inter-sensor delay as a self-supervised target** exists outside oceanography. Chen, Fouhey & Owens (ECCV 2022) learn the time delay between two microphones from unlabeled stereo audio with a cycle-consistent contrastive random walk and report parity with supervised methods on in-the-wild recordings. LODES (NeurIPS 2026 workshop on physical world models, single author) learns a distribution over offsets between sensor streams without timing labels, recovers a directed delay graph, matches or improves on synchrony-assuming baselines on seven benchmarks, and finds that re-aligning a desynchronised stream at the recovered offset raises frozen-probe accuracy. Neither abstract gives effect sizes. [ABS] — [arXiv 2204.12489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12489); [NeurIPS 2026 virtual](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/165829)
- **Simultaneous multi-view positives**: Time-Contrastive Networks pull together frames of the same instant from different cameras and push apart temporally nearby frames from the same camera. [ABS] — [arXiv 1704.06888](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06888)
- **Metadata-defined positives**: y-Aware InfoNCE (MICCAI 2021) weights positive pairs by an RBF kernel on a continuous variable known for every sample (age), pretraining on 10,420 brain scans from 74 sites. Fine-tuned AUC with 100 labelled subjects, schizophrenia: 76.33 vs 73.09 from scratch, 70.80 SimCLR, 72.90 supervised age-prediction pretraining; with 500: 88.11 vs 85.92, 86.35, 87.75. Alzheimer's with 300: 96.84 vs 93.68, 94.26, 96.37. Gains over the best of those three alternatives are 0.4–3.2 AUC points, largest at the smallest label count; plain SimCLR was *worse than training from scratch* in four of the six settings; and a supervised age-prediction variant with data augmentation matched or beat y-Aware in two of six. Rank-N-Contrast orders the embedding by a continuous variable (numbers under Q1). [FT] — [ar5iv 2106.08808](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.08808); [RnC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.01189)
- **Positives defined by a known physical invariance, quantified** (Geography-Aware SSL, ICCV 2021): using images of the same place at a different time as MoCo-v2 positives, instead of two augmentations of one image, raised fMoW frozen-feature top-1 from 60.69 to 68.32 % and fine-tuned top-1 from 64.34 to 71.55 % (ImageNet-supervised initialisation 69.07 %). Transfer gains were smaller: xView AP50 15.45 → 17.65, SpaceNet mIoU 78.05 → 78.48, NAIP 55.18 → 57.10. A geolocation-prediction pretext alone gave +3.4 points frozen; combining both was slightly below temporal positives alone on fMoW. [FT] — [ar5iv 2011.09980](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.09980)
- Wave-specific check: no self-supervised or contrastive pretraining on coastal or nearshore video for wave period or height was found. The June 2026 coastal peak-period paper relies on multi-stage sim-to-real transfer and physics-informed regularisation, i.e. supervised learning with a synthetic source domain. [SNIP] — [arXiv 2606.13302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13302)
- Equivariance to known transformations: predicting which transformation was applied, alongside an invariance loss, improved SimCLR to 72.5 % ImageNet linear probe (E-SSL, ICLR 2022). [ABS] — [arXiv 2111.00899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00899)
- Project results: continued DINOv3 self-supervised training on buoy views improved a frozen probe on validation (0.642 → 0.582 m); hand-built horizon roll/pitch features added to the image model did not improve Hs. [PROJ] — assignment context; [experiment log](../../docs/experiments/ndbc_buoycam_sea_state.md)
- Sampling limits from the earlier report: a 16-s window holds one to three waves; period survives shoaling while height does not. [PROJ] — [project report](../../reports/Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones.md)

### Inferences
- **Periodicity SSL for shore video (proposal; best supported).** Apply SiNC's three losses to a small spatio-temporal network that outputs a 1-D signal from a patch of sea, with the band set to the gravity-wave range (about 0.04–0.5 Hz, i.e. 2–25 s), plus SimPer-style speed augmentation. Differences from heart rate that need design choices: (i) sea states are often bimodal (sea + swell), so the sparsity loss must allow two peaks or be applied per sub-band — SiNC found that admitting a second harmonic hurt, so this is the main technical risk; (ii) the band is slow, so windows must be minutes long (the ten-minute clips at 20 fps give 12,000 frames; 2–4 fps is enough); (iii) H.264 compression destroyed the pulse signal in one SiNC dataset, but wave motion is a large-amplitude signal, so this should matter less. SimPer's near-null on the land-surface-temperature task is the warning that the advantage is task-dependent.
- **Why it is attractive here:** it needs no buoy, so every unlabelled clip from every camera trains it; its output spectrum can be compared with CDIP's peak period without any fitted mapping (period is conserved to the shore); and the physics front end already planned (pixel-intensity spectrum) is the natural baseline it must beat.
- **Sea-state-aware contrastive continuation (proposal).** The project's continued DINOv3 pretraining used generic augment-invariance. Three physically motivated changes: (a) positives = the six views of one buoy strip, or the two cameras at one shore site within the same ten minutes (same sea, different view; TCN logic); (b) negatives drawn from the *same camera* on other days, which removes camera identity, mounting and scenery as a way to solve the task — the direct attack on unseen-site transfer; (c) soft positives across sites weighted by a kernel on the hindcast state (wind speed, wind-sea Hs, swell Hs, Tp), which exists for every camera with or without a buoy (y-Aware logic). Evidence: positives that encode a true invariance were worth +7.6 points of frozen-feature accuracy in satellite imagery, and metadata kernels 0.4–3.2 AUC points in MRI where generic SimCLR often did worse than no pretraining; gains for regression transfer to unseen sites are untested. Note the direction is reversed relative to land cover: there the invariance is to time at a fixed place, whereas for sea state it is to viewpoint at a fixed time, and time at a fixed camera is the *discriminative* axis, hence (b).
- **Still → time-exposure / brightest prediction (proposal; no precedent found).** A time-exposure image is the ten-minute mean and the brightest image the per-pixel maximum; bright bands mark where waves break, which coastal practice uses as a dissipation proxy (see Q6). Predicting them from one frame forces the encoder to infer the wave field's statistics from its instantaneous texture. ~108k triplets exist with no labels needed. Two uses: as a dense pretext for the still encoder, and, following TerraMind's result, as a generated intermediate fed back to the regressor at sites that archive only stills.
- **Buoy-to-shore travel time (proposal; delay learning has precedents for other sensor pairs, none for buoy and camera).** Deep-water group speed is c_g = gT/4π: about 6.2 m/s at 8 s, 9.4 m/s at 12 s, 12.5 m/s at 16 s, so energy takes roughly 16–52 min (12 s) or 24–77 min (8 s) to cover 9–29 km, longer once it shoals (own arithmetic from linear theory). A synchronisation pretext would ask the model which lagged buoy record matches a clip. Weakness: CDIP records are 30-minute averages and sea states decorrelate over hours, so the lag is identifiable mainly at swell arrivals. Cheapest form needs no training: cross-correlate the camera's existing per-band predictions with buoy band energy across lags and check that the best lag grows with 1/c_g. If it does, lag-corrected labels are a free improvement to every shore experiment; the LODES abstract reports the same mechanism, a probe improving once a stream is re-aligned at the learned offset.
- **Tide and sun-geometry invariance (proposal).** Tide moves the breaker line across the image and sun elevation/azimuth changes glitter; both are known from ephemerides. Either condition on them (inputs) or decorrelate from them (adversarial or residualised features). Conditioning is safer: tide genuinely changes nearshore breaking for a given offshore sea.
- **Camera-motion equivariance** (predict the roll/pitch change between views) has E-SSL-type support in general, but the project's own null result for horizon features on Hs makes it low priority.

### Gaps
- No paper found applying SiNC, SimPer or RepNet-style periodicity learning to ocean waves.
- No paper found on travel-time or time-lag learning between an offshore instrument and a shore camera; the two delay-learning precedents were read at abstract level and give no effect sizes.
- No paper found predicting time-exposure or brightest images from single frames as a learning signal.
- SeCo's numbers were not retrieved (Geography-Aware and y-Aware were read in the second pass).
- Arrow-of-time and temporal-order pretext papers were not re-read; the project already plans a shuffled-frame control, which answers the practical question.

## Q6. The camera as a virtual sensor for another modality, and sensor translation

### Takeaway
Cameras have been turned into validated point sensors for wind speed, air temperature, rainfall and precipitation type, typically with errors that are useful but well short of the instrument and with poor transfer to new scenes unless the features are normalised per scene. For the sea surface, SAR images are routinely mapped to wave height, wind speed and near-surface air temperature by linear probes on self-supervised features. Any-to-any Earth-observation models now translate between sensors, but their authors describe the outputs as plausible renderings, not calibrated measurements, and the optical↔SAR translation literature states fundamental limits.

### Cited Findings
- **Wind speed from video** (Cardona, Howland & Dabiri, "Seeing the Wind", NeurIPS 2019): ResNet-18 + LSTM on two-second clips of a flag and a tree at one site, labelled by a 10-m cup anemometer. RMSE in the measurable range (0.75–11 m/s): 1.37 m/s (flag validation), 1.47 (tree), 1.85 on an identical flag elsewhere on the site, 1.82 on a smaller flag in a wind tunnel. **Subtracting each clip's temporal mean feature was what made transfer work: without it the errors were 3.10 and 9.61 m/s** on the two test sets. The authors say the model may be partly overfitted to the specific flag and tree. [FT] — [ar5iv 1905.13290](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1905.13290)
- **Air temperature from webcams**: SkyFinder (35,417 images, 44 cameras, −20 to 49 °C); supervised L1 MAE 2.95 °C, 2.86 °C with rank-contrastive pretraining; random split across the same cameras. [FT] — [RnC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.01189). A multi-task recurrent network on 53,378 images from 9 webcams estimates four properties with RMSE (paper's units) of 2.65 for temperature, 10.28 humidity, 1.42 visibility and 3.90 wind speed, against 4.47 for an earlier frequency-decomposition temperature method; train and test images come from the same cameras (90/10 per scene), and per-camera temperature RMSE ranges from 1.73 to 3.51 (Chu, Liang & Ho, Atmosphere 2021). [FT-pdf] — [Atmosphere 12, 584 (PDF)](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/atmosphere/atmosphere-12-00584/article_deploy/atmosphere-12-00584.pdf)
- **Rainfall from surveillance cameras**: a 2025 hybrid deep-learning framework trained on >60 hours of video reports R² up to 0.95 against rain gauges (press summary of Environmental Science and Ecotechnology, DOI 10.1016/j.ese.2025.100562). [SNIP] — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1083694)
- **Precipitation type from video**: MobileNetV2 + GRU, accuracy 0.9301 against a manually corrected 2-D video disdrometer (Atmos. Meas. Tech., Oct 2025). [SNIP] — [AMT 18, 5457](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/18/5457/2025/amt-18-5457-2025.pdf)
- **Visibility**: a 2025 Knowledge-Based Systems paper fuses fixed-camera RGB with tabular weather data on 1,000 manually labelled images; no instrument validation found. [SNIP] — [repository copy](https://dk.upce.cz/bitstreams/c9b00ace-e263-4396-95d1-ae9e99a580b1/download)
- **Sea surface from SAR**: frozen WV-Net features + linear head give Hs RMSE 0.50 m (altimeter labels) and RMSE 0.90 °C on an ERA5-derived near-surface air-temperature target (0.97 with ImageNet features); OceanSAR-1 reports wind speed RMSE 1.37–1.43 m/s by linear probe. [FT]; [CARD] — [WV-Net](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18765v1); [OceanSAR-1](https://huggingface.co/galeio-research/OceanSAR-1)
- **Sea-surface roughness from optical images**: Cox & Munk (1954) derived slope statistics from photographs of sun glitter and found mean-square slope rising roughly linearly with wind speed. [SNIP] — [JOSA 1954](https://www.doi.org/10.1364/JOSA.44.000838). No learned camera → mean-square-slope or camera → radar-backscatter model was found.
- Mean-square slope and Stokes drift as learned targets from *wind history* (not imagery): a June 2026 diffusion model samples global sea state from five days of wind forcing, trained on a 30-year WAVEWATCH III hindcast; abstract gives no skill numbers. [ABS] — [arXiv 2606.26389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26389)
- **Time-exposure images as a breaking-height sensor** (non-learned): Andriolo, Mendes & Taborda (Remote Sensing 2020) use the cross-shore length of the time-averaged foam band. With the beach profile known, the estimate differs from *modelled* breaking height (1–3 m) by an RMSE of 0.2 m at one Portuguese site over two days. A profile-free variant gives a normalised RMSE of 18 % on average over about 900 records at five sites (breaking heights 0.1–3.8 m), checked against a mix of modelled and measured wave sources. [FT-pdf, abstract and tables] — [Remote Sensing 12, 204 (PDF)](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-12-00204/article_deploy/remotesensing-12-00204.pdf)
- **Optical↔SAR translation has stated limits.** A 2024 survey of generative models for SAR calls optical→SAR the harder, ill-posed direction. Fuentes Reyes et al. (Remote Sensing 2019) analyse limits of SAR→optical translation that a network cannot compensate (different physical content, geometry-dependent distortion, speckle) and judge outputs by expert review and by a downstream task, road extraction. A change-detection study found that the more useful translation direction varied from case to case. [SNIP] — [arXiv 2411.05027](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.05027); [DLR record](https://elib.dlr.de/129009)
- **Sensor translation**: TerraMind generates any of its modalities from any other (e.g. S-1 → S-2, → land cover) and its model card presents the outputs as the model's internal renderings, not reconstructions; using a generated modality as an extra input gives +1.4 to +2.6 mIoU (numbers under Q2). AION-1 generates high-resolution spectra from low-resolution ones qualitatively and warns that samples are not calibrated posteriors. [FT]; [CARD] — [TerraMind](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11171v3); [model card](https://huggingface.co/ibm-esa-geospatial/TerraMind-1.0-base); [AION-1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17960)
- Privileged optical → SAR-only representation transfer (Crossfire) is under Q2.

### Inferences
- **How these were validated matters more than the headline error.** SkyFinder and the Chu et al. webcam-weather results split within cameras, and the time-exposure breaking-height method was checked mostly against a wave model; Seeing the Wind is the one that tests a new object and shows a 2–5× error increase without per-clip normalisation. Virtual-sensor claims for the project's cameras should be stated at unseen buoys/cameras only.
- **Virtual anemometer (proposal).** Buoy strips have same-hull wind speed, gust and direction; a camera → wind model is trainable now on 41k strips and testable at unseen buoys against ERA5 10-m wind. It is both a product (wind where no anemometer exists) and the auxiliary target Q2 recommends. Published camera-wind error on a flag is ~1.4–1.9 m/s; SAR-probe wind error is ~1.4 m/s; those are the external yardsticks, with ERA5's own error at the buoys as the baseline to beat.
- **Virtual slope / roughness sensor.** The companion report already recommends a frequency-limited slope target; the evidence here adds only that no learned optical precedent was found, so it would be new, and that Cox–Munk's wind–slope relation is the sanity check.
- **Sensor translation as measurement is not supported.** Use generated modalities (e.g. a predicted time-exposure image, Q5) as *features* whose value is tested by downstream error, never as stand-ins for the instrument.

### Gaps
- Optical↔SAR translation is covered from search summaries only; camera → radar/lidar translation and learned camera → backscatter models were not found.
- No camera-based visibility study validated against a transmissometer or forward-scatter sensor was found.
- Chu et al. 2021 gives its RMSE table without units in the portion read; Andriolo et al. 2020 validates largely against modelled, not measured, breaking heights.
- The rainfall figure is from a press release, not the paper.

## Q7. Multimodal learning with language: captions from buoy records, vision–language fine-tuning, and whether language encodes continuous quantities better than plain regression

### Takeaway
The evidence says no: language-aligned models estimate continuous quantities worse through text than a linear head does on the same embedding, and the best "numeric CLIP" variants only reach parity with specialised ordinal regressors. Language adds value for retrieval and zero-shot categorical queries, not for the regression error. Captions generated from buoy numbers contain no information beyond the numbers.

### Cited Findings
- **Text-prototype regression vs linear probe on the same encoder** (EarthAlign/MeteoLIP, Oct 2026): wind estimation R² 60.2–62.3 through value-conditioned text prototypes vs 71.3–71.9 by linear probe on the frozen encoder; pressure 67.6–73.4 vs 76.4–77.4. The paper's CLIP/SigLIP baselines through text: R² 5.3 and −11.6 for wind. The meteorology-specific alignment improved the *linear-probe* R² by about 1–3 points over those baselines (68.1 → 71.3; 70.5 → 71.9); the baselines' exact setup was not verified. Retrieval improved far more (R@1 20–32 → 59–66). No ablation of numeric content in captions. [FT] — [arXiv 2610.14624](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14624v1)
- **Why CLIP is weak at numbers** (NumCLIP, ECCV 2024): captions containing specific numbers are rare in pretraining and the contrastive loss aligns whole sentences, not magnitudes; zero-shot CLIP age MAE 6.09 on MORPH II. Their fix (coarse language bins + fine regressor + rank regulariser) reaches 2.08, against 2.13 for the best non-CLIP method listed (MWR) and 2.13 for L2RCLIP, 2.32 OrdinalCLIP, 2.39 CoOp. **No plain regression head on the same CLIP encoder is reported.** The headline "10 %" gain on image dating is against a weaker baseline; against the strongest CLIP baseline it is 2.4 points. Few-shot (1–2 samples per class) is where the language prior helps most (MAE 3.61 vs 4.54). [FT] — [arXiv 2408.03574](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03574v1)
- **Zero-shot regression through text bins is far behind supervision**: DepthCLIP maps image patches to a handful of distance words tied to depth bins; on NYU Depth v2 it reaches δ<1.25 of 0.394 and RMSE 1.167 m, against 0.92–0.95 and 0.27–0.34 m for supervised models (random lower bound 0.140). [FT] — [ar5iv 2207.01077](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.01077)
- OrdinalCLIP (NeurIPS 2022) learns rank-ordered prompt embeddings; MORPH II MAE 2.32 vs 2.39 CoOp. OrdinalCLIP reports zero-shot CLIP at 14.45, NumCLIP at 6.09 — different setups. [SNIP] — [arXiv 2206.02338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02338)
- Label-space contrastive learning *without* language (Rank-N-Contrast) gives comparable or larger gains over L1 (Q1). [FT] — [RnC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.01189)
- Building image–text pairs from structured annotations is established: RemoteCLIP converts boxes and masks to captions for a 12× larger corpus and gains up to 6.39 % mean zero-shot accuracy over CLIP on 12 datasets. [ABS] — [arXiv 2306.11029](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11029). RS5M/GeoRSCLIP builds 5 M pairs by filtering public pair datasets and by captioning label-only datasets with a pretrained vision–language model, and reports 3–20 % zero-shot classification gains over its baselines. [SNIP of abstract] — [arXiv 2306.11300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11300)
- Text as a third modality can help or hurt alignment of the other two: +0.045 Recall@100 when all encoders were fine-tuned, −0.024 with the audio–text model frozen (GeoCLAP). [FT] — [GeoCLAP](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.10667)
- IMU2CLIP shows the interface value: free-text queries retrieve sensor windows (text → IMU R@10 28.8, MRR 0.143, close to text → video 33.7 / 0.168 on the same test set). [FT-pdf] — [arXiv 2210.14395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.14395)

### Inferences
- **Do not expect numeric captions to lower RMSE.** A caption such as "wind sea 1.2 m at 5 s, swell 0.8 m at 12 s from 110°" is a lossy re-encoding of the label vector; EarthAlign's 10-point R² deficit of text-prototype regression against a linear probe, and NumCLIP's parity-not-superiority, are the two quantitative reasons. If a label-structured embedding is wanted, Rank-N-Contrast or the image↔spectrum alignment of Q1 does it without a text encoder.
- **Where language is worth a small experiment (proposals):** (a) retrieval for people — "show me days like this" works equally from an image↔spectrum space (query by image, by spectrum, or by numbers) and needs no text; add text only if natural-language queries matter to users; (b) zero-shot *categorical* attributes that the buoy does not record and that could explain camera errors (fog, glare, rain on lens, whitecaps, sea ice), using an off-the-shelf SigLIP/CLIP on the stills as a quality-flag generator feeding the gate in Q4; (c) marine forecast text and surf reports as *independent* weak labels would add information only if they come from human observers; forecast text derived from the same models as ERA5/WIS adds nothing.
- Human surf reports and observer logs are not among the project's listed data; using them would need a new, licensed source.

### Gaps
- No study found that fine-tunes a vision–language model on sea-state imagery with captions, numeric or otherwise.
- "RegCLIP" was not found in two searches. Remote-sensing vision–language results are abstract- or snippet-level.
- Apart from EarthAlign's table, no head-to-head of text-prompt regression against a linear head on the same frozen encoder was found; the CLIP depth-estimation follow-ups add trained regression heads on top of text-derived context and do not report a pure linear-probe baseline [SNIP] — [arXiv 2604.01118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01118).

## Q8. Sequence and context methods: in-context or retrieval-augmented regression, test-time adaptation, domain generalisation, few-shot site calibration

### Takeaway
Purpose-built domain-generalisation algorithms have not beaten plain training on standard benchmarks, but methods that look at *unlabeled data from the new environment* have: a context of 25 unlabeled samples raised unseen-environment accuracy by 2–22 points over plain training across four benchmarks in one line of work, and a regression-specific test-time method improved R² where classification-style adaptation diverged. Per-scene feature normalisation was what made a camera wind sensor transfer. In hydrology, one model with site descriptors outperformed per-site calibrated models at sites it never saw. These are the precedents closest to "new buoy, new camera, new year", and their cheapest versions run on cached embeddings.

### Cited Findings
- Domain generalisation baselines: under controlled model selection, plain empirical risk minimisation was state of the art on every dataset (DomainBed; 7 datasets, 9 algorithms). [ABS] — [arXiv 2007.01434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01434). On WILDS's 10 real-world shifts, the out-of-distribution gap persists for models trained with existing shift-robust methods. [ABS] — [arXiv 2012.07421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07421)
- **Fixed cameras at new locations, quantified** (Terra Incognita, 2018; 10 training locations, 9 held out): classification error 19.06 % at seen locations vs 41.04 % at unseen ones on full images, 8.14 % vs 19.56 % on cropped animals; detection mAP 77.10 vs 70.17. Error roughly doubles at an unseen camera even with the background cropped away. [FT] — [ar5iv 1807.04975](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1807.04975)
- Training an encoder *with* context so that it adapts without gradient steps: context-aware multimodal pretraining reports up to four-fold better test-time sample efficiency across 21 tasks and average few-shot gains above 5 %, with zero-shot performance retained, and finds simple metric-based, training-free adaptation better than optimisation-based schemes (Roth et al., arXiv Nov 2024). [ABS] — [arXiv 2411.15099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15099)
- Retrieval as the decision rule: a visual memory that classifies by nearest-neighbour search over a frozen embedding lets data be added or removed without retraining (Geirhos et al., ICML 2025; no numbers in the abstract). Retrieval-augmented *regression* was found only for tabular data (NN-kNN, IJCAI 2026; TabR; RowNet), and a 2026 preprint on cache-based CLIP adaptation cautions that an embedding good for image–text matching may give poor image–image neighbourhoods under shift. No study of retrieval-augmented vision regression under site shift was found. [ABS]; [SNIP] — [arXiv 2408.08172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08172); [IJCAI 2026](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/93); [arXiv 2609.23409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23409)
- Camera-as-gauge precedent for new sites: river-stage regression from existing webcams has been trained per site against that site's stream gauge (Vanden Boomen et al., Water Resources Research 2021; sites in Iowa, Ohio and Wisconsin), and a 2025 preprint doing the same from water-surface pixel fractions drew a referee objection that it needs gauge data at every new location. No accuracy-versus-labels curve at a new site was found. [SNIP] — [UW–Milwaukee record](https://minds.wisc.edu/items/4ab2cb38-27cb-4b50-aa66-b81e75687463/full); [EGUsphere 2025-2902](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2025/egusphere-2025-2902/)
- Instrument-to-instrument calibration transfer (spectroscopy, gas sensors) is the mature form of few-shot site calibration: classical methods need standard samples measured on both instruments, model updating adds a few samples from the new condition, and a 2023 gas-sensor comparison reports that fine-tuning a network on few transfer samples can beat the classical methods. No rule for the number of transfer samples was found. [SNIP] — [Spectroscopy Online 2025](https://www.spectroscopyonline.com/view/universal-calibration-can-models-travel-successfully-across-instruments-); [Atmosphere 14, 1123 (repository copy)](https://scidok.sulb.uni-saarland.de/bitstream/20.500.11880/36092/1/atmosphere-14-01123.pdf)
- **Unlabeled context from the test environment** (In-Context Risk Minimization, ICLR 2024): a sequence model sees previous unlabeled inputs from the same environment. Average out-of-distribution accuracy with 0 → 25 context samples: FEMNIST 78.7 → 87.2 (ERM 79.3); Rotated MNIST 93.6 → 96.1 (ERM 94.2); worst-environment FEMNIST 59.8 → 69.3 (ERM 59.0). WILDS Camelyon17 92.0 with *no* context vs ERM 68.6, i.e. training with context improved the featurizer itself. Gains saturate by 25–50 samples. Classification only. [FT-pdf] — [arXiv 2309.09888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.09888); code [GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/ICRM)
- **Test-time adaptation for regression** (Adachi et al., ICLR 2025; source-free, unlabeled target): aligning target features to the source within the principal subspace the regressor actually uses. R²: SVHN→MNIST 0.406 → 0.511 (oracle 0.874); California Housing non-coastal → coastal 0.605 → 0.639 (oracle 0.729); UTKFace corruptions −0.678 → 0.731; Biwi 0.706 → 0.778. Classification-derived methods often made things worse: batch-norm adaptation 0.605 → 0.318 on housing; entropy/variance minimisation diverged (R² −685). [FT] — [arXiv 2410.03263](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03263v2); code [GitHub](https://github.com/kzkadc/regression-tta)
- **Per-scene normalisation in a camera sensor**: subtracting each clip's mean feature cut wind-speed RMSE on unseen objects from 3.10 → 1.85 m/s and 9.61 → 1.82 m/s (Seeing the Wind). [FT] — [ar5iv 1905.13290](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1905.13290)
- **Unseen sites in geoscience**: an LSTM trained on other basins with static catchment attributes reached median Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency 0.69 across 531 basins it was never trained on, against 0.64 for a conceptual model calibrated *at each basin* and 0.58 for the US National Water Model (Kratzert et al., Water Resources Research 2019). [ABS] — [JKU record](https://research.jku.at/en/publications/toward-improved-predictions-in-ungauged-basins-exploiting-the-pow/)
- Caveat on site descriptors: a 2026 preprint finds most CAMELS static attributes can be replaced by simple statistics of the dynamic inputs, and architectural refinements give only modest gains in pseudo-ungauged cross-validation. [SNIP] — [EGUsphere 2026-1965](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2026-1965/egusphere-2026-1965.pdf)
- Fine-tuning for a site without its labels: Regionalised Fine-Tuning adapts a continental LSTM to each ungauged catchment using nearby gauged catchments, improving efficiency in >66 % of 218 Australian catchments; head-only fine-tuning beat full fine-tuning. [SNIP] — [EGUsphere 2026-2950](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2026-2950/)
- Personalisation by unlabeled video: SiNC+ fine-tunes on one subject's unlabeled clips with its label-free losses. [ABS] — [arXiv 2404.13449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13449)
- Supervised fine-tuning can reduce out-of-distribution accuracy relative to frozen features (FINO: camera traps 51.4 → 43.4), and tabular in-context models assume context rows are exchangeable with the query. [PROJ] — [project report](../../reports/Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones.md); [heads_uncertainty_fusion.md](../Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones/heads_uncertainty_fusion.md)
- Rank-N-Contrast transfer: frozen-encoder linear probe on a new dataset MAE 12.25 → 11.12 and unseen-target MAE 18.40 → 15.92. [FT] — [RnC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.01189)

### Inferences
- **Skip domain-generalisation algorithms (IRM, CORAL-type penalties); test adaptation instead.** DomainBed and WILDS say the former rarely pays; ICRM, the regression subspace method and the wind-camera normalisation say the latter can.
- **A three-rung ladder, all on cached embeddings (proposals):**
  1. *Unlabeled camera context.* For each camera (buoy or shore view), standardise embeddings by that camera's own mean and covariance computed from its unlabeled archive, or align them to the training distribution in the regressor's principal subspace (Adachi). Then apply the frozen head. This uses no labels from the new site and is legitimate at deployment. Risk: a camera's mean embedding also encodes its sea climate, so centring removes real signal at sites that are persistently rough or calm; subtract a mean fitted on a climatology-matched subset (e.g. conditioned on hindcast state) to protect against that.
  2. *Context as input.* Give the head a set of k other embeddings from the same camera (ICRM/set-transformer style) so it can infer "what this camera's calm and rough look like". Train by sampling context from the same training buoy.
  3. *Few-shot labelled calibration.* With k labelled hours at a new buoy, fit a per-site affine correction on log-energy (head-only, per the hydrology result) and plot error against k. Where no instrument exists (most shore cameras), calibrate against the hindcast's long-run distribution instead (quantile mapping of camera wind-sea to hindcast wind-sea), which corrects site bias without claiming to correct the hindcast.
- **Site descriptors that transfer** (the Kratzert lesson): camera height above water, lens field of view, view azimuth, hull type, water depth, distance to coast, fetch by direction. The earlier notes ruled out buoy-identity embeddings; descriptors are the generalisable replacement, with the 2026 caveat that they may add little beyond what the images' own statistics provide.
- **Retrieval-augmented regression**: nearest labelled neighbours within the same camera only exist after rung 3; across cameras, retrieval in a sea-state-ordered embedding (Q1, Q5) is a kNN regressor whose value is interpretability and spectra-by-example rather than a demonstrated accuracy gain.
- The grouped cross-validation over 43 buoy groups already planned is the right harness: each fold's held-out buoys play "new site" for all three rungs.

### Gaps
- No published few-shot site-calibration curve for a camera-based ocean or coastal measurement was found.
- Retrieval-augmented regression in vision under site shift: searched in the second pass, none found (hits were classification or tabular).
- ICRM has no regression experiments; the regression subspace method has no multi-site sensor experiments.
- The river-camera and calibration-transfer items are from search summaries; their papers were not opened.

## Q9. Evaluation pitfalls specific to multimodal claims, and the ablations reviewers expect

### Takeaway
Three failure patterns recur in the literature: the multimodal model is no better (or worse) than its best single input; it scores well while ignoring one modality; and its validation score is inflated by spatial or temporal dependence between training and test samples. For this project the first is already observed (hindcast + wind explains total Hs and the camera adds 0.001 m), so every multimodal claim has to be made against the co-input-only model and on the partition the camera can affect.

### Cited Findings
- Joint multimodal networks can underperform the best unimodal network because modalities overfit at different rates: on Kinetics, naive audio + RGB scored 71.4 % and audio + RGB + flow 70.0 % against 72.6 % for RGB alone; re-weighting the modality losses by their overfitting behaviour lifted these to 74.7 % and 74.9 % (Wang, Tran & Feiszli, CVPR 2020). [FT] — [ar5iv 1905.12681](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.12681)
- "Greedy" multimodal learning: networks rely on one modality and under-fit the others; the *conditional utilization rate* (accuracy gain from adding modality B given A) quantifies it (Wu et al., 2022; venue not shown on the arXiv page). Gradient modulation by per-modality contribution is one remedy (Peng et al., CVPR 2022 oral). [ABS] — [arXiv 2202.05306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05306); [arXiv 2203.15332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15332)
- Permutation test for modality use: the *perceptual score* permutes one modality across samples and measures the drop; applied to VQA it showed that more accurate models tended to use the image *less* (Gat et al., NeurIPS 2021). A 2026 ICLR study applying it to 23 VQA benchmarks found that larger models exploit unimodal shortcuts more. [SNIP] — [arXiv 2110.14375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14375); [arXiv 2509.23499](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.23499v2)
- Redundancy / uniqueness / synergy: partial information decomposition estimators quantify what each modality contributes uniquely and jointly to a target (Liang et al., NeurIPS 2023). [ABS] — [arXiv 2302.12247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12247)
- Model-agnostic conditional-independence test: the conditional predictive impact uses knockoff copies of a feature group to test whether it adds predictive value given the rest, with controlled type-I error; R package `cpi` (Watson & Wright; arXiv 2019, journal version not checked). [ABS] — [arXiv 1901.09917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09917)
- Missing-modality stress: a multimodal transformer fell below its unimodal counterpart when 70 % of test text was removed (Q4). [FT] — [ar5iv 2204.05454](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.05454)
- A teacher that is more accurate thanks to a second modality does not imply a better student (Q2). [FT] — [ar5iv 2206.06487](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.06487)
- Location alone is a strong predictor: raw coordinates give R² 0.82 for air temperature and pretrained location embeddings 0.91, with no image at all (SatCLIP). [FT] — [arXiv 2311.17179](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17179v3)
- Spatial dependence inflates scores: random hold-out overestimated CNN performance by up to 28 % relative to spatial block cross-validation in drone-based tree-species segmentation; the optimism grew as training samples shrank, and autocorrelation was much higher within one acquisition than between acquisitions (Kattenborn et al., ISPRS Open J. 2022) [ABS]. Secondary sources summarise Ploton et al. (Nature Communications 2020) the same way: random validation suggested a forest-biomass model explained over half the variance, spatial validation showed almost no predictive power [SNIP; primary not opened]. A 2026 preprint adds that blocking is necessary but not sufficient, that buffering can reverse a study's conclusion, and that spatial validation can be over-pessimistic when the use case is interpolation. [SNIP] — [KIT record](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000156879); [arXiv 2609.28194](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28194)
- Camera-specific overfitting: the authors of a wind-from-video model judged it partly overfitted to its one flag and one tree (Seeing the Wind); classification error roughly doubles at camera-trap locations absent from training (19.06 → 41.04 %; [Terra Incognita](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1807.04975), [FT]); webcam-temperature benchmarks split within cameras (SkyFinder as used by RnC). [FT] — [ar5iv 1905.13290](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1905.13290); [RnC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.01189)
- Nearly all SAR spectral-inversion work validates against model spectra, not instruments. [PROJ] — [heads_uncertainty_fusion.md](../Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones/heads_uncertainty_fusion.md)
- Project evidence of the first pitfall: ERA5 + wind + camera 0.258 m vs 0.259 m without the camera for total Hs, while the camera cuts wind-sea error by 30 %; validation buoys carry swell share 0.40 vs 0.67 at test buoys. [PROJ] — [project report](../../reports/Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones.md)

### Inferences
Checklist for any multimodal or crossmodal claim in this project (own synthesis; each item maps to a cited pitfall):
1. **Input-withheld baselines, always in the same table:** camera only; co-input only (hindcast + wind ridge, the real baseline); location/time only (latitude, longitude, day of year, hour — the SatCLIP lesson); joint. Report the *conditional utilization* of the camera: joint minus co-input-only, with a bootstrap over buoys or days.
2. **Modality-shuffled controls, at three strictness levels:** permute camera embeddings (a) globally; (b) within buoy/camera — kills site-identity shortcuts; (c) within camera *and* within a hindcast-state bin — a conditional permutation that tests whether the image adds anything beyond what the co-input already says. Only (c) supports "the camera measures something the hindcast lacks".
3. **Formal conditional-independence check:** residual-on-residual (regress target and camera prediction on the co-inputs; correlate residuals; block-bootstrap by site) or the knockoff-based CPI on embedding groups.
4. **Report by partition and regime:** wind-sea height, swell height, period and direction separately, and by swell share, because total Hs is dominated by the co-input at swell-dominated buoys.
5. **Time controls:** label-lag control (pair each image with the same site's label from another day in the same month); frame-shuffled video (already planned); for shore sites, lag-corrected vs uncorrected labels (Q5).
6. **Leakage audit:** grouped folds by 300-km buoy cluster; blocks of days, not frames; for *cross-modal* pretraining and distillation, exclude all pairs from test sites and periods because the pair contains the label; declare any image-only self-supervision that saw test-site images as transductive; teacher models for distillation must be fitted out-of-fold.
7. **Missing-input table:** both inputs, camera missing, hindcast missing, camera degraded (Q4).
8. **Instrument truth:** score against buoys; where a hindcast is both an input and a pseudo-label (Q1 alignment to fields, Q8 quantile calibration), state it and never score against that same hindcast.
9. **Seeds and sites:** five seeds and site-level bootstrap intervals, as the earlier report already requires.

## Q10. Ranking: the top eight ideas, with hypothesis, data pairing, baseline, compute on one H200, and novelty

### Takeaway
Ranked by information gained per independent site and by how cheaply the idea can be falsified, the order is: (1) label-free site adaptation and few-shot calibration, (2) privileged spectral/wind supervision with a per-bin visibility curve, (3) gated, dropout-trained partition fusion, (4) self-supervised periodicity on shore video, (5) sea-state-aware contrastive continuation, (6) still → time-exposure prediction, (7) an image↔spectrum retrieval embedding, (8) a small any-to-any masked model. The first three cost hours and run on predictions or embeddings the project already has; none of the eight appears in Choi 2020, Kim 2023, Yang 2026 or Kamagata 2026.

### Cited Findings
Baselines and costs the proposals are measured against (all from project documents unless marked):
- Buoy stills: ConvNeXt-Tiny (DINOv3-distilled, gentle fine-tune) Hs RMSE 0.440 m at 12 unseen buoys; camera wind-sea height 0.215 m vs ERA5 0.341 m; ERA5 + wind ridge total Hs 0.259 m. One training run takes 40–55 min on one shared H200. [PROJ] — [project report](../../reports/Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones.md)
- Shore video: frozen V-JEPA 2 ViT-L features, ~1.5 s per clip including decoding; Kamagata 2026 held-out Hs R² 0.107 and Tp error 1.42 s as the published comparison. [PROJ] — [project report](../../reports/Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones.md)
- Frozen-probe gain from continued DINOv3 pretraining on buoy views: 0.642 → 0.582 m on validation. [PROJ] — assignment context
- What the four target papers used: Choi 2020 an ImageNet VGG16 on single views and frame-difference ConvLSTM; Kim 2023 GoogLeNet features + BiLSTM; Yang 2026 ResNet-18 + attitude MLP + attention on simulated data; Kamagata 2026 a self-supervised V-JEPA backbone + SlowFast + optical flow with Airy-dispersion constraints. [PROJ] — [project report](../../reports/Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones.md); [Yang note](../../docs/literature/papers/yang2026.md); [arXiv 2607.11998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11998)
- External effect sizes that set expectations: unlabeled-context adaptation +2 to +22 points over plain training ([ICRM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.09888)); regression test-time alignment +0.03 to +0.10 R² on mild shifts ([Adachi](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03263v2)); privileged-modality students +1 to +7 points, zero or negative when the privileged information is modality-specific, and about zero once labelled data are plentiful ([Garcia](https://arxiv.org/html/1806.07110); [MFH](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.06487); [Lambert](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1805.11614)); unseen-camera error about double seen-camera error ([Terra Incognita](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1807.04975)); gated fusion +7 to +13 AP over late fusion with a degraded sensor ([Bijelic](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1902.08913)); label-free periodicity learning at supervised accuracy ([SiNC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.07944)); rank-contrastive pretraining +0.09 °C on 35k webcam images, more under transfer ([RnC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.01189)); physically defined contrastive positives +7.6 points of frozen-feature accuracy in satellite imagery and 0.4–3.2 AUC points from metadata kernels in MRI ([Geography-Aware](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.09980); [y-Aware](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.08808)); generated intermediate modality +1.4 to +2.6 mIoU ([TerraMind](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11171v3)); image↔spectrum alignment adding up to +0.19 R² under nearest-neighbour read-out but only 0.00–0.03 under a trained probe, with both aligned and un-aligned self-supervised image embeddings at or above a supervised image model ([AstroCLIP](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03024v2)); any-to-any pretraining +1 to +2 mIoU over from-scratch at 9 M samples ([TerraMind](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11171v3)).

**1. Label-free site adaptation and few-shot site calibration**
- Hypothesis: a large share of the unseen-buoy error is a per-camera shift in embedding space (mounting, lens, hull, local colour) that can be removed using that camera's own unlabeled images; a few labelled hours remove most of the rest.
- Data pairing: cached embeddings of all ~41k strips (79 buoys) with same-hull labels; per-buoy unlabeled archives for the statistics; grouped folds over the 43 buoy groups. Shore: the four camera views with CDIP labels, plus ERA5/WIS distributions for label-free quantile calibration.
- Arms: (a) frozen head; (b) per-camera centring/whitening; (c) climatology-matched centring (statistics computed within hindcast-state bins); (d) significant-subspace alignment (Adachi); (e) context-set head; (f) affine calibration with k = 1, 3, 10, 30, 100 labelled hours.
- Baseline to beat: arm A (0.440 m at the sealed buoys; grouped-fold value once available) and, for wind sea, 0.215 m.
- Kill criterion: no arm improves the paired buoy-level difference beyond the seed spread.
- Novelty vs the wave literature: none of the four papers tests adaptation to a new camera; Choi and Kim split randomly within cameras, Kamagata trains on six scenes.

**2. Privileged spectral and wind supervision**
- Hypothesis: auxiliary targets the camera can see (high-frequency spectral bins, wind speed and direction, wind-sea partition) improve the wind-sea estimate at unseen buoys; targets it cannot see (swell bins, Tp) do not, and the per-bin R² curve shows where the boundary is.
- Data pairing: each strip ↔ same-hull 46-bin spectrum, r1/r2/α1/α2, wind speed/gust/direction, air–sea temperature difference. Shore variant: still + time-exposure + brightest ↔ video-derived pixel spectrum on the ~1,300 clips, then applied to the ~108k image triplets.
- Arms: (a) heights only (current); (b) + 46-bin head (normalised shape × energy); (c) + wind and partition heads; (d) hallucination loss towards a shared-trunk teacher that also sees wind and high-frequency bins; (e) control: teacher trained on shuffled spectra; (f) contrastive image↔spectrum alignment (rank 7) as an alternative way of using the same privileged signal, on the same folds.
- Baseline: arm A wind-sea 0.215 m, total 0.440 m; ERA5 for period and direction.
- Kill criterion: wind-sea error unchanged within seed spread and total Hs not harmed → keep the spectral head as an output only.
- Novelty: Yang 2026 uses a second signal as a *test-time input*; nobody in the set uses the buoy's richer record as *training-only* supervision. The visibility curve is a publishable result in its own right: a measured statement of which wave frequencies an optical camera sees.

**3. Gated, dropout-trained partition fusion**
- Hypothesis: the partition swap's gain grows, and its worst cases shrink, when the camera's wind-sea energy is weighted by a learned quality gate and its calibrated variance; with the camera unusable the fusion equals the hindcast.
- Data pairing: out-of-fold camera predictions (mean and variance) ↔ ERA5 partitions and wind at the 79 buoys; WIS partitions at shore sites; image-quality statistics per strip.
- Arms: partition swap (current); + gate; + inverse-variance weighting; + cross-attention over partition tokens (ablation); each evaluated in the four missing-input conditions.
- Baseline: ERA5 + wind ridge 0.259 m total, and the existing partition-swap figure in the experiment log.
- Kill criterion: gate and variance weighting do not improve the night/haze subset.
- Novelty: SAR work fuses a wind-sea prior with a swell spectrum (Wang 2026) but against ERA5, not buoys, and without a missing-sensor protocol.

**4. Self-supervised periodicity on shore video**
- Hypothesis: a network trained only with band-limit, sparsity and batch-variance losses on unlabeled clips outputs a signal whose spectral peak tracks CDIP peak period better than a raw pixel-intensity spectrum, and transfers across cameras because no site labels are involved.
- Data pairing: ~1,300 ten-minute 20-fps videos (≈217 hours) unlabeled for training; CDIP spectra only for scoring; later, any unlabeled WebCOOS clips.
- Arms: pixel-intensity spectrum (physics floor); SiNC losses with single-peak sparsity; two-band variant (sea and swell bands separately); + SimPer speed-equivariance; V-JEPA 2 supervised probe as the labelled comparator.
- Baseline: pixel-intensity spectrum Tp error and Kamagata's 1.42 s.
- Kill criterion: no improvement over the pixel-intensity spectrum on held-out cameras.
- Novelty: Kamagata uses a self-supervised backbone but a supervised period head; no label-free period estimator for waves was found.

**5. Sea-state-aware contrastive continuation**
- Hypothesis: replacing augment-invariance with (i) simultaneous-view positives and (ii) same-camera negatives produces features in which sea state, not camera identity, is the dominant axis, lowering unseen-buoy frozen-probe error below the existing continued checkpoint.
- Data pairing: six views per strip (positives); other days from the same buoy (negatives); the two paired-camera shore sites (cross-view positives at matched times); optional soft positives across sites by a kernel on ERA5/WIS state. Variant needing new data: stills from additional public shore cameras with ERA5 at each.
- Baseline: continued DINOv3 frozen probe 0.582 m (validation) and the gentle fine-tune 0.440 m (test).
- Kill criterion: frozen-probe gain does not survive grouped folds, or disappears after gentle fine-tuning.
- Leakage note: the hindcast-kernel variant uses hindcast state in pretraining; then the hindcast cannot be claimed as an independent comparator for the camera-only model.
- Novelty: no wave paper uses multi-view or metadata-defined positives.

**6. Still → time-exposure / brightest prediction**
- Hypothesis: an encoder trained to predict the ten-minute mean and maximum images from one frame learns breaker-zone energy statistics, giving a better frozen Hs probe at shore cameras than generic features; a generated time-exposure image helps at cameras that archive only stills.
- Data pairing: ~108k time-exposure and ~109k brightest images with the stills from the same clips; CDIP labels for the probe only.
- Baseline: DINOv3 frozen probe on stills; a model given the *real* time-exposure image (upper bound).
- Kill criterion: probe no better than DINOv3, or real time-exposure input itself adds nothing over the still.
- Novelty: time-exposure images have been used by hand-built breaker-line methods; no learned cross-prediction was found.

**7. Image↔spectrum contrastive embedding with a retrieval head**
- Hypothesis: a shared embedding of strips and buoy spectra gives spectra-by-retrieval whose bulk parameters match the regression head while producing physically shaped spectra and a query interface.
- Data pairing: 41k strip ↔ (46-bin spectrum, directional moments, wind vector) pairs from training buoys only; shore stills ↔ WIS 2-D spectra as a second corpus.
- Baseline: the spectral regression head from rank 2; ERA5 spectrum-derived parameters.
- Kill criterion: retrieval error clearly worse than regression and no user need for retrieval.
- Novelty: image-only SSL exists for SAR sea imagery; cross-modal alignment with spectra was not found for any ocean imager.

**8. Small any-to-any masked model**
- Hypothesis: one masked-token model over image tokens, spectral tokens, met scalars and hindcast tokens matches the purpose-built fusion of rank 3 with all inputs and degrades more gracefully with inputs missing, while also returning conditional estimates of swell and direction.
- Data pairing: everything listed for the buoys (and separately for shore sites with tide and sun tokens); cached image tokens.
- Baseline: rank 3's fusion in the four missing-input conditions.
- Kill criterion: worse than rank 3 with all inputs, or image tokens ignored (conditional permutation test shows no camera contribution).
- Novelty: none of the wave papers models more than image (+ attitude) → bulk parameters.

Ideas considered and left out of the eight: numeric-caption vision–language fine-tuning (evidence says language regresses quantities worse than a linear head; Q7); buoy-to-shore travel-time pretext (delay learning has precedents for other sensor pairs, but here run only the free lag-correlation diagnostic; Q5); camera-motion equivariance (project null on horizon features; Q5); reusing published EO/astronomy any-to-any weights (wrong domain; Q3).

What would change the order:
- If rank 1's centring removes most of the unseen-buoy gap, ranks 5 and 7 lose their main motivation (transfer) and drop.
- If rank 2's visibility curve shows usable skill below the wind-sea separation frequency, swell is not invisible after all and ranks 7–8 (which can exploit it) move up.
- If rank 4 fails on bimodal seas, period estimation stays with the physics front end and the pooled V-JEPA 2 head.
- If additional shore cameras with hindcast fields are collected, rank 5's hindcast-kernel variant moves to the top, because it is the only idea that increases the number of independent sites without new instruments.
