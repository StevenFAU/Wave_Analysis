# Perception and physics model families beyond encoder backbones for camera-based wave measurement

Scope and status: current as of 2026-11-19. This file covers vision-language models, promptable segmentation, geometry and calibration models, motion estimation, physics-informed and operator learning, inverse graphics of water, simulation-to-real, and alternative sensors. It does not repeat the two earlier reports on backbones, heads, pooling, uncertainty and domain pretraining ([backbones](../../reports/Vision%20backbones%20for%20wave%20cameras.md); [architectures](../../reports/Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones.md)).

How each source was read is tagged on every finding: **[FT]** full text (or full slide deck) read; **[ABS]** abstract or landing page only; **[SS]** search-engine summary only, a secondary reading that must be checked before it is quoted in a paper; **[API]** licence or date read from the GitHub or Hugging Face metadata API on 2026-11-19; **[PN]** the project's own notes. "Cited Findings" hold what is demonstrated in a source. "Inferences" hold my reasoning, arithmetic and all cost estimates, and are speculative unless a line says otherwise.

Search limit: the session's shared web-search budget ran out before the last four queries (event cameras a second time, horizon-based rectification of uncalibrated shore cameras, zero-shot weather/quality classification with CLIP-type models, and synthetic X-band pretraining numbers). Those are listed under Gaps. ScienceDirect, MDPI, Taylor & Francis and SpringerLink pages returned 403 or redirects and were not retried; for those papers only the search summary was available and they carry [SS].

## 1. Vision-language models: zero-shot or fine-tuned estimation of sea state and other physical quantities, and useful roles

### Takeaway
No published test of a vision-language model (VLM) estimating sea state, Beaufort or Douglas scale, or wave height from camera images was found, zero-shot or fine-tuned. On the nearest benchmarks VLMs guess quantities from prior knowledge more than they measure them from pixels (the best model scores 53 against a human 56 on QuantiPhy, and removing the video barely changes the score), so a VLM is a baseline row and a quality-control and weak-labelling tool here, not a candidate wave regressor.

### Cited Findings
**Sea state and marine imagery**
- Two extended searches found no paper that evaluates a VLM zero-shot on Beaufort sea state or wave height from photos; the nearest work is supervised CNN/ViT classification from a ship-bridge camera (ResNet-101, NASNet, MobileNet v2, ViT-B/32), which is fine-tuned and not zero-shot. [SS] — [Vorkapić et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03755)
- MarineEval (submitted 24 Dec 2025, WACV 2026) has 2,000 image question-answer pairs over 7 task dimensions and 20 capacity dimensions and tests 17 VLMs. Its abstract concludes that "existing VLMs cannot effectively answer the domain-specific questions". The abstract names no sea-state or wave-height task. [ABS] — [MarineEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21126)
- ClimateIQA (v1 June 2024, v4 Jan 2026) has 26,280 meteorological heatmaps and 762,120 instruction samples covering wind gust, precipitation, wind chill and heat index. Its abstract says GPT-4o, Qwen-VL and LLaVA 1.6 struggle with precise colour identification and spatial localisation, and that models fine-tuned on the set "significantly outperform" them. These are rendered reanalysis maps, not camera photographs. [ABS] — [ClimateIQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09838)
- A search summary of the ClimateIQA v1 PDF quotes GPT-4-Vision declining to identify "powerful gales, patterns or ocean conditions from an image". The abstract page does not contain this, so treat it as unverified. [SS] — [ClimateIQA v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09838v1)

**Do VLMs measure quantities from pixels?**
- QuantiPhy (CVPR 2026) gives a model a short video plus one physical prior (a size, speed or acceleration in real units) and asks for other kinematic quantities: 569 videos, 3,355 questions, scored by mean relative accuracy (MRA). Overall MRA: ChatGPT-5.1 53.1, Gemini-2.5 Pro 49.6, Gemini-2.5 Flash 48.6, Qwen3-VL-Instruct-32B 46.0 (best open-weight), Grok 4.1 45.0; humans 55.6. [FT, first 100,000 characters of the HTML] — [QuantiPhy](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19526)
- In the same paper, answering from the prior alone with the video removed is "often close to, and sometimes only slightly below" the video-plus-prior score; multiplying the prior by a scalar (a counterfactual) drops most models' MRA by 80 % and the strongest by 70 %; chain-of-thought prompting helps 3 of 21 models. The authors conclude "VLMs rely more on learned prior knowledge than visual inputs" and that current VLMs "are not yet input-faithful quantitative reasoners". The paper states its MRA threshold set two different ways (Section 4.1 against Appendix A.2). [FT] — [QuantiPhy](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19526)
- MeasureBench (CVPR 2026) has 2,442 image-question pairs over 26 instrument types; 18 current VLMs show gaps in low-level perception and precise reading of gauges. [SS; the PDF returned 403] — [MeasureBench](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Lin_Do_Vision-Language_Models_Measure_Up_Benchmarking_Visual_Measurement_Reading_with_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)
- FG-BMK (ICLR 2026): even with fine-tuning or a linear classifier, large VLMs trail purpose-built models on fine-grained recognition (one fine-tuned VLM with a linear head 78.88 % against 95.40 % for a specialist on aircraft). [SS] — [FG-BMK](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/4a0a8ab1552821883f6cd41a6c6fc8f3-Paper-Conference.pdf)

**Fine-tuning a multimodal LLM for image regression**
- Jennings et al. (WACV 2026) report that multimodal LLMs fine-tuned for image regression with preset output vocabularies and generic task prompts "perform equivalently to image-only models", so the language input is unused. Their bin-based head (RvTC, regression via transformer-based classification) sets the state of the art on four image-assessment datasets from images alone, and prompts help only when they carry information specific to each image (challenge titles on the AVA aesthetics set). [ABS] — [arXiv 2507.14997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14997). A search summary gives the AVA correlation as about 0.83 rising to about 0.90 with those prompts; not confirmed in the abstract. [SS]

**The closest water analogue: flood depth from photographs**
- FloodVision (v2 Sep 2026) pairs a general-purpose VLM with a knowledge base of real object dimensions (wheel arch, curb top) and no task training. On 654 crowdsourced New York flood photos it cuts mean absolute error from 15.62 cm (VLM alone) to 8.75 cm. Labels are resident-reported depths. [ABS] — [FloodVision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04772). A search summary of an earlier version gives 110 images, 8.17 cm, and names the baseline as GPT-4o at 10.28 cm; the versions differ. [SS]
- Lyu et al. (Water Resources Research 2025): GPT-4 reaches 58.8 % average accuracy classifying urban flood depth level; Gemini 48.8 %, rising to 59.2 % with a more elaborate prompt, and it tends to put nearly every image in one level. [SS] — [Lyu et al. 2025](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2024WR039494)
- FloodLlama, a Llama 3.2-11B Vision model fine-tuned with QLoRA on about 190,000 synthetic images, reported MAE below 0.97 cm but was **withdrawn** by its authors on 23 Mar 2026 pending updated results. Do not cite its numbers. [ABS] — [arXiv 2603.17108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17108)

### Inferences
- **Zero-shot baseline (worth one row in a table, not a method).** QuantiPhy's result predicts what a VLM will do on wave height: return a plausible number from scene semantics (whitecaps mean "rough") without measuring. That is the same information the project found its own camera model uses, the wind sea. The fair test is a class task (Choi's calm/rough, Kim's five classes, or Douglas scale), plus a numeric H_s answer scored by RMSE and slope beside ERA5 and the trained ConvNeXt. Expect it to lose to both; the value is a documented baseline no wave paper has published.
- **Quality-control filter (the most useful role, but undemonstrated for this data).** Lens droplets, fog, sun glare, night, a boat or person filling the frame are semantic judgements of the kind VLMs handle well in general; no source measured it on marine cameras, and the search that would have checked CLIP-type zero-shot weather classification was cut off. A cheaper first version needs no LLM: zero-shot text prompts against SigLIP 2 or PE-Core embeddings (both Apache-2.0), which the project already extracts as a contrast encoder.
- **Weak labels.** Breaker type on the Yin et al. 2025 clips gives a ready accuracy check for VLM labelling before using it on unlabelled WebCOOS clips; "visible swell lines" and "foam coverage low/medium/high" are candidate tags. All speculative until checked against the Yin labels.
- **Language-conditioned heads.** The one controlled study says generic prompts add nothing over the image and only image-specific text helps. For this project the image-specific context (site, camera heading, time of day, tide) is numeric and already available as tabular inputs, so a language pathway has no evident advantage over the existing late fusion.
- **Retrieval.** A k-nearest-neighbour H_s read from frozen embeddings is the retrieval baseline (the earlier report cites ocean-SAR work doing exactly this); it needs no VLM.
- **Cost on one H200 (estimates, unmeasured).** Qwen3-VL-8B is about 16–18 GB of bf16 weights and fits a partly free GPU. Budget 1–3 GPU-hours to label the 6,183 test strips and 500 quality-control frames; measure throughput on 200 strips first.
- **Cost by API (my arithmetic from the cited formula and prices).** One 480 × 270 buoy view is 18 × 10 = 180 visual tokens, so a six-view strip is 1,080. With an assumed 300-token prompt and 100-token answer, 6,183 strips are about 8.5 million input and 0.6 million output tokens: about $1.2 on Haiku 5.5, $23 on Sonnet 5.5, $46 on Opus 5.5, halved by batching. Models whose thinking cannot be turned off bill thinking as output, so the output share can be several times larger. A 2,688 × 1,520 shore frame hits the 4,784-token cap; downsampled to 1,344 × 760 it is 48 × 28 = 1,344 tokens, so 10,000 frames are about 16 million input tokens ($1.6 Haiku 5.5, $33 Sonnet 5.5, $66 Opus 5.5 before output).
- **Data handling.** Shore frames can show identifiable people on the beach. A local open-weight model avoids sending those frames to an outside service; if an API is used, mask people first (Section 2).

### Gaps
- No evidence, positive or negative, on any VLM (Gemini, GPT, Claude, Qwen-VL, InternVL, LLaVA-OneVision, PaliGemma 2, video-LLMs) estimating sea state from camera imagery. The project's own zero-shot run would be the first.
- Not researched: video-LLM handling of minutes-long clips, PaliGemma 2 fine-tuned as a regressor, Perception Encoder language-aligned features on texture tasks, SigLIP 2 zero-shot accuracy on weather or image-quality classes (search cut off).
- Gemini and GPT prices were not checked; no figures are given for them.
- MarineEval's per-task results and model list were not read (abstract only).

## 2. Promptable and open-vocabulary segmentation: whitecaps, foam, water/sky/shoreline, breaking crests, runup edge

### Takeaway
Zero-shot promptable segmentation is demonstrated for water, sky, land and the shoreline (water IoU 0.97–0.99, beating a supervised segmenter trained on 350 local images at unseen shores), and SAM 2 has been used for shoreline and total-water-level tracking from a fixed coastal camera. Nothing was found that applies SAM-family models to whitecaps, active breaking or crest tracking; those remain the territory of small task-trained networks (whitecap IoU about 73 %, active-breaking balanced accuracy about 90 %).

### Cited Findings
**Models and licences**
- SAM 3 (arXiv 20 Nov 2025, v2 28 Mar 2026) detects, segments and tracks every instance of a concept given a short noun phrase, image exemplars or both, in images and video; the authors state it "doubles the accuracy of existing systems" on promptable concept segmentation and release it with the SA-Co benchmark (about 4 million unique concept labels from the data engine). [ABS] — [SAM 3 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16719)
- The repository gives 848 M parameters (a DETR-style detector and a SAM 2-style tracker sharing one vision encoder); SAM 3.1 "Object Multiplex", a faster joint multi-object tracker, was released 2026-03-27; checkpoints are gated on Hugging Face; video result on SA-V test is 30.3 cgF1 and 58.0 pHOTA against human 53.1 and 70.5. No memory or speed figure is given. [FT of README] — [facebookresearch/sam3](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam3)
- The SAM License grants use, copying, modification and distribution with no field-of-use restriction on commercial use; it bars military or warfare, nuclear, espionage and ITAR-regulated uses, and requires that published research acknowledge use of the SAM materials. [FT of LICENSE] — [SAM License](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam3/blob/main/LICENSE)
- SAM 2.1 weights and repository are Apache-2.0; Grounding DINO (base) is Apache-2.0; the Grounded-SAM-2 repository is Apache-2.0. [API] — [SAM 2.1](https://huggingface.co/api/models/facebook/sam2.1-hiera-large); [sam2 repo](https://api.github.com/repos/facebookresearch/sam2); [Grounding DINO](https://huggingface.co/api/models/IDEA-Research/grounding-dino-base); [Grounded-SAM-2](https://api.github.com/repos/IDEA-Research/Grounded-SAM-2)

**Water, sky, land and shoreline: demonstrated**
- A vessel-localisation study (21 Aug 2026) compared zero-shot models on 30 hand-labelled coastal images from three Hawaiian sites. Mean IoU / water IoU / 95th-percentile shoreline error / speed on an RTX 4070: Grounded-SAM 2 0.95 / 0.99 / 7.0 px / 17.5 fps; Grounded-MobileSAM 0.95 / 0.99 / 5.7 px / 5.2 fps; SAM 3 0.89 / 0.97 / 9.3 px / 6.1 fps (land IoU 0.81); YOLOE 0.88 / 0.98 / 9.0 px / 117 fps. A supervised YOLO11-seg trained on 350 labelled images from one site scored 0.75 / 0.83 / 34.8 px with 7 % failures and "failed to generalize to unseen shorelines". Sky IoU was 0.90 for Grounded-SAM 2 and SAM 3. [FT, first 100,000 characters] — [arXiv 2608.21276](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21276v1)
- A 2026 Taylor & Francis article applies SAM and SAM 2 to shoreline and total-water-level extraction from a fixed station in Bali (90-second clips captured hourly over about four months), scored by mean absolute deviation and bias against hand-digitised shorelines. The SAM 2 tiny variant is reported best for single-transect monitoring, and SAM workflows ignored pedestrians that a gradient edge detector mistook for the shoreline. The error values were not retrieved. [SS; page returned 403] — [doi 10.1080/21664250.2026.2732511](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21664250.2026.2732511)
- "Shoreline-SAM" adds a temporal module to SAM and is reported robust to tidal shifts and occlusion. [SS; ScienceDirect not accessed] — [Shoreline-SAM](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0952197626017641)
- Where zero-shot text prompting meets unusual coastal classes it can be weak: SAM 3 text prompts reached F1 0.51 on estuary mudflats in satellite imagery. [ABS] — [UNH honors thesis, Spring 2026](https://scholars.unh.edu/honors/958)
- A common working pattern is SAM for masks plus a small trained classifier: SAM 2 masks with a fine-tuned captioner-classifier reached macro-F1 about 0.90 on five seafloor classes in side-scan sonar; SAM 3 text prompts were used without a score. [ABS] — [EGU26-3878](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-3878.html)
- High mask IoU does not guarantee an accurate waterline: across deep models on aerial imagery, pixel accuracy and IoU were high while shoreline alignment "often remained poor". [SS] — [Geomatics 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/geomatics6010021)

**Whitecaps, foam and breaking: task-trained networks only**
- Three searches found no paper applying SAM, SAM 2 or SAM 3 to whitecaps, sea foam, breaking crests or swash in ocean video. [SS]
- EMA-SE-ResUNet (Electronics, Oct 2025), trained on shipborne images from the northwest Pacific, reports whitecap IoU 73.32 %, whitecap F1 84.60 and pixel absolute error 0.081 %, above U-Net, DeepLabv3+, HRNet and PSPNet. [SS; MDPI returned 403] — [Electronics 14(21):4286](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/21/4286/htm)

[ABS; API] — [arXiv 2101.11478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11478); [deepwaves repo](https://api.github.com/repos/caiostringari/deepwaves)
- V-BeachNet, a fully convolutional network, segments swash in rectified video to estimate runup along 200 m of shoreline (Coastal Engineering 2024). [SS] — [Salatin et al. 2024](https://www2.whoi.edu/staff/elgar/wp-content/uploads/sites/153/2024/05/153.pdf)
- For foam in a different setting, SAM 2 has been fine-tuned for wastewater-plant foam segmentation in a federated setup; the abstract gives no accuracy. [ABS] — [arXiv 2511.08130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08130)
- CoastSeg is GPL-3.0 and actively maintained (last push 2026-10-10). [API] — [CoastSeg repo](https://api.github.com/repos/SatelliteShorelines/CoastSeg)

### Inferences
- **What is ready to use.** Sea/sky/land masks from Grounded-SAM 2 or SAM 3 give three things the project now does by hand or by fixed rule: the horizon line on shore cameras (boundary of the sky and water masks), an automatic surf-zone crop that follows a camera after it pans or is replaced, and masks for people, boats and the pier for quality control and privacy. These are infrastructure gains, not wave measurements.
- **What is unproven.** Whitecaps are small, amorphous and numerous; SAM-family training data are objects. On 480 × 270 buoy views a distant whitecap is a few pixels. Whether the text prompt "whitecap" or "sea foam" returns usable masks is an open empirical question. A whitecap fraction would also be a wind-sea feature, which the camera model already reads, so its expected gain on H_s is small; its value is interpretability and a physically defined intermediate quantity.
- **Crest tracking by mask propagation is speculative.** SAM 2/3 memory tracking assumes persistent objects; crests form, merge and vanish within seconds. The validated route for crest speed remains the timestack U-Net cited in the earlier report.
- **Quantity each could unlock.** Sea/sky boundary → horizon tilt on shore cameras (calibration, Section 3). Foam mask per frame → breaking fraction time series and breaker-line position (surf-zone width is a proxy for wave height over a fixed bar). Waterline mask per frame → runup time series, whose spectrum carries swash period and infragravity energy.
- **Cost on one H200 (estimate).** At the cited 6–17 fps on an RTX 4070, expect a few tens of frames per second on an H200: about 3–5 GPU-hours for all 246,000 buoy views, minutes for a 5,000-frame pilot. SAM 3 at 848 M parameters is under 4 GB of weights.

### Gaps
- No accuracy of any promptable model on whitecap fraction, active breaking, crest or swash masks against hand labels or against the task-trained networks above.

- Sáez et al. (U-Net wave-by-wave breaking) and the wider whitecap literature (Eadi Stringari and co-authors' later work) were not re-read; only the 2021 arXiv abstract was.
- No SAM 3.1 speed or memory figure was found on the repository page.

## 3. Geometry models: can they auto-calibrate an uncalibrated shore camera well enough to rectify pixels to metres on the sea plane?

### Takeaway
Partly. A learned single-image calibrator gives focal length to about 7–14 % (median field-of-view error 3–5°) but pitch only to about 1–2°, which is far too coarse for a low-grazing sea view; pitch and roll must come from the horizon line, and camera height above the water must come from site information. The combination has not been tested on any coastal or maritime camera. The one open tool built for exactly this case, remote calibration of existing surf cameras from lidar-derived control points, reached 3.4 m and 6.5 m horizontal error at two cameras.

### Cited Findings
**New since the earlier report**

The retrained Perspective Fields network (ParamNet) is worse on every entry (for example 1.14 / 1.94 / 9.01 on Stanford2D3D). Supplying a known gravity direction lowers the field-of-view error (3.21° to 2.30° on Stanford2D3D). **Coastal-camera calibration without a site survey**
- SurfRCaT is an open-source Python tool (GPL-3.0, last pushed 2023-08) that calibrates and rectifies existing coastal cameras by extracting ground control points remotely from airborne lidar. [SS; API] — [USGS publication page](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/surfrcat-a-tool-remote-calibration-pre-existing-coastal-cameras-enable-their-use); [SurfRCaT repo](https://api.github.com/repos/conlin-matt/SurfRCaT)

[SS] — [catalogue record of the Remote Sensing paper](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsdoj,edsdoj.b1046b3291454ddbbf04c70688785c2a)

[PN] — [earlier notes](../Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones/physical_measurement_models.md)

**Monoscopic rectification feeding a wave measurement**
- An ICPR 2026 oral (La Rochelle) rectifies a single oblique mast-camera video to a top-down view with a pinhole-model homography onto the z = 0 plane, then takes a 3-D FFT; stabilisation is listed as "not required for static cameras". On real monoscopic sequences (Black Sea 2011, wave-gauge reference, H_s 0.30–0.55 m) peak direction had a bias of about 15° and a standard deviation of about 20°, and peak frequency a bias of about −0.1 Hz and a standard deviation of about 0.1 Hz at true peaks of 0.27–0.38 Hz. [FT of the slide deck] — [ICPR 2026 slides](https://icpr2026orgteam.github.io/OralPres/1062.pdf)
- Yu et al. (2024) convert pixel displacement to metres with a pinhole model and a range taken from a co-located X-band radar (0.03 m per pixel at 245 m for a 10° vertical field of view over 1,440 px). Their single preliminary wave-height case gave 0.98 m (zero-upcrossing) and 1.05 m (spectral) against 1.22 m from the radar, and they state that the result "requires further validation". [FT, pages 1–6] — [Acta Oceanol. Sin. 43(9):136–144](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13131-024-2359-y)

**Horizon detection**
- IceHorizon (Aug 2026; 30 ship and 8 drone videos) compares four classical and two hybrid deep-plus-classical horizon detectors; the hybrids gave "the highest accuracy and most reliable horizon estimates", and all methods did better on ship than on drone imagery. No angle errors are in the abstract. [ABS] — [IceHorizon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07018)

### Inferences
- **What the sea plane needs.** Rectifying a planar sea needs four numbers: focal length, pitch, roll and camera height above the instantaneous water level. Given the focal length and the horizon line (which fixes pitch and roll), every pixel row below the horizon maps to a depression angle θ, and ground range is R = h / tan θ. The geometry is then known up to the single scale h.
- **Why learned pitch is not enough (my arithmetic).** Range error from a pitch error δ is δ / (sin θ cos θ), about δ/θ at low angles. For a camera 10 m above the water looking 300 m out, θ = 1.9°: a 0.2° pitch error is an 11 % range error, and GeoCalib's median 0.9–1.9° pitch error would be 50–100 %. At 1,000 m, 0.2° is 35 %. The horizon, by contrast, can be located to a pixel or two, about 0.03–0.06° at a 2,000 px focal length. So pitch and roll must come from the horizon, and metric work should stay inside roughly 30 camera heights of range.
- **Focal length from a learned model (my arithmetic).** A 3.2° field-of-view error on a 40–60° lens is a 7–9 % focal error; 4.9° is 10–14 %. Range, wavelength and celerity inherit that fraction. Deep-water period inferred from wavelength goes as the square root, so inherits half (3–7 %). Supplying gravity from the horizon should help, as GeoCalib's own prior experiment shows, but nothing measures it on sea scenes.
- **Camera height is the unlearnable part.** No model estimates it (earlier notes), and on a pier it changes with the tide: by 10–20 % if the tide range is 1–2 m and the mount is 8–12 m above the water (both assumed, neither checked for the project's sites). It has to come from the pier's deck elevation plus a tide gauge, or from control points on the pier itself, which is what SurfRCaT does. WebCOOS cameras at piers have exactly the "immobile hard structures" that method needs.
- **What it would unlock.** Wave direction in compass degrees, wavelength and celerity in physical units, a period cross-check through the dispersion relation, breaker height in metres through a known range, and the per-site "metres per pixel at the breaker line" input the backbone report suggested. Peak period itself needs none of this, because frequency is the same in pixels as in metres.
- **Paired cameras at one site.** If the east and west views at a site do not overlap, two-view geometry (MASt3R, VGGT) gives nothing beyond a consistency check that both views return the same focal length and height. If they overlap, wide-baseline stereo of a moving surface would need frame-level synchronisation that public streams are not known to provide. This is speculation; overlap was not checked.
- **Re-registration after pans.** Month-to-month pans show the same static land from new angles. Matching land features between an old calibrated view and a new one (classical or learned matchers) is the UCalib idea and is the natural automatic step; no source tested learned matchers (LoFTR, RoMa, MASt3R) on coastal cameras.
- **Buoy cameras (speculative).** The horizon gives roll and pitch, the camera height is fixed by the hull, and the focal length is common to the fleet, so one fleet-wide focal estimate (median of GeoCalib over many views) would rectify the near field of every view. The range footprint of one pixel row is about R² Δθ / h. Under assumed values of h = 3 m and 0.15° per pixel (neither sourced), that is 0.8 m at 30 m, 2.2 m at 50 m and 8.7 m at 100 m, while wind-sea wavelengths for 3–6 s periods are 14–56 m. So only the nearest 50–60 m resolves individual wind waves, and the hull's own pitching moves that band. A dominant-wavelength estimate from a single still is therefore marginal but testable.
- **Does the earlier rejection of monocular depth on water change?** No. Nothing new was found showing metric depth on a water surface working; the licence clarifications above (GeoCalib Apache-2.0, MoGe-2 MIT) make the land-side sanity check easier to run and nothing more.
- **Cost.** GeoCalib and a horizon detector on a few hundred frames per camera view: minutes of GPU or CPU.

### Gaps
- No published accuracy of GeoCalib, Perspective Fields, MoGe, UniDepth, Depth Anything 3, VGGT or DUSt3R/MASt3R on coastal or open-sea imagery; no test of horizon-plus-learned-focal rectification against surveyed control points. The targeted search for horizon-based rectification of uncalibrated shore cameras was cut off by the search limit.
- USGS CoastCam software releases and any "PTZ re-registration" literature were not found in the searches run; one query on learned feature matching for shore cameras returned nothing relevant.
- SurfRCaT's two error figures come from a search summary, not the paper.
- DUSt3R, MASt3R, VGGT and UniDepth licence terms remain unverified.
- The buoy-camera height and field of view used in the footprint arithmetic are assumptions.

## 4. Motion estimation on water: optical flow, PIV, celerity, timestack and spectral methods, and their published accuracy

### Takeaway
Every published single-camera period estimate that agrees with a reference does so by taking a spectrum of a scalar motion or brightness signal over minutes, not by a learned motion network: texture-displacement spectra matched X-band radar peak period within 3 % on average (7 % at worst), and a 3-D FFT of a rectified patch recovered direction to about 20° but with a −0.1 Hz frequency bias. Modern learned optical flow (SEA-RAFT, MEMFOF) has no validation for wave celerity or period; optical flow and PIV are validated for surf-zone currents (about 0.1–0.2 m/s error), which is a different quantity.

### Cited Findings
**Period and direction from motion or spectra**
- Yu, Chu and Yuan (2024) track the vertical displacement of ripple texture in a 20 × 20 px box by template matching between consecutive frames (25 fps, 2,560 × 1,440, 20-minute records, camera no more than 5° below the horizon), accumulate it, and take its spectrum. Over 11 twenty-minute intervals the peak period differed from the co-located X-band radar inversion by 3 % on average and 7 % at most, and from a period derived from a buoy's motion seen in the same video by 5 % on average and 11 % at most. Three boxes in the first record gave 6.40, 6.52 and 5.82 s against 6.06 s (radar) and 5.70 s (buoy motion), maximum deviations of 8 % and 14 %, which are the figures the abstract quotes. All cases are near 6 s. A box 690–776 m away failed (a featureless, monotonically falling spectrum), and slow drift had to be removed with a 0.8 rad/s cutoff. The references are a radar inversion and video-tracked buoy motion, not a wave-buoy instrument. [FT, pages 1–6] — [Acta Oceanol. Sin. 43(9):136–144](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13131-024-2359-y)
- The ICPR 2026 method (bird's-eye rectification, mean removal, 3-D Hann window, 3-D FFT, cylindrical projection to a frequency-direction spectrum, peak pick) gave, on 684 rendered clips, direction error with mean about 0° and standard deviation about 30°, and frequency bias about +0.05 Hz with standard deviation about 0.06 Hz; on real sequences, direction bias about 15° and standard deviation about 20°, frequency bias about −0.1 Hz and standard deviation about 0.1 Hz. One real sequence showed a bimodal error attributed to wind sea plus swell. One window of 1,024 frames at 256 × 256 took 2.9 s and 2.4 GiB on a laptop CPU. [FT of the slide deck] — [ICPR 2026 slides](https://icpr2026orgteam.github.io/OralPres/1062.pdf)
- Kamagata et al.'s peak-period model was trained partly on labels "automatically generated based on an optical flow pipeline" whose accuracy is never quantified; its physics term is only a penalty on predictions outside 8–20 s; and all reported metrics are on the set used for fine-tuning. [FT] — [arXiv 2606.13302](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13302v1)
- The earlier reports already record cBathy's 0.51 m depth error after about 33 hours of observation, a timestack crest-tracking U-Net at 0.17 m, and a webcam timestack celerity method at 0.43 m. [PN] — [architectures report](../../reports/Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones.md); [backbones report](../../reports/Vision%20backbones%20for%20wave%20cameras.md)

**Currents from foam motion (a different quantity)**
- Foam-tracking PIV from a 40 m tower agrees with in-situ current meters with R² about 0.8 alongshore and 0.5–0.7 cross-shore for 10–60-minute means, when there is enough foam, usually at significant wave heights above about 0.5 m. [FT] — [Salatin, Elgar & Raubenheimer 2026](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025JH000906)
- Optical-flow wave-averaged surf-zone currents: RMSE 0.15 m/s alongshore and 0.13 m/s cross-shore in the mid surf zone, worse in the inner surf zone. [SS; MDPI returned 403] — [Remote Sensing 13:690](http://dx.doi.org/10.3390/rs13040690)
- Under high-energy waves, time-averaged optical-flow velocities against current profilers: r² 0.5–0.8, RMSE 0.12–0.24 m/s. [SS] — [Remote Sensing 13:1874](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13101874)
- A wavelet optical-flow method on drone video agreed with an ADCP within 0.06 m/s and 16°. [SS] — [Dérian & Almar 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317548114_Wavelet-Based_Optical_Flow_Estimation_of_Instant_Surface_Currents_From_Shore-Based_and_UAV_Videos)

**Learned optical flow: availability, not validation**
- MEMFOF (ICCV 2025) is a multi-frame RAFT-style model needing 2.09 GB at 1080p for inference and 28.5 GB for native-1080p training; at submission it ranked first on Spring (1-px outlier rate 3.289), Sintel clean (EPE 0.963) and KITTI-2015 (Fl-all 2.94 %). [ABS] — [MEMFOF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23151)
- MEMFOF, SEA-RAFT and RAFT code are BSD-3-Clause; AllTracker is MIT. [API] — [memfof](https://api.github.com/repos/msu-video-group/memfof); [SEA-RAFT](https://api.github.com/repos/princeton-vl/SEA-RAFT); [RAFT](https://api.github.com/repos/princeton-vl/RAFT); [alltracker](https://api.github.com/repos/aharley/alltracker)

**Learned successors to celerity-based bathymetry**
- A conditional variational autoencoder (ResNet-101 encoder, shallow-water residual loss, total-variation loss) maps 30-minute PIV surface-flow fields at Duck to surf-zone depth: RMSE 0.18–0.50 m on bathymetries seen in training and 0.66 m on an unseen one, where only 30 % of observations fell inside its 90 % intervals. The authors set this beside 0.17–0.81 m for cBathy variants and call the two complementary: cBathy works in mild waves, foam-tracking in energetic ones. Training code is released. [FT, pages 1–12] — [Salatin, Elgar & Raubenheimer 2026](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025JH000906)

### Inferences
- **Why this matters for the project's null result.** The project found that no learned probe recovers peak period from shore video. The two methods above that do recover it share three features: minutes of record, a scalar signal with a physical meaning, and a spectrum. That points at the signal fed to the spectrum, not at the encoder.
- **Displacement spectra as a complement to brightness spectra.** Brightness at a pixel depends on sky reflection and sun angle as well as wave slope; the vertical displacement of surface texture follows the orbital motion more directly. Yu's method is a few lines of template matching on near-field boxes. Whether it beats the project's existing brightness spectrum against a buoy is untested anywhere, since Yu's reference was a radar.
- **Dense flow features versus brightness spectra.** Flow adds what a scalar spectrum lacks: the direction of crest motion and its speed in pixels per second. Speed becomes celerity only after rectification (Section 3). No source compares flow-fed and brightness-fed period estimators. A reasonable order is template-matched displacement first (CPU, no model), dense flow only if displacement helps.
- **Does the earlier rejection of point trackers on water change?** No new evidence was found for or against them. Yu's result is new evidence that a motion signal can carry period, but it is box-level template matching scored against a radar, not a point tracker scored against a buoy, so the earlier statement that no flow method has buoy validation still stands.
- **The frequency bias is a warning.** The 3-D FFT method's bias changed sign between rendered (+0.05 Hz) and real (−0.1 Hz) data. At a 0.1 Hz swell peak, a 0.1 Hz bias is useless; the method as published is a wind-sea tool, and any use here needs the bias measured against CDIP.
- **Cost.** All of the above is CPU work on existing clips: seconds per window for the FFT, less for template matching. MEMFOF at 1080p fits in about 2 GB of GPU memory if dense flow is wanted later.

### Gaps
- No instrument-validated accuracy for wave celerity, period or direction from RAFT, SEA-RAFT, FlowFormer or MEMFOF on ocean video.
- No head-to-head of flow features against pixel-brightness spectra for period or direction.
- Radon-transform and large-scale PIV celerity accuracies beyond those in the earlier report were not gathered; three of the current figures above rest on search summaries.
- Yu et al.'s cases are all wind sea near 6 s at one site; nothing shows the displacement method on 10–16 s swell.

## 5. Physics-informed and operator learning; spectral-domain inductive biases

### Takeaway
Physics-informed networks and neural operators for waves are demonstrated almost entirely on synthetic data and need an input the project's cameras do not supply (a phase-resolved elevation, a radar-like intensity field, or wavenumbers plus sparse wave heights). The one idea with real-data support that transfers directly is the spectral front end: on X-band radar, a 0.7 M-parameter transformer fed the 512 strongest wavenumber-frequency points matched a 3-D CNN on the raw sequence (0.23 against 0.25 m RMSE) at about one-ninetieth of the compute, and both beat a single-image model (0.51 m) by half.

### Cited Findings
**Phase-resolved reconstruction with operators and PINNs: synthetic**
- Ehlers et al. (Ocean Engineering 2023) compare a U-Net and a Fourier neural operator (FNO) for reconstructing surface elevation from sparse radar data, trained on synthetic one-dimensional wave fields from the high-order spectral method with a geometric radar model; accurate reconstruction required several historic radar snapshots per input, and the FNO handled the data structure better. [ABS] — [arXiv 2305.11913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11913)
- A multi-scale FNO for phase-resolved prediction from radar images, trained on simulated nonlinear wave fields, reports about 14 % lower error than a standard FNO. [SS] — [Physics of Fluids 37, 117116](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/pof/article/37/11/117116/3371436/Phase-resolved-wave-prediction-from-radar)
- A physics-informed neural operator reconstructs phase-resolved nonlinear wave fields from sparse buoy time series or radar snapshots with no ground-truth fields in training, by putting the free-surface boundary-condition residuals in the loss; it was validated on "highly realistic synthetic wave data". [ABS] — [arXiv 2508.03315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03315)

**Phase-averaged physics (energy balance, dispersion) in the loss**
- NWnets (ICCE 2022) is a physics-informed network encoding the wave energy balance equation and the dispersion relation. With all 126, or 63, or 31 laboratory wave gauges over a circular shoal it reconstructs the wave-height field; with wavenumbers plus sparse wave heights, or with sparse heights and depths, it inverts bathymetry. The inversion tests use synthetic XBeach output for Duck: "we take a simplified approach by using synthetic model data instead of actual remotely-sensed data". [FT, pages 1–3] — [NWnets](https://icce-ojs-tamu.tdl.org/icce/article/download/12653/11926)
- A 2025 preprint puts the extended Boussinesq equations (Celeris solver) in the loss to infer bathymetry; it requires wave-height information and is trained on synthetic waves. [SS] — [ESS Open Archive preprint](https://essopenarchive.org/doi/pdf/10.22541/essoar.174129307.73050322/v1)
- The only field-data example found is the flow-to-depth model in Section 4 (shallow-water residual loss on real PIV flows; 0.66 m RMSE on an unseen bathymetry). Its authors note that physics-informed networks "face challenges in trainability and predictive accuracy" as solutions become multiscale. [FT] — [Salatin, Elgar & Raubenheimer 2026](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025JH000906)
- Kamagata et al.'s "physics-guided" loss contains no dispersion relation, only a range penalty. [FT] — [arXiv 2606.13302](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13302v1)

**Spectral-domain front ends: real radar data**
- Spectral Point Transformer (Jan 2026): take the 3-D FFT of each 64-frame, 128 × 128 X-band radar sequence, keep the top points by power, and feed each point's wavenumber, frequency and power to a small transformer. On 1,620 sequences from one East China Sea site (1,080 train, 540 test; buoy reference accurate to ±(0.1 m + 10 %); H_s 0.5–6.3 m): single-image transformer 0.51 m RMSE and correlation 0.85 (86.4 M parameters); full 3-D-spectrum transformer 0.24 m and 0.97 (47.9 M, 3.4 GFLOPs); 3-D VGG on the raw sequence 0.25 m and 0.97 (0.43 M, 94.6 GFLOPs); spectral points 0.23 m and 0.97 (0.70 M, 1.04 GFLOPs). The selected points cluster along the dispersion shell. The authors note the dataset is small and from one site, and they report no classical signal-to-noise baseline. [FT] — [arXiv 2601.04581](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04581v1)
- A 2024 radar study converts the image sequence to wavenumber-frequency space by 3-D FFT, band-passes around the gravity-wave dispersion relation, and feeds the filtered spectrum to a U-Net that outputs the directional spectrum. [SS; MDPI not accessed] — [JMSE 12:1609](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse12091609)
- For stereo cameras, a 2021 network embeds the dispersion relation in its architecture to complete sparse reconstructed wave fields. [SS] — [Remote Sensing 13:3780](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13183780)
- No paper was found that applies a dispersion-shell filter to an optical 3-D FFT and then a learned model. [SS]

### Inferences
- **What does not fit.** Operators and PINNs that reconstruct phase-resolved fields need a calibrated, spatially resolved observable tied to elevation or slope. An uncalibrated oblique RGB view gives brightness, whose relation to slope depends on sky and sun. The stereo and lidar research sets in the project could host such a model, but that would be a different study from beating the four target papers.
- **What does fit: a spectral front end.** The radar result is the same lesson the project met from the other side: a single image is a wind-sea reader (0.51 m), and the gain comes from the time axis, which a 3-D spectrum exposes directly and cheaply. The optical analogue is a 3-D FFT of a near-field patch over a few minutes, reduced to the strongest wavenumber-frequency points or to a frequency-direction map, read by a small transformer. Nobody has published it for cameras.
- **Dispersion as a self-calibrator (speculative but physically simple).** In deep water ω² = g k. A spectral peak measured at frequency ω and at wavenumber k_px in radians per pixel of a rectified but unscaled plane gives the scale directly: metres per pixel = g k_px / ω². Each energetic point is one estimate, and their agreement is a quality score. In finite depth the unknown depth enters through tanh(kh), so the scale is identifiable only with a known depth or energy spread across a wide band. This is how marine radar recovers currents and depth with a known scale; using it to recover the scale itself is my proposal, not a cited result.
- **A differentiable dispersion layer** is trivial to write (the relation is closed-form); its value is as a constraint tying a predicted wavelength, period and depth together, and as the filter that suppresses energy off the shell (foam drift, cloud shadows, camera shake).
- **Spectrum-shaped outputs.** A head that outputs a non-negative normalised spectrum times a separately predicted energy, with H_s and periods computed from its moments, is already rank 5 in the architectures report and is consistent with everything above. Nothing new changes that design.
- **Cost.** CPU for the FFTs (2.9 s per 1,024-frame window in the ICPR slides); minutes of GPU for a sub-million-parameter transformer.

### Gaps
- No neural operator or PINN was found that takes optical video as input, or that is validated against wave buoys.
- No learned wavenumber-frequency front end (trainable filters instead of a fixed FFT) was found for either radar or optical data.
- The radar spectral result is one site and one paper; the earlier radar deep-learning literature was not re-read.
- PINO, multi-scale FNO and Ehlers et al. accuracy numbers were not available from abstracts.

## 6. Differentiable rendering and inverse graphics of water: slope sensing, neural surface reconstruction, monocular-to-elevation

### Takeaway
Nothing found recovers a metric sea-surface elevation from a single ordinary camera by inverse rendering, neural radiance fields or Gaussian splatting; the Gaussian-splatting work that mentions water models the underwater medium, not the moving surface. Metric elevation still comes from stereo (a Dec 2025 neural stereo method reports millimetre-level elevation in the image centre) or from polarimetric slope sensing, which has an open laboratory dataset but no field validation.

### Cited Findings
- A targeted search for monocular water-surface reconstruction with differentiable rendering or Gaussian splatting returned underwater-medium methods (AquaFlow, Gaussian Splashing) and generic dynamic-scene methods, none for a wavy free surface. [SS] — [AquaFlow](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22906); [Gaussian Splashing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.19588)
- Liu et al. (Dec 2025; A. Benetazzo is a co-author) reconstruct the 3-D free surface with an attention-augmented pyramid network "built on a stereo-vision dataset", then recover velocity fields with physics constraints: millimetre-level elevation in the central region, dominant-frequency errors below 0.01 Hz, two million points in 1.35 s, tested under real-sea conditions. No baseline metrics or code release are stated in the abstract. [ABS] — [arXiv 2512.06024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06024)
- A 2025 Ocean Engineering study renders synthetic stereo pairs in Blender and fine-tunes RAFT-Stereo on them, entirely in simulation; it argues that WASS-generated depth maps paired with real stereo datasets "do not constitute reliable ground-truths". [SS; ScienceDirect not accessed] — [Ocean Engineering 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0029801825006717)
- Polarimetric slope sensing: a Technion dataset (Oct 2024) pairs polarimetric images of mechanically generated waves in a laboratory basin with resistance wave-gauge records, covering monochromatic trains of several steepnesses, irregular JONSWAP fields and breaking cases from several camera positions; it is hosted on ScienceDB under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 and is intended for supervised learning of elevation from polarimetric images. [ABS] — [arXiv 2410.22849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22849)
- The same group's 2023 method infers surface slope and elevation from polarimetric images with neural networks; results found are laboratory only. [SS] — [search record of Meas. Sci. Technol. 2023](https://scite.ai/authors/noam-ginio-02z40)
- Brightness-to-height regression from one camera has been attempted with a neural network trained on paired brightness and measured heights; the abstract stresses the "multitude of possible physical models" and the need for calibration coefficients and gives no accuracy. [ABS] — [Salin & Vitalsky 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09967)
- Ocean renderers with physical spectra are available: Blender's Ocean modifier offers Phillips, Pierson-Moskowitz, JONSWAP and Texel-Marsen-Arsloe (shallow-water) spectra with fetch and peak-sharpening controls. [SS of the Blender API reference] — [Blender OceanModifier](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.1/bpy.types.OceanModifier.html). The Crest ocean system for Unity, used by Yang 2026, has an MIT-licensed repository last pushed 2026-11-17. [API; PN] — [crest repo](https://api.github.com/repos/wave-harmonic/crest); [Yang 2026 notes](../../docs/literature/papers/yang2026.md)

### Inferences
- **Why monocular inverse rendering of the sea is hard.** A pixel's brightness depends on the local slope, the sky radiance in the reflected direction, the sun, and foam. One image leaves slope ambiguous without a sky model; a fixed camera has no parallax; the surface never repeats. Analysis-by-synthesis (fit spectrum parameters until a rendered clip matches the real one in statistics) is conceivable with the renderers above, but no one has shown it, and it would be a research programme, not a test.
- **What slope statistics would give.** Glitter and polarimetric methods measure slope, and slope variance is dominated by short waves. That is a wind-sea quantity, the same part of the sea the project's camera model already reads. These methods are therefore unlikely to help with swell or total H_s, and likely to help with a physically defined wind-sea target (the "wave slope" target proposed in the third project report).
- **Monocular-to-elevation trained on stereo truth** is the only route here that could use project data (the small stereo sets), and the Ocean Engineering paper's warning about WASS depth maps as labels applies to it directly.
- **Rank.** Lowest of the families for the current goal: highest effort, no demonstrated monocular result, and aimed at a quantity the camera already captures.

### Gaps
- Cox-Munk glitter statistics and the Zappa et al. polarimetric slope-sensing field work were not re-verified this session and so are not cited; no 2025–2026 learned shape-from-glitter or shape-from-shading method for the sea was searched for (search budget).
- No release status found for the Liu et al. stereo network.
- Realism of Unreal, Unity and Omniverse ocean shaders against measured sea-surface statistics was not researched.

## 7. Simulation-to-real: synthetic views from prescribed spectra, sim-trained estimators and their transfer gap, generative video

### Takeaway
No published camera wave estimator trained in simulation has been shown to transfer to real instrument-labelled data: Yang 2026 is simulation only, Kamagata's synthetic pretraining has no controlled ablation, and the one public rendered-ocean video set comes with a classical method whose frequency bias changed sign between rendered and real clips. Generative video models offer no physical control of sea state and score poorly on physics benchmarks (Sora 10 % on Physics-IQ). Simulation is best used here to test whether a quantity is visible at all from a given camera geometry, not as a source of training labels.

### Cited Findings
**Sim-trained or sim-evaluated wave estimators**
- Yang et al. 2026 train and test entirely in a Unity/Crest digital twin (47 sea states, about 4,700 image-attitude pairs, labels from virtual buoys) and name the lack of real synchronised data as their key limitation. [PN, full text] — [Yang 2026 notes](../../docs/literature/papers/yang2026.md)
- Kamagata et al. pretrain on "over 1000" synthetic sequences generated from linear Airy theory (60 frames at 30 fps) and report synthetic-validation RMSE falling from 2.40 s to 0.31 s. The paper has no controlled comparison of pretraining against none on real data; the nearest is one from-scratch run at 2.17 s after a weight mismatch. It describes the synthetic depth assumption two different ways. [FT] — [arXiv 2606.13302](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13302v1)
- The L3i Ocean Simulation Dataset (La Rochelle, 2026): 684 rendered videos, 171 per Douglas sea state 3–6; 20 s each at 1,920 × 1,080 and 30 fps; Blender 5.1 with the Tessendorf ocean algorithm and JONSWAP spectrum; camera 20 m above the water pitched down 45°; random peak direction, sun position and water colour; labels for peak frequency (0.07–0.18 Hz), H_s (0.65–4.5 m) and direction. About 31 GiB, obtained by contacting the authors; no licence stated; the page warns that the simulations are "not guaranteed to accurately depict real ocean waves". [FT of dataset page and slides] — [dataset page](https://l3i-share.univ-lr.fr/2026OceanSimulationDataset/); [ICPR 2026 slides](https://icpr2026orgteam.github.io/OralPres/1062.pdf)
- On that dataset and on real Black Sea sequences, the accompanying classical 3-D FFT method's errors differ in kind: frequency bias about +0.05 Hz rendered against −0.1 Hz real; direction unbiased rendered against a 15° bias real. [FT of slides] — [ICPR 2026 slides](https://icpr2026orgteam.github.io/OralPres/1062.pdf)
- Collins et al. (2020) trained a fully convolutional network on synthetic surf-zone imagery from the Celeris wave model to infer bathymetry; a single-frame input cut test error by about 10 % against time-exposure input alone; real imagery was left to a 2021 follow-up at Duck. [SS; MDPI not accessed] — [Remote Sensing 12:3364](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12203364); [2021 follow-up](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-13-04907/article_deploy/remotesensing-13-04907.pdf)
- In X-band radar, pretraining on synthetic sea clutter generated from a Pierson-Moskowitz spectrum was proposed to offset scarce in-situ labels; the size of the gain was not retrieved. [SS] — [Moscow Univ. Phys. Bull. 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3103/S0027134923070275)
- The earlier report records a pose model trained on rendered seas with an infinite horizon that became biased in camera height. [PN] — [backbones report](../../reports/Vision%20backbones%20for%20wave%20cameras.md)

**Generative video and image editing as augmentation**
- A 2026 JMSE paper generates offshore video from text by mapping WMO sea-state levels to prompts (level 5 becomes "rough sea, high waves (2 m), crashing waves, heavy foam, strong wind"); the authors state the generated wave motion is not constrained or validated by hydrodynamic models. [SS] — [JMSE 14:1139](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse14121139)
- SafeSea (WACV 2024 workshop) edits real sea images to other "Sea State" backgrounds while keeping the vessels, filtering outputs with a sea-state classifier; its purpose is object-detection robustness and the abstract reports no downstream gain. [ABS] — [SafeSea](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14764)
- Physics-IQ (WACV 2026) tests video generators on continuing real clips across fluid dynamics, optics, solid mechanics, magnetism and thermodynamics, and concludes that physical understanding is "severely limited, and unrelated to visual realism". [ABS] — [arXiv 2501.09038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09038). The benchmark has 396 eight-second videos over 66 scenarios, and Sora scored 10.0 %. [SS] — [WACV 2026 page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Motamed_Do_Generative_Video_Models_Understand_Physical_Principles_WACV_2026_paper.html)
- Open video generators and licences: Wan 2.1 and 2.2 Apache-2.0; Mochi-1 Apache-2.0; Cosmos-Predict2 and 2.5 under the NVIDIA Open Model License; HunyuanVideo under a Tencent community licence. [API] — [Wan 2.2](https://huggingface.co/api/models/Wan-AI/Wan2.2-T2V-A14B); [Mochi-1](https://huggingface.co/api/models/genmo/mochi-1-preview); [Cosmos-Predict2.5](https://huggingface.co/api/models/nvidia/Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B); [HunyuanVideo](https://huggingface.co/api/models/tencent/HunyuanVideo)

### Inferences
- **The evidence for synthetic pretraining where labels are scarce is absent, not negative.** Nobody has run the controlled experiment (simulation-only, real-only, simulation-then-real at several real label counts, scored on real instruments) for camera wave estimation. The sign flip in the 3-D FFT bias is a small warning that rendered seas mislead even a training-free method.
- **Where simulation is safe and valuable: identifiability.** Render the same wind sea with and without a swell of realistic steepness at the project's actual camera geometries and resolutions, and ask whether any front end (brightness spectrum, displacement spectrum, 3-D FFT) can tell the two apart. If not, "the camera cannot see swell" becomes a statement about optics and geometry, not about the model, and swell stays with ERA5 on principle. If it can, the feature that separates them is the one to look for in real clips. This use carries no sim-to-real risk because it makes a claim about information content, not about a trained model.
- **Second safe use: unit tests.** A rendered clip with a known peak period and direction checks that each physics front end and the rectification code return the prescribed values before they touch real data.
- **Third, riskier use: pretraining for scarce targets** (swell period, direction, calm and extreme seas). The gap to expect is in foam, breaking, shoaling over real bathymetry, haze and compression, none of which a Tessendorf deep-water ocean reproduces. Linear or higher-order spectral wave synthesis fixes the kinematics, not the appearance.
- **Generative video as augmentation is not ready.** There is no parameter that sets H_s or T_p, only words, and the physics benchmarks say the motion cannot be trusted. Diffusion-feature probes were already rejected in the architectures report for related reasons; nothing here changes that.
- **Cost (estimates).** Rendering a few hundred 20-second clips at reduced resolution in Blender is on the order of 10–30 GPU-hours on one H200, or far less with a real-time engine; this is unmeasured. The public L3i set would avoid rendering but requires contacting its authors and has no stated licence, which is a decision for the project owner.

### Gaps
- No quantified sim-to-real transfer gap for any camera wave estimator, including Yang 2026's simulator.
- Not researched: Unreal and Omniverse ocean pipelines, higher-order spectral wave synthesis tools, domain-randomisation studies for water, and any 2026 video generator with numeric physical conditioning.
- Collins et al.'s real-imagery numbers and the X-band synthetic-pretraining gain were not retrieved.
- Physics-IQ scores for 2025–2026 generators (Veo, Cosmos, Wan) were not found.

## 8. Event cameras, thermal infrared, polarization and low-light imaging

### Takeaway
None of these sensors is in the project's data streams, and the evidence for wave measurement is thin: thermal infrared has one solid result (breaker type classified at 89–93 %), polarimetry has a laboratory dataset, and for event cameras the only maritime work treats waves as noise to be filtered out.

### Cited Findings
- Event cameras: in maritime vessel tracking, waves "produce the majority of events" and are filtered as clutter. [ABS] — [arXiv 2202.04231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04231). A 2025 neuromorphic-camera study for offshore wave compensation measures the displacement of hydraulic cylinders, not the water surface. [SS] — [Frontiers in Neurorobotics 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2025.1648713). No sea-surface wave measurement with an event camera was found in the one search that ran. [SS]
- Thermal infrared: Buscombe and Carini (Remote Sensing 2019) classify breaker type from close-range infrared images of the surf zone using features from six standard CNNs and logistic regression, at 89 % average accuracy without augmentation and 93 % with it; infrared is relatively insensitive to reflected sunlight. [SS] — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/dbc7e742a0c249248584ce815335380e)
- Infrared and optical imagery share weather limits: "Clouds, fog, heavy rain, or sea-spray can obscure optical and IR imagery". [FT] — [Salatin, Elgar & Raubenheimer 2026](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025JH000906)
- Polarization: the laboratory polarimetric-plus-wave-gauge dataset in Section 6 is the only open resource found (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0). [ABS] — [arXiv 2410.22849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22849)

### Inferences
- Infrared extends breaking detection and breaker-type classification to night, and would let the project test its infrared research set against the Yin et al. breaker-type clips with the same classifier.
- Polarimetry measures slope by physics, which is a wind-sea and short-wave quantity; it does not address swell.
- An event camera's native output is brightness change, which is close to the temporal derivative the pixel-spectrum method uses, at very high time resolution and low data rate. That is a plausible sensor for period at low power, but it is speculation with no published support.
- None of this bears on beating the four target papers with existing data.

### Gaps
- Low-light and night imaging for wave measurement was not searched (budget).
- A second event-camera search was cut off; the absence of sea-surface event work rests on one query.
- Open datasets for infrared or event-based wave measurement were not identified beyond the project's own.

## 9. For each family: the cheapest decisive experiment on existing data, the result that would justify more, and a ranked shortlist

### Takeaway
Run the spectral and displacement front ends for peak period first, then put promptable segmentation and a VLM to work as infrastructure (crops, horizon, quality control, a zero-shot baseline), then test horizon-plus-learned-focal calibration; defer physics-informed operators, inverse rendering, generative video and new sensors. The deciding principle is to prefer tests that attack a result the project is stuck on, using a method with at least one real-data demonstration, at CPU or single-digit GPU-hour cost on data already held.

### Cited Findings
The ranking rests on these demonstrated results, each cited in full above:
- Texture-displacement spectra matched radar peak period within 3 % on average. — [Yu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13131-024-2359-y)
- A rectified 3-D FFT gave direction to about 20° but frequency biased by −0.1 Hz on real video. — [ICPR 2026 slides](https://icpr2026orgteam.github.io/OralPres/1062.pdf)
- Spectral points matched a raw-sequence 3-D CNN on radar H_s at about one-ninetieth of the compute; a single image was twice as bad. — [arXiv 2601.04581](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04581v1)
- Zero-shot segmentation reached water IoU 0.97–0.99 and beat a locally trained supervised model at unseen shores. — [arXiv 2608.21276](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21276v1)
- VLMs rely on priors more than pixels for quantities. — [QuantiPhy](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19526)
- Learned single-image calibration: field of view to 3–5°, pitch to 1–2°. — [GeoCalib](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06704v2)
- Remote calibration of surf cameras from lidar control points: 3.4 and 6.5 m. — [SurfRCaT summary](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsdoj,edsdoj.b1046b3291454ddbbf04c70688785c2a)
- No sim-trained camera wave estimator has a real-data transfer result. — [Yang 2026 notes](../../docs/literature/papers/yang2026.md); [arXiv 2606.13302](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13302v1)
- Project baselines the tests are scored against: Kamagata's held-out T_p error of 1.42 s, the 453 packaged clips on 12 dates, and the measured 40–55 minutes per buoy training run. — [architectures report](../../reports/Wave%20camera%20architectures%20beyond%20backbones.md); [measurements report](../../reports/Wave%20measurements%20to%20learn%20next.md)

**The single cheapest decisive experiment per family**
- *VLMs:* rank 4. One afternoon; decides the quality-control role and yields a baseline no wave paper has.
- *Promptable segmentation:* rank 3. Decides whether fixed per-camera crops can be retired.
- *Geometry:* rank 5 on one pier camera. Decides whether direction and wavelength in physical units are reachable.
- *Motion:* rank 1(a). A displacement spectrum against the brightness spectrum on the same boxes.
- *Physics-informed and spectral:* rank 2, with rank 6 as a free by-product.
- *Inverse graphics:* none worth running now on existing data. The cheapest would be a monocular elevation regressor on the project's stereo set, which the WASS-label caution undermines.
- *Simulation:* rank 8, because it answers a question about the camera, not about a model.
- *Alternative sensors:* run the existing breaker-type classifier on the infrared research set; it informs night-time coverage only.

**Dependencies and order.** Ranks 1 and 2 need no calibration, because frequency is the same in pixels and in metres. Direction in compass degrees, wavelength and celerity need rank 5. Rank 3 feeds rank 5 (horizon) and every shore experiment (crop). Rank 8 should precede any spending on rank 9.

**What would change the order.** If rank 1 fails on swell-period days while working on wind-sea days, rank 8 moves up to explain why. If rank 3's masks are unstable across lighting, keep fixed crops and drop rank 7. If rank 5's scale is off by more than 20 %, direction work waits for surveyed or lidar control points (SurfRCaT), and rank 6 becomes the only route to a scale.

**Previously rejected items, revisited as instructed.** No new 2026 evidence was found that changes the reasoning for point trackers on water, monocular depth applied to the sea surface, long-memory video models, diffusion features or tabular foundation models as the main combiner. Two details changed at the margin: GeoCalib (Apache-2.0) and MoGe-2 (MIT) licences are now confirmed, which removes a practical obstacle to the land-side calibration check; and one 2024 paper shows a box-level motion signal carrying peak period against a radar, which supports motion front ends without validating trackers or dense flow against a buoy.

### Gaps
- Every cost figure in the table is an estimate; none was measured on the shared H200s. The only measured throughput in the project remains the buoy training run and the 1.5 s per clip decode.
- The acceptance thresholds (IoU 0.8, F1 0.9, 10–15 % scale agreement) are proposals to be fixed before each test runs, in the same way the project fixes its bars; no source supplies them.
- Whether paired site cameras overlap, the buoy cameras' height and field of view, and the availability of pier geometry were not checked in the project data.
- The search budget prevented a final sweep for late-2026 papers on SAM-based whitecap segmentation, learned horizon-based rectification, and CLIP-type quality classification; a follow-up search on those three topics is the most likely to change a rank.
